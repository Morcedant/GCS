Every fairy tale was a warning. The Brothers Grimm wrote down what their family had hunted for centuries, and the things in those stories never went away. In fog-bound towns and lamplit camps, Wesen walk among the living wearing human faces. Grimms, the last of the old hunters, can see through them. The Royal Families pull strings from across the ocean, and men and women with real magic hide inside lodges and country churches. You know the truth, and you stand between it and the people who don't.

This guide uses the same rules as the USS Pelion game: concrete skills, 3d6 roll-under, and a small pool of Grit points. It is an unofficial fan game inspired by the television series *Grimm* and is not affiliated with its makers.

## The rule to remember

Roll 3d6 at or below your adjusted skill to succeed. Your skill already includes your training and attribute. Do not add the attribute again.

*Example:* Tracking 13, with a −2 penalty for rain, gives a target of 11. A roll of 9 succeeds by 2. A roll of 12 fails by 1.

## The Grimm-verse

**Wesen** look like ordinary people but have a hidden form, almost always a two-legged blend of human and animal. The change between the two is the **woge** (VOH-gə). Each kind of Wesen has its own culture and traditions, and many of the oldest involve hunting or eating humans. The Blutbad, a wolf-like Wesen, gave rise to the werewolf legend. Their names are German, as are many of the old words of this hidden world.

**Grimms** are supernaturally gifted warriors, stronger and faster than ordinary people, who can see the Wesen hidden among us. Their old Latin name, *Decapitare*, comes from their habit of beheading the creatures they hunt. The gift passes only occasionally, sometimes skipping a generation or two, and the Grimm families keep themselves anonymous. Grimms enforce the **Code of Swabia**, the old law between Wesen and the rest of the world. Some Grimms enforce it fairly; others believe every Wesen is an abomination to be destroyed.

The **seven Royal Families** are ancient, wealthy and influential. They no longer rule whole countries, but they still manipulate events through their public cousins, field loyal Wesen armies, and seek control over all Wesen and over ordinary humanity too.

**Incantare** is the catch-all name for anyone who works real magic. Most magic is ritual, in many styles and beliefs, and each practitioner sees it through a **Lens**. The Wesen Council and the Royal Families both consider Incantare extremely dangerous, and Grimms are often tasked with watching or destroying them. **The blood of a Grimm is a powerful anti-magic agent.** A Hexenbiest or Zauberbiest who drinks it under a full moon loses their powers, and a Chymiater can brew it into a draught that strips anyone's magical gifts for days (see Grimm blood).

Three orders matter on the frontier:

- **Bruderschaft der Heiligen Erde** (Brotherhood of the Holy Earth) protects the Earth from industry. Luddites with magic, they claim descent from druidic orders, and the railroad is their enemy.
- **Ordo Sancti Cultelli** (Order of the Holy Knife) is a Church-affiliated order of assassins sworn to cleanse the Earth of supernatural threats, Wesen and Incantare alike.
- **Ordo Hermeticus Aurorae Aureae** (the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn) presents itself as a fraternal order with odd trappings. Inside it, layer under layer, are secrets and real magicians.

# Create your character

Work through the five steps in order. The Grimm character creator follows the same steps and checks the rules for you.

## Step 1: Choose your calling

Choose a calling and describe what your character brings. Your calling places you in one of three groups, which only changes your sheet's colors and badge. A title such as "Deputy U.S. Marshal" or "Doc" is agreed with the GM and gives no bonus.

**Occultists and magic.** Magic comes from your heritage, not your calling: an Occultist who is an **Incantare** works real magic through their Lens. With any other heritage, an Occultist knows the lore but can't work the art: a scholar of the occult, a charlatan, or someone still searching for a teacher.

Every calling has its own **ability** (in the table above), printed on your sheet. Callings follow one recipe: a fighting skill, a trail skill, a people skill and a signature skill. The Politician and the four scholars and healers of the Learned aren't fighters: they take a second signature skill instead, though anyone can still train a fighting skill.

The creator marks your calling's four **core skills** (in the table above) Trained when you choose it. Keep them, raise one to your Specialty, or swap any of them for other skills; changing calling swaps the core skills you haven't changed.

### Well-rounded or Old hand

Choose how your character trained (see Step 3):

- **Well-rounded:** one Specialty at +3 and nine Trained skills.
- **Old hand:** one Expert skill at +5 and seven Trained skills. Old hands have spent a lifetime at one thing.

## Step 2: Choose a heritage, spend Edges, assign attributes

### Attributes

| Attribute | Used for |
|---|---|
| Body | Strength, endurance, resisting injury |
| Agility | Coordination, reflexes, movement |
| Intellect | Reasoning, observation, knowledge |
| Presence | Leadership, persuasion, social influence |
| Will | Resolve, concentration, resisting fear and magic |

### Heritage and tier

Choose a heritage from the **Heritages** chapter: Human, Grimm, Incantare, or one of the Wesen kinds. Every attribute starts at 10, and your heritage adjusts some of them; the result is your **heritage base**. Adjustments always total zero.

Heritages differ in power, so each has a **tier** from 0 to 3. Every character has **3 points** to split between heritage power and **Edges**: Tier 0 (Human) leaves 3 Edges, Tier 1 leaves 2, Tier 2 leaves 1, and Tier 3 leaves none. Edges are useful in every scene; a Wesen's power mostly shows while woged, and that risks Exposure.

**Horror heritages.** Naschazer, Taniwha and Vallachi must feed on human flesh or lifeforce. They're available only when the whole table has agreed to a horror-tinged campaign.

### Edges

Spend all your Edges. Each one buys:

| Edge | What you get | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| +1 attribute point | One more point to spend in the next step, within the normal maximum | 2 |
| Extra Trained skill | One more Trained skill in Step 3 | 3 |
| Second advantage | A second general advantage, with no extra complication | 1 |
| True grit | Start every session with 3 Grit instead of 2 | 1 |
| A friend in town | A named contact who helps once per case | 1 |
| A fine weapon or tool | An heirloom weapon, a master's instruments or a charm-maker's kit: +2 to one named skill when it's used | 1 |
| Additional Defense | A second defense each round: Dodge, Parry or Block, in any mix. A second parry is still −2. | 1 |
| Hard to put down | Once per fight, shrug off a telling hit's effect: you still take its boxes (see *Telling hits*) | 1 |
| Talisman | A ready charm made for you by a Braucher, a hexenbiest or a root doctor (a Himmelsbrief, a mojo hand, a blessed buckeye), holding a Minor ritual agreed with the GM; break it to release it. Renewed between cases; doesn't count against a charm limit | 1 |

### Spending points

After applying your heritage, distribute **five additional points** among the attributes, plus one for each attribute Edge.

- The starting maximum is **13**, plus any heritage bonus to that attribute. A Jägerbär can start with Body 15.
- You cannot lower an attribute below its heritage base to gain points.

### Custom heritage

You can build your own heritage with the GM. See *Making your own* for the steps.

## Step 3: Choose trained skills

A **Well-rounded** character chooses ten different skills: one **Specialty** at its governing attribute +3 and nine **Trained** skills at attribute +1. An **Old hand** chooses **one Expert skill at +5** and **seven Trained skills at +1**. All other skills are **Untrained** at attribute −4 when an attempt is plausible. Each skill has its own final target number; do not add the attribute again when rolling.

The Expert's extra bonus matters most when the work is hard. With Intellect 13, an Expert gambler has a target of 18, which only natural 17–18 can fail; under a −4 "very difficult" penalty the target is still 14. If Athletics is the Expert skill, Dodge adds +2, the same as a Specialty.

Each Extra Trained skill Edge adds one more Trained skill. A **Grimm's** Wesen Lore is always at least Trained and doesn't use one of the Trained picks.

**Broadening.** An Old hand can broaden later (the GM's call, usually after a significant case): the Expert skill becomes a Specialty at +3, and two more skills become Trained.

## Step 4: Describe your character

Choose your name, title if any, and a short reason you stand against the dark. Then choose **one advantage** and **one complication**.

### Top 10 reasons to stand against the dark

1. **The family gift.** The Grimm blood woke in me, and it came with a duty.
2. **Vengeance.** Something that wore a human face killed someone I loved.
3. **Protect the town.** Somebody has to stand between these people and what's out there.
4. **Keep the peace.** Wesen and humans can live side by side, and I mean to prove it.
5. **I saw it.** I saw what I saw, and no one else believes me.
6. **Redemption.** I did harm once. I mean to balance the ledger.
7. **The bounty.** The pay's good, and nobody else will take these jobs.
8. **Knowledge.** The hidden world is the greatest mystery there is, and I want to understand it.
9. **Faith.** The Lord sent me here for a reason, and I believe this is it.
10. **Nowhere else to go.** These people took me in when no one else would.

- **Advantage.** Choose either a general advantage or one of your heritage advantages. A heritage advantage replaces the general advantage and always brings its companion complication. An Incantare's heritage advantage is their **Lens**.
- **Complication.** Choose one complication. You keep it even if you take a heritage advantage, so a character with a heritage advantage has two complications.
- **Allegiance (optional).** Choose an allegiance below, or none.

An advantage gives +2 to one narrowly defined kind of task when the GM agrees it applies. It cannot improve every use of a broad skill. A complication creates choices and problems; it is not a permanent penalty.

### Allegiance

Anyone can owe allegiance to one of the powers of the hidden world, or of the law. It's a benefit paired with a burden; the burden works like a complication and earns Grit when it causes you a real problem.

The GM can add others met in play (the creator's GM view has a **Campaign allegiances** list), and a player can write one with the GM: choose *Another allegiance* and give it a name, a benefit and a burden.

| Allegiance | Benefit | Burden |
|---|---|---|
| The Church | Once per case, shelter, records or a favor from the clergy | Duty: the Church gives you orders. Sicarii discipline ties you to the Order of the Holy Knife. |
| A Royal Family | Once per case, money, papers or a door opened | Duty: an involuntary call to serve, and the family's rivals hunt you |
| The U.S. Marshals | Once per case, a federal warrant, a posse sworn in on the spot, or a telegram to the district marshal | Duty: the marshal sends you after federal fugitives, and the men you've jailed have friends |
| The Texas Rangers | Once per case, a Ranger company riding with you, a warrant good anywhere in Texas, or word from a frontier post | Duty: the Captain sends you wherever Texas is in trouble, and the Rangers' hard reputation rides with you |
| A County Sheriff's Office | Once per case, a deputy's help, the county jail, or the county records and wanted posters | Duty: the sheriff calls on you, and the county's feuds and elections become yours |
| A City Police Department | Once per case, a patrolman's help, a night in the cells for someone, or the station's files | Duty: the captain gives the orders, and some of your fellow officers are Wesen with loyalties of their own |
| The Pinkerton National Detective Agency | Once per case, an agency file on someone, an operative's help, or a client's money for the work | Duty: the client comes first, even a railroad or a mine owner breaking a strike, and working folk hate the name |
| A secret society | Once per case, lore, a meeting place or a fellow initiate (the Golden Dawn, the Brotherhood of the Holy Earth) | Duty and Vow: keep its secrets and follow its traditions |
| The Wesen Council | Contacts in the local Wesen community | Duty: the Council calls on you, and others know you're its operative |
| A Grimm family | Access to the family books and kin | Duty to the family name, and to fight its enemies |

## Step 5: Prepare for the hunt

Your Dodge is Agility −2, plus your Athletics training bonus: +0 if Untrained, +1 if Trained, or +2 if a Specialty or Expert. Begin uninjured, with two Grit points (three with True grit) and your gear. Name your friend in town or your fine weapon or tool, if you took those Edges. Describe one connection to another player's character.

### Top 10 connections to another character

1. **Rode together in the war.** We served in the same company and still watch each other's backs.
2. **They saved my life.** They cut me down from a lynch mob's rope, and I owe them my life.
3. **I saved theirs.** I pulled them out of a burning barn, and they haven't forgotten it.
4. **Mentor.** They taught me what I know about the hidden world.
5. **Friendly rivals.** We're always competing over the same bounty, the same card game or the same sweetheart.
6. **Same hometown.** We grew up in the same town and know each other's families.
7. **An unpaid debt.** I owe them money, or a favor, and I haven't paid it yet.
8. **A shared secret.** We share a secret from a job neither of us talks about.
9. **Saloon regulars.** We meet every Saturday night for cards, whiskey and lies.
10. **Bad blood.** They blame me for something that went wrong on a past job.

## Untrained actions

A plausible untrained attempt uses attribute −4. Specialized work such as surgery, a ritual, or brewing a Wesen remedy requires relevant training or direct guidance. Guidance permits an attempt only when the GM finds it credible; it does not grant the expert's skill.

# Actions and teamwork

## When to roll

Routine work succeeds automatically when you have appropriate training, gear and time. Roll when failure could change the situation. Before rolling, establish your goal, the relevant skill, any modifiers, and what is at stake.

| Circumstances | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Favorable conditions or excellent preparation | +2 |
| Ordinary challenge under pressure | 0 |
| Difficult | −2 |
| Very difficult | −4 |
| Extreme | −6 |

Apply the main circumstance modifier once. Add separately specified effects such as assistance or injury when they apply. The GM avoids charging twice for the same obstacle.

## Results and margins

Add the three dice. A total at or below the adjusted skill succeeds. Margin of success equals the adjusted skill minus the roll. A larger margin may mean better information, more precise work, or a stronger position.

A natural 3 or 4 always succeeds. A natural 17 or 18 always fails. An impossible action receives no roll.

In an opposed contest, each side rolls its relevant skill. One success beats one failure. If both succeed, the larger success margin wins. A tie, or two failures, favors the defender or preserves the situation. For an automatic success above its target, count its contest margin as zero.

## Assistance

One qualified assistant can roll a relevant skill before the lead character rolls. Success grants +1 to the lead roll; success by 5 or more grants +2. Failure gives no bonus. An 18 introduces a complication described by the GM. Additional characters can do other tasks but cannot stack assistance bonuses on the same roll.

## Failure and another attempt

Failure changes the situation: time passes, the trail goes cold, supplies run out, or danger grows. Do not repeat an unchanged roll until it succeeds. A new approach, new evidence, better tools, or more time may justify another attempt.

## Success at a cost

When a roll fails by only **1 or 2**, the GM may offer **success at a cost**: you get what you were after, but something else goes wrong. Typical costs are an injury box, a lost horse or broken gun, lost time (tick a clock), a box of Trouble, or someone noticing. The GM names the cost before you accept; you can always take the plain failure instead. A natural 17 or 18 is never a success at a cost.

## Progress clocks

A **clock** is a circle cut into 4, 6 or 8 segments that tracks something that takes time or builds toward disaster: *The mob forms (6)*, *Nightfall (4)*, *Find the lair (6)*, *Win the clan's trust (8)*.

- **Danger clocks** fill when things go wrong: a failed roll, a success at a cost, or time passing. When one fills, the danger happens.
- **Progress clocks** fill as the characters succeed: one segment for a success, two for success by 5 or more. When one fills, the goal is achieved.
- The GM sizes the clock (4 = urgent or simple, 6 = substantial, 8 = long or complex) and shows it to the players.

## Investigation and social scenes

Ask concrete questions and explain your approach. Evidence may require several different tasks. Persuasion can secure cooperation, Read People offers insight into behavior, and Wesen Lore explains what the evidence means. A successful roll does not force someone to abandon a core belief or reveal what they do not know.

## Chases

Run a chase as a progress clock for the pursuers against a danger clock for the quarry's escape. Each exchange, characters roll **Riding** (or Athletics on foot): success ticks the pursuers' clock, failure ticks the escape. Terrain, a tired horse, or a shot fired from the saddle (Shooting at −2) adjust the rolls.

# The woge and the hidden world

## Wesen and the woge

Every Wesen heritage follows the **core Wesen rules** and has a **woge profile** (both in the Heritages chapter). In short:

- **Woging** is a free action, in or out. Physical heritage advantages and the woge profile work only in **full woge**.
- **Losing control.** When an enemy appears, you're frightened or you're attacked, the GM can call for a **Will** roll; on a failure you woge.
- **Your profile.** While woged, the profile's attribute bonuses apply to rolls on those attributes and the skills based on them, even above the starting maximum; its other traits and drawbacks apply too. The creator's **In full woge** box adds them for you.
- **Who sees it.** A full woge is visible to anyone. A **Grimm** sees a flash of the Wesen face whenever a Wesen is stirred by strong emotion. A Wesen who looks into the eyes of a Grimm in that moment knows the Grimm for what they are.
- **Exposure.** A full woge in front of ordinary people breaks the Code of Swabia: mark a box of **Exposure** (two for a Tier 3 heritage).

## Grimms

A Grimm's core gifts come with the heritage: the Sight, Grimm eyes, an uneasy sense of nearby Wesen and magic, never freezing (+2 to Will against fear), resisting magic (+2 to Will against it), and Wesen Lore at least Trained for free. Each Grimm also chooses one **Grimm gift** as their heritage advantage. Grimms enforce the Code, and Wesen know it, so many flee, lie or fight on recognizing one.

### Grimm blood

- **Against a Hexenbiest or Zauberbiest.** A Hexenbiest or Zauberbiest who drinks a Grimm's blood under a full moon loses their powers. It happens whether the Grimm intends it or not, and it lasts until the *Contaminatio Ritualis* restores them.
- **The Chymiater's draught.** A Chymiater (alchemist) Incantare can brew a Grimm's fresh blood into a draught in a major ritual (6 segments). Injected or drunk, it neutralizes the target's magical gifts for **3d6 ÷ 3 days** (round down: one to six days): an Incantare's Lens, a Hexenbiest's telekinesis and spells, or a Wesen's magical signature power. It does not stop a woge.
- **A dangerous commodity.** Everyone in the hidden world knows what Grimm blood can do. Who gets it, and how, is always a serious matter.

## Magic

Magic works at two speeds, with a third tool that turns one into the other. An Incantare's **Lens** decides which they use and how; any Lens can attempt any effect.

| Tool | Speed | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Spells** | One action | A quick spell you know. Roll Occult; on a success it happens. Veneficare only. |
| **Rituals** | Minutes to nights | Anything else. Describe the effect; the GM sets its weight; fill a progress clock. |
| **Charms** | One action, no roll | A finished ritual stored in an object, released by breaking it. |

### Rituals

1. **Describe the effect.** The GM sets its weight: **minor** (2 segments), **significant** (4) or **major** (6), and says what it can and can't do before you start.
2. **Gather the energy.** Fill a progress clock of that size with **Occult** rolls. Each roll takes your Lens's casting time (usually ten minutes; an hour for a Magi). Success fills one segment; success by 5 or more fills two. A failure costs time or ticks a danger clock.
3. **The effect happens** when the clock is full. A ritual interrupted with energy gathered may go wrong.

**Trappings.** The right materials and correspondences (stones, herbs, stars, places) remove one segment from the clock.

### Charms, elixirs and potions

**Charms.** A finished ritual can be stored in an object and released by breaking it: a bottle thrown, a pendant snapped, a bullet-charm fired. You can carry as many ready charms as your Occult training allows: Trained 2, Specialty 3, Expert 4. A Scaenarfex carries one more; a Veneficare only one. Preparation is how slow magic wins a fast fight.

**Elixirs and potions** spoil. They stay good until the end of the day, or a week on a success by 5 or more.

### Spells (Veneficare)

A **Veneficare** trades preparation for speed: they know **spells**, cast with **one action and an Occult roll**, shaped by one or two Paths agreed with the GM. Spells known: **Trained 2, Specialty 3, Expert 4**.

Each spell has a short **base effect**: one target, for one scene, at Close or Short range. Occult as a Specialty gives **1 Boost**, as Expert **2**, chosen **as you cast**; each Boost spent is **−1 to the roll**.

| Boost | One Boost | Two Boosts |
|---|---|---|
| **Harder** | +1 box, or the penalty becomes −3 | +2 boxes, or −4 |
| **Wider** | A handful of targets | A crowd, or an area |
| **Longer** | A day | Until it's lifted |
| **Farther** | One range band farther | Two bands farther |

| Spell | Base effect |
|---|---|
| Flame | A lash of fire at Short range; marks 2 boxes. Dodge applies. |
| Force | Unseen force hurls the target down: 1 box, and they lose their next move (Body resists). |
| Ward | Attacks against one ally are at −2 for the scene. |
| Fluch | The target is at −2 on all rolls for the scene (Will resists). |
| Bind | The target is held where they stand for the scene, or until they break free (Body resists). |
| Snuff | Every lamp in a room goes out, or one gun jams; clearing a jam takes an action. |
| Unmask | A Wesen in sight rolls Will or woges, there and then. |
| Stanch | Stop the bleeding: clear one injury box, once per wound. |

A failed spell flares where people can see it: mark a box of **Exposure**. A natural 18 marks two, or something answers that shouldn't.

**Hexenbiests and Zauberbiests** work rituals the same way, through their innate magic, in their own style (curses, potions and sendings). Their **telekinesis** is their quick magic.

### Old-country magic in America

The old traditions crossed the ocean with the people who kept them, and many of the Lenses wear American clothes:

- **Braucherei**: the Pennsylvania Dutch healing craft of spoken charms, prayers and blessed objects, from Hohman's *The Long Lost Friend* (1820). A Braucher heals, protects and breaks hexes, takes no money for it, and passes the craft man to woman and woman to man. It is a Christian craft, and its practitioners would be insulted to be called witches.
- **Hexerei**: Braucherei's dark mirror. A *Hexenmeister* lays curses, or is hired to find and break someone else's, which makes them both feared and needed. The Malifica Lens.
- **The Himmelsbrief**, a "letter from Heaven": a protective letter carried on the body. Union and Confederate soldiers of German stock carried them against bullets.
- **Grimoires**: the *Sixth and Seventh Books of Moses* and the *Egyptian Secrets of Albertus Magnus* pass from hand to hand in German print. A Magi would kill for a good copy; some have.
- **The Pennsylvania mystics**: the Hermits of the Wissahickon and the Ephrata Cloister kept astrology, alchemy and stranger things alive in the last century. A Magi or Chymiater may claim their teacher's teacher studied there.
- **The Druid lodges**: the United Ancient Order of Druids keeps lodges across the West, California most of all. Most members are only there for the fellowship. Not all.
- **New arrivals**: German colonists from Russia have been settling Kansas and Nebraska since 1874, and brought their own healers and charm-workers with them.

**Magic draws attention.** Using it openly can mark a box of **Exposure**, the Order of the Holy Knife hunts known Incantare, and a Grimm gets +1 to sense your magic for each charm or potion you carry.

## The Code of Swabia

The Code of Swabia is the old law between Wesen and the rest of the world, and Grimms are its enforcers. At its heart, Wesen must not reveal themselves to humanity. How far it reaches beyond that, and how mercifully a Grimm enforces it, is a question your characters will answer for themselves.

# Personal combat

## Turn order and actions

Act in descending Agility order. Players choose the order of tied characters; the GM resolves other ties. A round lasts a few seconds. On your turn, move a short distance and take one meaningful action, such as shooting, reloading, helping, or attempting a task.

## Attacking and defending

Roll Shooting for a ranged attack, Melee for a weapon in hand, or Brawling for fists and grappling. If the attack succeeds, an aware target able to move may **defend**: Dodge, Parry or Block. A successful defense cancels the hit. Apply injury penalties to every defense.

- **One defense a round.** A target gets one defense a round, between its turns, and chooses which when an attack hits. The **Additional Defense** Edge gives a second.
- **Dodge** is Agility −2, with +1 for Trained Athletics or +2 for Athletics as a Specialty or Expert.
- **Parry.** Against a weapon in hand, fists, claws or fangs, roll Melee −3 with a weapon in hand. Bare-handed, Brawling −3 parries fists only: nobody turns a Blutbad's claws with an open hand. A second parry in the same round (with Additional Defense) is a further −2. Nobody parries a bullet or an arrow.
- **Block.** With a shield, or anything that can stand in for one (a chair, a wooden slat, a crate lid, a saddle), you can block a blade, a club, claws, fangs, fists or an arrow: roll Melee −2, or Melee −1 with a real shield. Nothing you can lift blocks a bullet. An improvised shield breaks the first time it blocks a hit of 2 or more boxes: a chair stops one swipe of a Blutbad's claws, and then it's kindling.
- **Attack quality.** A clean hit is harder to escape. When an attack succeeds by 3 or 4, the defense is at −1; by 5 or 6, −2; by 7 or more, −3. A Wesen in full woge often strikes that cleanly.
- **Aim.** Spend your action aiming at a target you can see: +1 to your next attack against it. **Careful Marksman** and **Hunter's Eye** add their +2 to this; a **Gunslinger** doesn't need to aim.

Solid cover blocks a shot. Partial cover imposes −2 on the attack. Weapon effects apply after a hit survives any defense. Success margin does not increase damage: a great hit decides *what* happens instead.

## Telling hits

A really good shot, or a blow that lands exactly where it was meant to, lets the attacker say what it does.

- **A telling hit** is an attack that succeeds by **5 or more**, or on a natural 3 or 4, and isn't stopped by a defense. The attacker picks **one effect** below and describes it. The weapon still marks its usual boxes.
- **A called shot.** Before rolling, name the effect you want and take −2 on the attack, or −4 for a small target (a hand, an eye, a rope, a lock). If the attack hits at all, the effect happens, whatever the margin. **Careful Marksman** and **Hunter's Eye** cut this penalty by 1.
- **Both ways.** Wesen, outlaws and worse land telling hits on characters too.

| Effect | What happens |
|---|---|
| Disarm | The weapon or held item drops, or flies a few yards away |
| Knock down | The target falls prone, or is driven back a few steps |
| Pin | The target is held, tangled or pinned down: their next action goes to getting free |
| Rattle | −2 to the target's next roll |
| Break gear | A shield, weapon or tool breaks |
| Open them up | The next attack on this target this round gets +2 |
| Precise | You hit exactly what you aimed at: the lantern, the rope, the hand on the trigger |

**Limits.** A telling hit never adds boxes and never gets past a defense. The effect has to make sense: nobody disarms a Blutbad of its claws or knocks down a stagecoach, and the GM can rule an effect out. Opponents who drop from one hit are already down, so a telling hit against them sets up what comes next.

**Staying alive.** A character on the receiving end can shrug off a telling hit's effect with **Hard to put down** (once per fight) or by spending a Grit point. Either way, they still take the boxes.

*Example:* a Blutbad in full woge claws at Deputy Ames and succeeds by 5. Ames hasn't defended yet this round: Dodge 11, −2 for the clean hit, needs a 9 or less. Ames has a saber in hand, so a parry is possible too: Melee 14 −3 −2 = 9, no better. Ames dodges. When the second Blutbad lunges before Ames's next turn, there's no defense left, unless Ames has Additional Defense.

## Range

Distances fall into five bands:

| Band | Distance | For example |
|---|---|---|
| Close | Up to 10 yards | Across a room or a saloon; one move covers it |
| Short | Up to 50 yards | Across a street or a corral |
| Medium | Up to 150 yards | Down the length of a town |
| Long | Up to 500 yards | Across a valley or a river crossing |
| Extreme | Up to a mile, in line of sight | Ridge to ridge |

Every ranged weapon has an **effective band**, shown in the weapons table. Attacks within it take no penalty. One band beyond it is −2, two bands beyond is −4, and anything farther is out of range. Weapons in hand, claws and fists reach only someone next to you. Your move each turn covers the Close band; give up your action to run and you cover Short.

## Injury

| Boxes marked | Condition | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Uninjured | No injury penalty |
| 1 | Wounded | −1 to physical actions |
| 2 | Seriously wounded | −2 to physical actions |
| 3 | Incapacitated | Unable to act; medical aid needed |

Injury penalties replace each other rather than stacking. They apply to movement, attacks, Dodge, and other tasks the injury physically hinders.

### Dying and death

- **Dying.** A hit from a weapon that marks **2 or more boxes** and brings you to three boxes leaves you Incapacitated and **Dying**. A Dying character dies at the end of the scene unless someone stabilizes them: one action and a successful Medicine roll with a doctor's bag.
- **A further hit.** While you are at three boxes, any hit from a weapon that marks 2 or more boxes kills you. Fists, clubs and knives just keep you down.
- **Beheading** and comparable fates bypass the track entirely.

Your protection is avoiding the hit: a defense (Dodge, Parry or Block), cover, and a Grit point, which reduces a hit by one box.

### Optional rule: gritty combat

For a harder, bloodier game, the GM can turn on **gritty combat** for the campaign (the creator has a switch on the Condition card and in the GM view). The rest of the rules stay the same.

**Grievous hits.** A hit that marks **2 or more boxes** is **grievous** when the attack succeeded by **7 or more**, rolled a **natural 3 or 4**, or left the target **Dying**.

- **A character** rolls 3d6 on the table below and suffers the result on top of the boxes.
- **An opponent** is simply taken out: no roll, no boxes to count. Describe how, or let the GM view roll a description.
- **With a telling hit,** the attacker also chooses which limb.

| 3d6 | Grievous wound | Effect | Heals |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3–4 | Lost limb | A hand, an arm or a leg is gone; the GM says which. −4 to tasks that need it, or impossible. Dying unless someone stops the bleeding. | Never: a complication |
| 5–6 | Lost eye | −2 to Shooting and to spotting things. | Never: a complication |
| 7–9 | Broken bone | The limb is useless until it's set with proper care. | Several weeks |
| 10–12 | Torn muscle | −1 to physical tasks. | Several weeks |
| 13–15 | Concussion | Dazed, and −2 to thinking tasks for the scene. | After the scene |
| 16–18 | Internal injury | Dying even with boxes to spare; it needs a doctor. | Several weeks after surgery |

- **Grit.** Spend a Grit point to **downgrade** a grievous wound one step: a lost limb becomes a broken bone, a lost eye a concussion, a broken bone or an internal injury a torn muscle, and a torn muscle or a concussion nothing lasting.
- **A limb for a life.** Once a fight, a character who would be left Dying or killed can **lose a limb, or the use of one**, instead. They live, Incapacitated but stable, and the limb becomes a complication. It replaces any grievous wound from the same hit.
- **Healing.** Grievous wounds heal on their own clock, whatever the boxes say. A lost limb, a lost eye or a disfigurement never heals.

## Weapons and damage

How many boxes a hit marks depends on the **weapon**, not on how well the attack succeeded.

| Weapon | Range | Boxes on a hit | What the injury looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Punch, kick, headbutt | Melee | 1 | Bruises, a split lip, cracked ribs |
| Club, bottle, pistol-whip | Melee | 1 | A concussion or a broken finger |
| Knife or hatchet | Melee; Close thrown | 1 | A cut or stab wound that bleeds |
| Arrow | Short | 1 | A cut or stab wound that bleeds |
| Axe, saber, or an old Grimm weapon | Melee | 2 | A deep wound or a broken bone |
| Crossbow | Short | 2 | A bolt deep in the flesh |
| Claws and fangs of a predator Wesen in full woge | Melee | 2 | Torn flesh |
| Derringer | Close (out of range beyond Short) | 2 | A gunshot wound |
| Revolver | Close | 2 | A gunshot wound |
| Rifle | Medium | 2 | A gunshot wound |
| Shotgun, at Close range | Close | 3, and Dying | A ruinous wound |
| Shotgun, at Short range | Short (no effect beyond) | 1 | Scattered pellets |
| Buffalo rifle | Long | 3, and Dying | A ruinous wound |
| Dynamite | Close (thrown) | 1 to everyone nearby, 3 and Dying at the centre | Blast and burns; Dodge means diving clear |
| Fall | — | 1 for a bad fall, 2 for a long one | A sprain or a fracture |
| Fire | — | 1 each round in the flames | Burns |

**Period ranges.** These are 1870s guns: a revolver is a close-quarters weapon, and most gunfights happen across a room or a street. A derringer is useless beyond Short range, and a shotgun beyond it does nothing.

*Example:* a Blutbad's claws leave Deputy Ames Seriously wounded (two boxes). A revolver shot that gets through marks two more, but the track stops at three: Ames is Incapacitated and Dying, and a further 2-box hit would kill. Spending a Grit point on that revolver shot reduces it to one box, leaving Ames at three boxes but, because the hit only marked one, not Dying.

## Guns and ammunition

Guns run out of rounds, and every shot counts.

| Gun | Rounds |
|---|---|
| Derringer | 2 |
| Revolver | 6 |
| Double-barrel shotgun | 2 |
| Lever-action rifle | 15 |

| Shot | Rounds | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Single shot | 1 | A normal attack |
| Fan the hammer (revolver) | 3 | One attack at −2; a hit marks one extra box |
| Both barrels (shotgun) | 2 | +2 to hit at Close range |
| Covering fire | 3 | No attack roll. Until your next turn, anyone who breaks cover in your line of fire takes −2 on their action |

- **Firing spends rounds whether the shot hits or misses.** A Grit reroll repeats the same shot and costs nothing extra.
- **An empty gun won't fire.** Reloading takes one action and one *reload* (a handful of cartridges). Restock reloads in town between jobs; having packed extra is a good use of a Grit point's *small preparation*.
- **Jams.** A natural 18 with a gun also jams it: clearing it takes an action.

To take someone down without killing them, use fists, a club, a grapple, or knock-out drops from an Apothecary.

# Medical care

Frontier medicine in 1889 has chloroform, carbolic acid, laudanum and steady hands, and not much else. Wesen heal like humans, and some injuries need remedies from older traditions.

## Diagnosis and stabilization

Roll Medicine when the diagnosis is uncertain or an urgent treatment decision matters. Emergency stabilization takes one action and a successful Medicine roll with a doctor's bag. It ends Dying and stops deterioration, but removes no boxes.

## Field treatment

With a doctor's bag and several uninterrupted minutes, successful Medicine removes one injury box; success by 5 or more removes two. Each injury receives one field-treatment attempt; further attempts need changed circumstances.

## A doctor's surgery

A proper surgery with instruments, chloroform and a clean table grants +2 to Medicine. A successful treatment removes two boxes; success by 5 or more removes three. Allow the time the operation requires.

## Remedies and poisons

Apothecary treats what Medicine can't: Wesen poisons, curses, magical sickness and Wesen-specific ailments. Finding the right remedy usually means first identifying the cause (Wesen Lore or Occult), then brewing it (Apothecary, with an apothecary kit). A remedy can substitute for field treatment against such harm.

## Natural recovery

Rest clears injury one box at a time, and the worse you're hurt, the longer each box takes. Rest means no riding, no fighting, nothing strenuous.

| Boxes marked | Rest to clear one box |
|---|---|
| 1 | A full day |
| 2 | At least three days |
| 3 | A week, and only in someone's care |

At three boxes, rest alone does nothing until someone has stabilized or treated you; after that, a week of rest in their care clears the first box. A doctor's treatment is faster. The times add up: from three boxes, rest alone takes about a week and a half to heal completely (a week, then three days, then a day).

# Grit points and Trouble

## Using Grit points

Begin each session with two Grit points (three with True grit); unused points do not carry over. Spend no more than one point on a single roll or injury event.

| Spend one point to | Timing and limits |
|---|---|
| Reroll your own roll | Declare after seeing the result but before its consequences. Keep the second result, even if worse. |
| Reduce an injury by one box | Declare when a hit would mark boxes, before applying them. Does not prevent beheading or similar fates. |
| Shrug off a telling hit | Cancel the effect an attacker picked against you; you still take the boxes. |
| Establish a small preparation | Propose a plausible detail, such as having packed an extra reload. GM approval is required. |

Gain one point when one of your complications creates a meaningful problem you accept, including a companion complication from a heritage advantage. You can decline the proposed complication without penalty.

## Trouble

The characters share three Trouble tracks. They fill as the hidden world strains against the ordinary one; the GM narrates what each box means.

| Track | Boxes | Effect as boxes fill |
|---|---|---|
| Suspicion | 4 | 1: talk in the saloon. 2: rumors; −2 to Persuasion with townsfolk. 3: the sheriff is watching you. 4: a warrant or a mob; start a *Lynch mob* clock. |
| Exposure | 4 | 1: odd stories. 2: the newspaper asks questions. 3: Wesen close ranks; −2 to Investigation among them. 4: the Wesen Council sends someone. |
| Attention | 4 | The Royals, the Order of the Holy Knife, or Reapers hunting the Grimm (the GM picks). 1: a stranger asks questions. 2: a mark or warning. 3: they're in town. 4: they strike; start a clock. |

**Marking Trouble.** A full woge seen by townsfolk, magic worked openly, a body left where it will be found, or a success at a cost can each mark a box. **Clearing Trouble.** Lying low between cases, a good cover story (Deception), or doing the town a public good can clear a box per case.

## Advancement

Characters grow by earning **advancement points** and spending them.

- **Earning.** After a significant case, the GM awards each character **1 point**. At a **milestone**, the end of a story arc, everyone earns **2 more**.
- **Spending.** Spend points when you earn them, or bank them for something bigger. Each purchase needs the GM's approval and a reason from the story: training with someone, something you lived through, a debt paid.

| Purchase | Cost | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| A skill from Untrained to Trained | 1 | — |
| A skill from Trained to Specialty | 2 | Specialty (+3) is the ceiling: only an Old hand's starting skill is Expert, unless they broaden (see Step 3) |
| Revise an advantage or complication | 1 | — |
| A new Edge | 3 | Any Edge except the attribute and Trained-skill Edges, within its own limit |
| +1 to an attribute | 4 | Once per attribute; no higher than one above the starting maximum (14, plus any heritage bonus) |

The creator keeps the ledger: award points in the advancement box under the skills (or from the GM journal), then spend them there.

### Magic advancement

An Incantare, Hexenbiest or Zauberbiest can also spend advancement points on these, with the GM's approval and a reason from the story.

| Option | Cost | Effect | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| New spell | 1 | A Veneficare learns one spell beyond their limit | Up to 2 extra |
| Deeper spell | 2 | One spell you know gets one more Boost | Each spell once |
| Another charm | 1 | Carry one more ready charm | Up to 2 extra |
| By heart | 1 | One kind of ritual takes one fewer segment (minimum 1) | Each kind once |
| Quicker hand | 1 | One ritual you know goes one pace faster: an hour becomes ten minutes, ten minutes one action | Each ritual once |
| Master of the Lens | 4 | Occult becomes Expert (+5) | Once, at the end of a long campaign |

# Making your own

The callings and heritages in this book are a starting point, not a limit. With the GM's agreement, any player can create their own calling or heritage. The creator has a **Custom** option at the end of each list, and its checklist holds a custom choice to the same rules as everything else.

## A custom calling

A calling is a concept, not a package of powers: it gives no bonus of its own. A custom one only needs a clear place in the group.

1. **Name it and describe it.** One sentence for what your character does that no one else does, in the style of the callings above: *"Undertaker: buries the dead, and makes sure they stay buried."*
2. **Choose a group.** Pick the one it belongs with: The Law, The Learned or The Frontier. The group only sets your sheet's colors and emblem.
3. **Pick four core skills.** The skills it can't do without. Mark them Trained yourself in step 3 (the built-in callings do this for you); they count toward your normal Trained skills.
4. **Check it with the GM.** It should fit the setting and overlap the existing callings as little as possible.

**In the creator:** in step 1, choose **Custom calling…** at the end of the Calling list, then enter its name and group. Write the description in *What you bring*.

## A custom heritage

Build it with the GM, one step at a time.

### 1. The concept

Two or three sentences: where they come from, what sets them apart, and how others see them. It should be something the heritages in this book don't already cover.

### 2. The tier

The tier measures how much the heritage gives on its own. Each tier costs one of your 3 Edge points, so stronger heritages start with fewer Edges.

| Tier | Edges | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 3 | Ordinary people: a homeland, upbringing or culture, with no special abilities |
| 1 | 2 | One small, constant edge: keen senses, a tough hide, a free Trained skill, resistance to one hazard |
| 2 | 1 | Clear inhuman or supernatural gifts: venom, a second form, strong resistances |
| 3 | 0 | Remarkable beings: several strong abilities, and serious weaknesses to match |

When in doubt, choose the higher tier.

### 3. Attribute adjustments

Give adjustments from **−2 to +2** per attribute that **total zero**: a +2 needs a −2 or two −1s. The adjustments move the attribute's base (+1 starts it at 11) and raise its starting maximum (13 plus the bonus). Most heritages change two to four attributes.

### 4. Core rules (tier 1 and up)

One to three short rules that always apply: what they can do (see in the dark, resist cold, a free Trained skill) and what it costs (a weakness, a people's distrust, a hazard they can't abide). A strength with no drawback is worth more than it looks, so price it a tier higher.

### 5. Heritage advantages

Write one to three. Each works like a general advantage: **+2 to one skill in a narrow situation**, or a small, limited permission ("once per scene…"). Each comes with a **companion complication**: a problem the trait causes, with a clear trigger and a real consequence, which earns a Grit point when it bites, like any complication. A character takes one advantage, general or heritage, so make them distinct choices.

### 6. A woge (optional, tier 2 or 3)

If the heritage changes shape, give the form attribute bonuses (usually +2 to one attribute and +1 to another), two or three traits, and its drawbacks. The shared rules for forms in *The woge and the hidden world* apply.

### In the creator

1. In step 2, choose **Custom heritage…** at the end of the Heritage list.
2. Name it, choose the tier you agreed, and set its adjustments. The checklist confirms they total zero.
3. Write its core rules in **Other ability (GM approval required)** in step 4.
4. In step 4, choose **Custom heritage advantage…** and enter the advantage and its companion complication.

### Example

**Hundjäger-blooded (tier 1).** A human family with a little Wesen blood: no woge, but the instincts. *Adjustments:* Body +1, Presence −1. *Core rule:* keen nose: you smell fear and blood on anyone close by. *Heritage advantage:* **Bloodhound's Nose**: +2 to Tracking by scent. *Companion:* **Wesen Kin**: Wesen recognize their own, and some want you back in the fold; accept the visit at the worst time.

# Quick reference

**Creation:** attributes start at 10, adjusted by heritage (always totaling zero), plus 5 points; maximum 13 plus any heritage bonus. 3 points split between heritage tier and Edges (Tier 0: 3 Edges; Tier 3: none). Well-rounded: one Specialty and nine Trained. Old hand: one Expert at +5 and seven Trained. One advantage (general or heritage) and one complication; an optional Allegiance.

**Skills:** Untrained = attribute −4; Trained = attribute +1; Specialty = attribute +3; Expert (Old hand) = attribute +5.

**Modifiers:** favorable +2; ordinary 0; difficult −2; very difficult −4; extreme −6. Natural 3–4 succeeds; natural 17–18 fails.

**Assistance:** success +1; success by 5 or more +2; 18 causes a complication. One assistant per roll.

**Combat:** move plus one action. Hit → one defense a round (Dodge, Parry or Block; −1 to −3 against a clean hit) → weapon effect. Dodge = Agility −2 plus Athletics training (+0, +1, +2). Telling hit (by 5+ or natural 3–4): the attacker picks an effect; called shot −2 (small target −4).

**Damage:** fists, clubs, knives and arrows 1 box; guns, heavy blades and Wesen claws 2; a close-range shotgun or buffalo rifle 3 and Dying. A 2-box weapon that brings you to three boxes leaves you Dying; at three boxes, another 2-box hit kills.

**Guns:** derringer 2, revolver 6, shotgun 2, rifle 15. Reload: one action. Natural 18 jams.

**Medicine:** stabilize in one action. Field care clears 1 box (2 on margin 5+). A surgery gives +2 and clears 2 (3 on margin 5+). Rest clears a box in a day at 1 box, three days at 2, a week at 3 (in someone's care).

**Woge:** free action for a Wesen; a forced woge on a failed Will roll. The woge profile's bonuses apply while woged. Grimms see woge under strong emotion. A full woge before townsfolk marks Exposure (2 boxes at Tier 3).

**Rituals:** describe the effect; fill a clock of 2, 4 or 6 segments with Occult rolls at your Lens's pace. Trappings remove a segment. Charms: Trained 2, Specialty 3, Expert 4.

**Grit:** start each session with two (three with True grit). Reroll, reduce an injury by one box, shrug off a telling hit, or establish a small preparation. Earn one from an accepted complication.
