The Oregon frontier is wide and the law is thin. Timber camps, silver strikes, the railroad and the river steamers bring strangers from every corner of the world, and some of them are not what they seem. Wesen walk among the settlers wearing human faces. Grimms, the old hunters, can see through them. The Royal Families pull strings from across the ocean, and men and women with real magic hide inside fraternal lodges and country churches. You ride with a small posse that knows the truth and tries to keep the peace, or at least to keep people alive.

This guide uses the same rules as the USS Pelion game: concrete skills, 3d6 roll-under, and a small pool of Grit points. It is an unofficial fan game inspired by the television series *Grimm* and is not affiliated with its makers.

## The rule to remember

Roll 3d6 at or below your adjusted skill to succeed. Your skill already includes your training and attribute. Do not add the attribute again.

*Example:* Tracking 13, with a −2 penalty for rain, gives a target of 11. A roll of 9 succeeds by 2. A roll of 12 fails by 1.

## The Grimm-verse

**Wesen** look like ordinary people but have a hidden form, almost always a two-legged blend of human and animal. The change between the two is the **woge** (VOH-gə). Each kind of Wesen has its own culture and traditions, and many of the oldest involve hunting or eating humans. The Blutbad, a wolf-like Wesen, gave rise to the werewolf legend. Their names are German, as are many of the old words of this hidden world.

**Grimms** are supernaturally gifted warriors, stronger and faster than ordinary people, who can see the Wesen hidden among us. Their old Latin name, *Decapitare*, comes from their habit of beheading the creatures they hunt. The gift passes only occasionally, sometimes skipping a generation or two, and the Grimm families keep themselves anonymous. Grimms enforce the **Code of Swabia**, the old law between Wesen and the rest of the world. Some Grimms enforce it fairly; others believe every Wesen is an abomination to be destroyed.

The **seven Royal Families** are ancient, wealthy and influential. They no longer rule whole countries, but they still manipulate events through their public cousins, field loyal Wesen armies, and seek control over all Wesen and over ordinary humanity too.

**Incantare** is the catch-all name for anyone who works real magic. Most magic is ritual, in many styles and beliefs, and each practitioner sees it through a **Lens**. The Wesen Council and the Royal Families both consider Incantare extremely dangerous, and Grimms are often tasked with watching or destroying them. **The blood of a Grimm is a powerful anti-magic agent:** injected or drunk, it neutralizes the powers of any human or Wesen magic user, and it kills the Wesen spirit within a Hexenbiest or Zauberbiest.

Three orders matter on the frontier:

- **Bruderschaft der Heiligen Erde** (Brotherhood of the Holy Earth) protects the Earth from industry. Luddites with magic, they claim descent from druidic orders, and the railroad is their enemy.
- **Ordo Sancti Cultelli** (Order of the Holy Knife) is a Church-affiliated order of assassins sworn to cleanse the Earth of supernatural threats, Wesen and Incantare alike.
- **Ordo Hermeticus Aurorae Aureae** (the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn) presents itself as a fraternal order with odd trappings. Inside it, layer under layer, are secrets and real magicians.

## Black Pine, Oregon

The default home of the posse is Black Pine, a timber and silver town in the Cascade foothills, two days' ride from Portland. It has a sheriff who would rather not know, a newspaper that would very much like to, a railroad survey camp, and more Wesen than anyone admits. Use it, or set your game anywhere in the West.

# Create your character

Work through the five steps in order. The Grimm Wild West Character Creator follows the same steps and checks the rules for you.

## Step 1: Choose your calling

Choose a calling and describe what you bring to the posse. Your calling places you in one of three groups, which only changes your sheet's colors and badge. A title such as "Deputy U.S. Marshal" or "Doc" is agreed with the GM and gives no bonus.

| Calling | Group | Typical contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Marshal | The Law | Lead the posse and carry what authority there is |
| Deputy | The Law | Keep order, guard prisoners, back up the marshal |
| Detective | The Law | Investigate crimes for the Pinkertons or a private client |
| Bounty Hunter | The Law | Track and bring in wanted men, and things that aren't men |
| Doctor | The Learned | Patch up the posse and read what a body tells you |
| Apothecary | The Learned | Brew remedies, antidotes and stranger things |
| Scholar | The Learned | Know the old books, the old languages and the occult |
| Preacher | The Learned | Comfort, counsel, and stand against the dark |
| Gunslinger | The Frontier | Be the fastest and steadiest gun in the room |
| Scout | The Frontier | Find the trail, read the land, keep the posse alive in it |
| Gambler | The Frontier | Read people, bluff, and hear what the saloon hears |
| Tinker | The Frontier | Fix guns, build traps, and make the impossible gadget |

### Well-rounded or Old hand

Choose how your character trained (see Step 3):

- **Well-rounded:** one Specialty at +3 and nine Trained skills.
- **Old hand:** one Expert skill at +5 and seven Trained skills. Old hands have spent a lifetime at one thing.

## Step 2: Choose a heritage and assign attributes

### Attributes

| Attribute | Used for |
|---|---|
| Body | Strength, endurance, resisting injury |
| Agility | Coordination, reflexes, movement |
| Intellect | Reasoning, observation, knowledge |
| Presence | Leadership, persuasion, social influence |
| Will | Resolve, concentration, resisting fear and magic |

### Heritage adjustments

Every attribute starts at 10. Your heritage then adjusts some of them; the result is your **heritage base**. Each heritage's adjustments total zero, so no heritage starts ahead of another.

| Heritage | Adjustments |
|---|---|
| Human | None |
| Grimm | Body +1, Agility +1, Presence −2 |
| Incantare | Intellect +1, Will +1, Body −1, Presence −1 |
| Blutbad | Body +1, Agility +1, Intellect −1, Will −1 |
| Fuchsbau | Intellect +1, Presence +1, Body −1, Will −1 |
| Eisbiber | Intellect +1, Will −1 |
| Hexenbiest or Zauberbiest | Will +1, Presence +1, Body −1, Agility −1 |
| Jägerbar | Body +2, Agility −1, Intellect −1 |
| Bauerschwein | Body +1, Will +1, Agility −1, Presence −1 |
| Mauzhertz | Agility +1, Body −1 |
| Ziegevolk | Presence +2, Intellect −1, Will −1 |
| Steinadler | Agility +1, Intellect +1, Body −1, Will −1 |
| Coyotl | Agility +1, Presence +1, Intellect −1, Will −1 |

### Spending points

After applying your heritage, distribute **five additional points** among the attributes.

- The starting maximum is **13**, plus any heritage bonus to that attribute. A Jägerbar can start with Body 15.
- You cannot lower an attribute below its heritage base to gain points.

### Heritage descriptions

**Human.** Most of the frontier, and most of the posse. You know the truth about the hidden world, which already sets you apart.

**Grimm.** A descendant of the old hunters, stronger and faster than ordinary people. You see through a Wesen's human face whenever it is stirred by strong emotion. Wesen who recognize you are afraid of you, and with reason.

**Incantare.** A human who works real magic through a Lens: a tradition, a belief, a way of seeing. Choose your Lens as your heritage advantage. The Council, the Royals and the Order of the Holy Knife all watch for people like you.

**Blutbad.** Wolf-like Wesen with keen senses and a predator's appetite. Some live clean, abstaining from the hunt, and others never try.

**Fuchsbau.** Fox-like Wesen, clever and charming, with a long tradition of trade in herbs, remedies and harder-to-find ingredients.

**Eisbiber.** Beaver-like Wesen: builders, neighbors and gossips, close-knit and peaceable, and easily frightened by violence.

**Hexenbiest or Zauberbiest.** Witch-like Wesen (Zauberbiest for the men) with innate magic. Their power lives in their Wesen spirit, which Grimm blood can kill.

**Jägerbar.** Bear-like Wesen, enormously strong, with old hunting rites that their families still keep.

**Bauerschwein.** Pig-like Wesen: stubborn, hard-working, and old enemies of the Blutbaden.

**Mauzhertz.** Mouse-like Wesen, small, quick and wary, with excellent hearing.

**Ziegevolk.** Goat-like Wesen whose pheromones make them unnaturally persuasive, and unnaturally popular.

**Steinadler.** Eagle-like Wesen with remarkable eyesight and a hunter's pride.

**Coyotl.** Coyote-like Wesen of the desert country who live in clans bound by old customs.

### Custom heritage

Agree on a heritage with the GM and give it adjustments from −2 to +2 per attribute that total zero. The normal starting maximum and heritage base rules apply.

## Step 3: Choose trained skills

A **Well-rounded** character chooses ten different skills: one **Specialty** at its governing attribute +3 and nine **Trained** skills at attribute +1. An **Old hand** chooses **one Expert skill at +5** and **seven Trained skills at +1**. All other skills are **Untrained** at attribute −4 when an attempt is plausible. Each skill has its own final target number; do not add the attribute again when rolling.

The Expert's extra bonus matters most when the work is hard. With Intellect 13, an Expert gambler has a target of 18, which only natural 17–18 can fail; under a −4 "very difficult" penalty the target is still 14. If Athletics is the Expert skill, Dodge adds +2, the same as a Specialty.

**Broadening.** An Old hand can broaden later (the GM's call, usually after a significant case): the Expert skill becomes a Specialty at +3, and two more skills become Trained.

## Step 4: Describe your character

Choose your name, title if any, and a short reason you ride with the posse. Then choose **one advantage** and **one complication**.

### Top 10 reasons to ride with the posse

1. **The family gift.** The Grimm blood woke in me, and it came with a duty.
2. **Vengeance.** Something that wore a human face killed someone I loved.
3. **Protect the town.** Somebody has to stand between these people and what's out there.
4. **Keep the peace.** Wesen and humans can live side by side, and I mean to prove it.
5. **I saw it.** I saw what I saw, and no one else believes me.
6. **Redemption.** I did harm once. I mean to balance the ledger.
7. **The bounty.** The pay's good, and nobody else will take these jobs.
8. **Knowledge.** The hidden world is the greatest mystery there is, and I want to understand it.
9. **Faith.** The Lord sent me here for a reason, and I believe this is it.
10. **Nowhere else to go.** The posse took me in when no one else would.

- **Advantage.** Choose either a general advantage or one of your heritage advantages. A heritage advantage replaces the general advantage and always brings its companion complication. An Incantare's heritage advantage is their **Lens**.
- **Complication.** Choose one complication. You keep it even if you take a heritage advantage, so a character with a heritage advantage has two complications.

An advantage gives +2 to one narrowly defined kind of task when the GM agrees it applies. It cannot improve every use of a broad skill. A complication creates choices and problems; it is not a permanent penalty.

## Step 5: Prepare to ride

Your Dodge is Agility −2, plus your Athletics training bonus: +0 if Untrained, +1 if Trained, or +2 if a Specialty or Expert. Begin uninjured, with two Grit points and your gear. Describe one connection to another member of the posse.

### Top 10 connections to another rider

1. **Rode together in the war.** We served in the same company and still watch each other's backs.
2. **They saved my life.** They cut me down from a lynch mob's rope, and I owe them my life.
3. **I saved theirs.** I pulled them out of a burning barn, and they haven't forgotten it.
4. **Mentor.** They taught me what I know about the hidden world.
5. **Friendly rivals.** We're always competing over the same bounty, the same card game or the same sweetheart.
6. **Same hometown.** We grew up in the same town and know each other's families.
7. **An unpaid debt.** I owe them money, or a favor, and I haven't paid it yet.
8. **A shared secret.** We share a secret from a job neither of us talks about.
9. **Saloon regulars.** We meet every Saturday night for cards, whiskey and lies.
10. **Bad blood.** They blame me for something that went wrong on a past job.

## Example detective

Human. Body 10, Agility 10, Intellect 13, Presence 11, Will 11.

- **Specialty:** Investigation 16.
- **Trained:** Read People 14, Wesen Lore 14, Tracking 14, Survival 14, Gambling 14, Persuasion 12, Deception 12, Shooting 11, Riding 11.
- **Dodge:** Athletics is Untrained, so Dodge is 8.
- **Advantage:** Astute Observer. **Complication:** Cannot Leave a Mystery Alone.

| Wesen Lore | Intellect | Identify Wesen, their customs, strengths and weaknesses |
| Occult | Intellect | Rituals, magical traditions, curses and secret orders |
| Medicine | Intellect | Diagnose, treat and perform surgery |

## Untrained actions

A plausible untrained attempt uses attribute −4. Specialized work such as surgery, a ritual, or brewing a Wesen remedy requires relevant training or direct guidance. Guidance permits an attempt only when the GM finds it credible; it does not grant the expert's skill.

## Gear

Gear makes tasks possible rather than easier. Ordinary gear grants no blanket bonus; exceptional tools may justify +2. Missing essential tools can make a task impossible, or very difficult (−4) when the GM finds an attempt plausible.

| Item | What it does |
|---|---|
| Revolver | Six shots; reloading takes an action. Hits mark 2 boxes. See Guns and ammunition. |
| Rifle | A lever-action repeater with fifteen shots and long range. Hits mark 2 boxes. |
| Shotgun | Two barrels. Devastating close up (3 boxes and Dying), weak beyond a few paces (1 box). |
| Knife | A tool and a weapon; hits mark 1 box. |
| Doctor's bag | Instruments, laudanum, carbolic and bandages. Needed to stabilize a patient and give field treatment. |
| Apothecary kit | Dried herbs, powders, Wesen ingredients, mortar and scales. Needed for remedies, antidotes and potions away from a shop. |
| Tool roll | Gunsmith's and carpenter's tools. Needed for most Tinkering in the field. |
| Horse and tack | Carries you across the territory. Riding in a chase needs one. |
| Trail kit | Bedroll, canteen, rations, rope and flint for a few days. Turns impossible survival and climbing tasks into ordinary ones; no bonus beyond that. |
| Lantern and matches | Light for a few hours. Searching in the dark without light is very difficult (−4) or impossible. |
| Grimm's journals | Copied pages from a Grimm family's books. With an hour to consult them, +2 to Wesen Lore to identify a Wesen or find its weakness. Irreplaceable, and coveted. |
| Dynamite | Three sticks. Throw with Athletics; the fuse burns for a round. See Weapons and damage. |

# Actions and teamwork

## When to roll

Routine work succeeds automatically when you have appropriate training, gear and time. Roll when failure could change the situation. Before rolling, establish your goal, the relevant skill, any modifiers, and what is at stake.

| Circumstances | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Favorable conditions or excellent preparation | +2 |
| Ordinary challenge under pressure | 0 |
| Difficult | −2 |
| Very difficult | −4 |
| Extreme | −6 |

Apply the main circumstance modifier once. Add separately specified effects such as assistance or injury when they apply. The GM avoids charging twice for the same obstacle.

## Results and margins

Add the three dice. A total at or below the adjusted skill succeeds. Margin of success equals the adjusted skill minus the roll. A larger margin may mean better information, more precise work, or a stronger position.

A natural 3 or 4 always succeeds. A natural 17 or 18 always fails. An impossible action receives no roll.

In an opposed contest, each side rolls its relevant skill. One success beats one failure. If both succeed, the larger success margin wins. A tie, or two failures, favors the defender or preserves the situation. For an automatic success above its target, count its contest margin as zero.

## Assistance

One qualified assistant can roll a relevant skill before the lead character rolls. Success grants +1 to the lead roll; success by 5 or more grants +2. Failure gives no bonus. An 18 introduces a complication described by the GM. Additional riders can do other tasks but cannot stack assistance bonuses on the same roll.

## Failure and another attempt

Failure changes the situation: time passes, the trail goes cold, supplies run out, or danger grows. Do not repeat an unchanged roll until it succeeds. A new approach, new evidence, better tools, or more time may justify another attempt.

## Success at a cost

When a roll fails by only **1 or 2**, the GM may offer **success at a cost**: you get what you were after, but something else goes wrong. Typical costs are an injury box, a lost horse or broken gun, lost time (tick a clock), a box of Trouble, or someone noticing. The GM names the cost before you accept; you can always take the plain failure instead. A natural 17 or 18 is never a success at a cost.

## Progress clocks

A **clock** is a circle cut into 4, 6 or 8 segments that tracks something that takes time or builds toward disaster: *The mob forms (6)*, *Nightfall (4)*, *Find the lair (6)*, *Win the clan's trust (8)*.

- **Danger clocks** fill when things go wrong: a failed roll, a success at a cost, or time passing. When one fills, the danger happens.
- **Progress clocks** fill as the posse succeeds: one segment for a success, two for success by 5 or more. When one fills, the goal is achieved.
- The GM sizes the clock (4 = urgent or simple, 6 = substantial, 8 = long or complex) and shows it to the players.

## Investigation and social scenes

Ask concrete questions and explain your approach. Evidence may require several different tasks. Persuasion can secure cooperation, Read People offers insight into behavior, and Wesen Lore explains what the evidence means. A successful roll does not force someone to abandon a core belief or reveal what they do not know.

## Chases

Run a chase as a progress clock for the pursuers against a danger clock for the quarry's escape. Each exchange, riders roll **Riding** (or Athletics on foot): success ticks the pursuers' clock, failure ticks the escape. Terrain, a tired horse, or a shot fired from the saddle (Shooting at −2) adjust the rolls.

# The woge and the hidden world

## Wesen and the woge

A Wesen character can **woge** at will, as a free action, and return just as easily. Physical heritage advantages, such as claws or a Blutbad's fighting form, work only in **full woge**.

**Losing control.** Fury, terror or pain can force a woge. When it matters, the GM calls for a **Will** roll; on a failure you woge, briefly or fully as the GM decides.

**Who sees it.** A full woge is visible to anyone. A **Grimm** sees a flash of the Wesen face whenever a Wesen is stirred by strong emotion, even when the Wesen means to hide it. A Wesen who looks into the eyes of a Grimm in that moment knows the Grimm for what they are.

**Exposure.** A full woge in front of ordinary people breaks the Code of Swabia. Mark a box of **Exposure** on the Trouble tracks.

## Grimms

A Grimm sees woge as described above, and is stronger and faster than ordinary people; the Grimm heritage adjustments reflect that. Grimms enforce the Code, and Wesen know it, so many flee, lie or fight on recognizing one. A Grimm's blood neutralizes magic: drunk or injected, it strips an Incantare or Wesen magic user of their power, and it kills the Wesen spirit in a Hexenbiest or Zauberbiest. Who gets that blood, and how, is always a serious matter.

## Incantare and their Lenses

An Incantare's **Lens** is their heritage advantage: an ability, not a +2. To work magic, describe the effect, then roll **Occult** (or the skill the Lens names). The GM sets what the magic can do, how long it takes and what it needs **before** the roll. Most magic is ritual: it needs minutes or hours, components, and a place to work. Quick magic in a fight is the exception, not the rule.

Magic draws attention. Using it openly can mark a box of **Exposure**, and the Order of the Holy Knife hunts known Incantare. Grimm blood neutralizes any Incantare's magic.

## The Code of Swabia

The Code of Swabia is the old law between Wesen and the rest of the world, and Grimms are its enforcers. At its heart, Wesen must not reveal themselves to humanity. How far it reaches beyond that, and how mercifully a Grimm enforces it, is a question your posse will answer for itself.

# Personal combat

## Turn order and actions

Act in descending Agility order. Players choose the order of tied riders; the GM resolves other ties. A round lasts a few seconds. On your turn, move a short distance and take one meaningful action, such as shooting, reloading, helping, or attempting a task.

## Attacking and defending

Roll Shooting for a ranged attack, Melee for a weapon in hand, or Brawling for fists and grappling. If the attack succeeds, an aware target able to move may attempt Dodge. Dodge is Agility −2, with +1 for Trained Athletics or +2 for Athletics as a Specialty or Expert. Each target gets one Dodge attempt per round. A successful Dodge cancels the hit. Apply injury penalties to Dodge.

Solid cover blocks a shot. Partial cover imposes −2 on the attack. Weapon effects apply after a hit survives any Dodge. Success margin does not increase damage.

## Injury

| Boxes marked | Condition | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Uninjured | No injury penalty |
| 1 | Wounded | −1 to physical actions |
| 2 | Seriously wounded | −2 to physical actions |
| 3 | Incapacitated | Unable to act; medical aid needed |

Injury penalties replace each other rather than stacking. They apply to movement, attacks, Dodge, and other tasks the injury physically hinders.

### Dying and death

- **Dying.** A hit from a weapon that marks **2 or more boxes** and brings you to three boxes leaves you Incapacitated and **Dying**. A Dying character dies at the end of the scene unless someone stabilizes them: one action and a successful Medicine roll with a doctor's bag.
- **A further hit.** While you are at three boxes, any hit from a weapon that marks 2 or more boxes kills you. Fists, clubs and knives just keep you down.
- **Beheading** and comparable fates bypass the track entirely.

Your protection is avoiding the hit: Dodge, cover, and a Grit point, which reduces a hit by one box.

## Weapons and damage

How many boxes a hit marks depends on the **weapon**, not on how well the attack succeeded.

| Weapon | Boxes on a hit | What the injury looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Punch, kick, headbutt | 1 | Bruises, a split lip, cracked ribs |
| Club, bottle, pistol-whip | 1 | A concussion or a broken finger |
| Knife, hatchet, arrow | 1 | A cut or stab wound that bleeds |
| Axe, saber, or an old Grimm weapon | 2 | A deep wound or a broken bone |
| Crossbow | 2 | A bolt deep in the flesh |
| Claws and fangs of a predator Wesen in full woge | 2 | Torn flesh |
| Derringer, revolver or rifle | 2 | A gunshot wound |
| Shotgun, within a few paces | 3, and Dying | A ruinous wound |
| Shotgun, farther away | 1 | Scattered pellets |
| Buffalo rifle | 3, and Dying | A ruinous wound |
| Dynamite | 1 to everyone nearby, 3 and Dying at the centre | Blast and burns; Dodge means diving clear |
| Fall | 1 for a bad fall, 2 for a long one | A sprain or a fracture |
| Fire | 1 each round in the flames | Burns |

*Example:* a Blutbad's claws leave Deputy Ames Seriously wounded (two boxes). A revolver shot that gets through marks two more, but the track stops at three: Ames is Incapacitated and Dying, and a further 2-box hit would kill. Spending a Grit point on that revolver shot reduces it to one box, leaving Ames at three boxes but, because the hit only marked one, not Dying.

## Guns and ammunition

Guns run out of rounds, and every shot counts.

| Gun | Rounds |
|---|---|
| Derringer | 2 |
| Revolver | 6 |
| Double-barrel shotgun | 2 |
| Lever-action rifle | 15 |

| Shot | Rounds | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Single shot | 1 | A normal attack |
| Fan the hammer (revolver) | 3 | One attack at −2; a hit marks one extra box |
| Both barrels (shotgun) | 2 | +2 to hit at close range |
| Covering fire | 3 | No attack roll. Until your next turn, anyone who breaks cover in your line of fire takes −2 on their action |

- **Firing spends rounds whether the shot hits or misses.** A Grit reroll repeats the same shot and costs nothing extra.
- **An empty gun won't fire.** Reloading takes one action and one *reload* (a handful of cartridges). Restock reloads in town between jobs; having packed extra is a good use of a Grit point's *small preparation*.
- **Jams.** A natural 18 with a gun also jams it: clearing it takes an action.

To take someone down without killing them, use fists, a club, a grapple, or knock-out drops from an Apothecary.

# Medical care

Frontier medicine in 1889 has chloroform, carbolic acid, laudanum and steady hands, and not much else. Wesen heal like humans, and some injuries need remedies from older traditions.

## Diagnosis and stabilization

Roll Medicine when the diagnosis is uncertain or an urgent treatment decision matters. Emergency stabilization takes one action and a successful Medicine roll with a doctor's bag. It ends Dying and stops deterioration, but removes no boxes.

## Field treatment

With a doctor's bag and several uninterrupted minutes, successful Medicine removes one injury box; success by 5 or more removes two. Each injury receives one field-treatment attempt; further attempts need changed circumstances.

## A doctor's surgery

A proper surgery with instruments, chloroform and a clean table grants +2 to Medicine. A successful treatment removes two boxes; success by 5 or more removes three. Allow the time the operation requires.

## Remedies and poisons

Apothecary treats what Medicine can't: Wesen poisons, curses, magical sickness and Wesen-specific ailments. Finding the right remedy usually means first identifying the cause (Wesen Lore or Occult), then brewing it (Apothecary, with an apothecary kit). A remedy can substitute for field treatment against such harm.

## Natural recovery

Each **two full days of rest** clear one injury box. Rest means no riding, no fighting, nothing strenuous. At three boxes, rest alone clears nothing: treatment must clear the first box.

# Grit points and Trouble

## Using Grit points

Begin each session with two Grit points; unused points do not carry over. Spend no more than one point on a single roll or injury event.

| Spend one point to | Timing and limits |
|---|---|
| Reroll your own roll | Declare after seeing the result but before its consequences. Keep the second result, even if worse. |
| Reduce an injury by one box | Declare when a hit would mark boxes, before applying them. Does not prevent beheading or similar fates. |
| Establish a small preparation | Propose a plausible detail, such as having packed an extra reload. GM approval is required. |

Gain one point when one of your complications creates a meaningful problem you accept, including a companion complication from a heritage advantage. You can decline the proposed complication without penalty.

## Trouble

The posse shares three Trouble tracks. They fill as the hidden world strains against the ordinary one; the GM narrates what each box means.

| Track | Boxes | Effect as boxes fill |
|---|---|---|
| Suspicion | 4 | 1: talk in the saloon. 2: rumors; −2 to Persuasion with townsfolk. 3: the sheriff is watching you. 4: a warrant or a mob; start a *Lynch mob* clock. |
| Exposure | 4 | 1: odd stories. 2: the newspaper asks questions. 3: Wesen close ranks; −2 to Investigation among them. 4: the Wesen Council sends someone. |
| Attention | 4 | The Royals, the Order of the Holy Knife, or Reapers hunting the Grimm (the GM picks). 1: a stranger asks questions. 2: a mark or warning. 3: they're in town. 4: they strike; start a clock. |

**Marking Trouble.** A full woge seen by townsfolk, magic worked openly, a body left where it will be found, or a success at a cost can each mark a box. **Clearing Trouble.** Lying low between cases, a good cover story (Deception), or doing the town a public good can clear a box per case.

## Advancement

After a significant case, the GM may authorize one relevant skill to advance from Untrained to Trained or from Trained to Specialty, or allow revision of an advantage or complication. Only an Old hand's starting skill is Expert; a skill raised later tops out at Specialty (+3) unless they broaden (see Step 3).

# Running cases

## Case seeds

Roll 1d12 on each table, or pick what you like: a *case* at a *place*, caused by *the hidden world*, turned by a *twist*, against a *ticking clock*, with a *key character* at its heart. The GM view in the creator rolls these for you.

## The case journal

End each case with a short journal entry, written together, as the Grimms have always kept their books:

1. **The case.** What happened, and did we resolve it: closed, partly, or failed?
2. **Who stood out?** A moment worth remembering.
3. **Complications.** Which ones came into play, and what did they cost?
4. **For the books.** A new Wesen kind, weakness or custom worth recording.
5. **Advancement.** After a significant case, the GM may authorize one skill step for each rider.

# Complications

Choose one complication. These are sources of meaningful choices, not constant penalties. The GM proposes a concrete problem and offers one Grit point if you accept it. Every complication needs a trigger agreed with the GM; the creator suggests three for each.

| Complication | What it does |
|---|---|
| Cannot Leave a Mystery Alone | A strange discovery tempts you to linger, spend something scarce, or take a risky look. Accept a specific cost. |
| Protects Others at Personal Risk | You shield someone or volunteer for a hazardous rescue at a real cost to yourself. |
| Code of the West | You won't shoot an unarmed man, break your word, or strike from behind. Accept the setback your code causes. |
| Law and Order | You insist on arrest, trial and due process, even for Wesen. Accept the delay or danger it costs. |
| Reputation to Uphold | Pride tempts you to refuse help or take on too much. Take a real consequence when it matters. |
| Haunted by the War | A specific reminder of the war causes hesitation or a hard choice. Define the reminder together. |
| Keeps a Dangerous Secret | An agreed secret threatens trust or safety. Earn a point when protecting or revealing it has a real consequence. |
| Old Rival | A recurring rival complicates your work with concrete problems. |
| Someone Back Home | A family or a sweetheart pulls you away from the posse's work. |
| Sworn Vengeance | You've sworn to kill someone. Accept the risk when the chance comes. |
| Short Fuse | You answer insults with fists or guns. Accept the fight you start. |
| The Bottle | Whiskey or laudanum has a hold on you. Accept the lost night or the slip at a bad time. |
| Can't Walk Away from a Game | A card game, a bet or a dare is irresistible. Accept the time or money lost. |
| Wanted | There's a price on your head somewhere. Accept trouble when lawmen or bounty hunters come. |
| Owes the Wrong People | A debt to dangerous people comes due. Accept their demand. |
| Reluctant to Kill | You seek a way that doesn't end a life, even a Wesen's. Accept the extra danger. |

# Quick reference

**Creation:** attributes start at 10, adjusted by heritage (always totaling zero), plus 5 points; maximum 13 plus any heritage bonus. Well-rounded: one Specialty and nine Trained. Old hand: one Expert at +5 and seven Trained. One advantage (general or heritage) and one complication.

**Skills:** Untrained = attribute −4; Trained = attribute +1; Specialty = attribute +3; Expert (Old hand) = attribute +5.

**Modifiers:** favorable +2; ordinary 0; difficult −2; very difficult −4; extreme −6. Natural 3–4 succeeds; natural 17–18 fails.

**Assistance:** success +1; success by 5 or more +2; 18 causes a complication. One assistant per roll.

**Combat:** move plus one action. Hit → optional Dodge → weapon effect. Dodge = Agility −2 plus Athletics training (+0, +1, +2).

**Damage:** fists, clubs, knives and arrows 1 box; guns, heavy blades and Wesen claws 2; a close-range shotgun or buffalo rifle 3 and Dying. A 2-box weapon that brings you to three boxes leaves you Dying; at three boxes, another 2-box hit kills.

**Guns:** derringer 2, revolver 6, shotgun 2, rifle 15. Reload: one action. Natural 18 jams.

**Medicine:** stabilize in one action. Field care clears 1 box (2 on margin 5+). A surgery gives +2 and clears 2 (3 on margin 5+). Rest: one box per two days, not from three.

**Woge:** free action for a Wesen; a forced woge on a failed Will roll. Grimms see woge under strong emotion. A full woge before townsfolk marks Exposure.

**Grit:** start each session with two. Reroll, reduce an injury by one box, or establish a small preparation. Earn one from an accepted complication.
