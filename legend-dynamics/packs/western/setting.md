<!-- SECTION:intro -->

The railroad is coming, and it is bringing more than settlers. In the fog-bound timber towns and lamplit mining camps, the old things of the world have followed the wagons west: wolves that walk as men, the dead that will not stay buried, pale strangers who only come out after dark, and witches who know their names. *Trails of Blood & Dust* is a Gothic Western for the Legend Dynamics RPG system. Most people never see the darkness. You do, and you stand between it and them.

Trails of Blood & Dust is not tied to a single place or year. Set your game in any territory, from the 1860s to the 1890s.

{{GEN:arms-table}}

**Period ranges.** These are 1870s guns: a revolver is a close-quarters weapon, and most gunfights happen across a room or a street. A derringer is useless beyond Short range, and a shotgun beyond it does nothing.

*Example:* an outlaw's knife leaves Deputy Ames Wounded (one box). A revolver shot that gets through marks two more: Ames is at three boxes, Incapacitated and Dying, and a further 2-box hit would kill. Spending a Grit point on that revolver shot reduces it to one box, leaving Ames Seriously wounded and still standing.

To take someone down without killing them, use fists, a club, a grapple, or a lasso.

### Fighting in Trails of Blood & Dust

Trails of Blood & Dust adds these to the attack and defense rules above.

- **Shields.** A real shield is rare out West: a hide shield or an iron-bound buckler gives +1 to Block. Anything at hand stands in: a chair, a wooden slat, a crate lid, a saddle.
- **Careful Marksman.** Aiming with the advantage gives +3 in all: Aim's +1 and the advantage's +2.
- **A heavy coat.** A buffalo coat or a canvas duster turns a blade, a claw or an arrow, but not a bullet. Once per fight, a hit of 2 or more boxes from a blade, claw, fang or missile marks one fewer.

*Example:* a werewolf's claws succeed by 5 against Deputy Ames, who hasn't defended yet this round. Dodge is 11, −2 for the clean hit: Ames needs a 9 or less. A parry with the saber would be Melee 14 −3 −2 = 9, no better, so Ames dodges. When the second wolf lunges before Ames's next turn, there's no defense left, unless Ames has Additional Defense: a second defense at −2, on top of the attack-quality penalty.

### Stagecoach chases

A coach on the road with riders closing in is a chase where distance is everything. Run it on the battle map's **Stagecoach chase** track, in the GM view, instead of the chase clocks.

- **The lanes.** The stagecoach has its own lane, and each rider stands in a range band from it: Close (alongside), Short, Medium, Long or Extreme. Riders usually start at Medium or Long.
- **Each exchange.** The driver rolls Riding against each rider's Riding (11 for a threat without it). The winner moves that rider one band: closer if the rider won, farther if the driver did. A tie leaves it where it is, and a rider pushed past Extreme drops out of the chase. A fresh horse or a good road gives +2; rough ground or a tired team gives −2.
- **Fighting on the move.** Everyone else acts as usual. Shooting from the box, the roof or the saddle is at −2, on top of the range penalty. At Close, a rider can board with an Athletics roll, and then it is fists, knives and shotguns at arm's length. A passenger can climb out to the box or onto the roof as their move.
- **Hurting the coach.** A hit aimed at the team or a wheel (a called shot, −2) marks one box on the coach's track. Dynamite, a crash, or a driver who fails by 5 or more on rough ground marks one more.
- **The driver goes down.** If the driver is Incapacitated or knocked from the box, the team bolts. Someone must reach the box and take the reins (Riding −2) before the next exchange, or the coach marks a box.

| Boxes | The coach |
|---|---|
| 0 | Rolling: no damage |
| 1 | Holed: splinters and bullet holes; no penalty yet |
| 2 | A horse hit or a wheel cracked: −2 to the driver's Riding |
| 3 | Lamed: −4 to the driver's Riding, and riders close on a tie |
| 4 | Wrecked: the coach overturns or stops dead; everyone aboard rolls Athletics or marks a box of Injury |

<!-- SECTION:medicine -->

Frontier medicine has chloroform, carbolic acid, laudanum and steady hands, and not much else. Infection kills more men than bullets do, and no doctor's bag holds a cure for a bite from something that should be dead. See *Bites and curses* below.

<!-- SECTION:tracks -->

The characters share three Trouble tracks. **Heat** is the law and the powerful taking notice; **Whispers** is the town talking about what it saw; **The Dark** is the things in the night learning your names. They fill as your deeds catch up with you; the GM narrates what each box means. A killing in the street, a monster seen in the open, a ritual worked carelessly, or a success at a cost can mark a box. Lying low between jobs, a good alibi (Deception) or doing the town a public good can clear one per job.

# Lore cards

What your character knows about the dark, and how they come to know it: the difference between rumor and truth, how an investigation runs, and the lore cards that record what you've established.

## Folklore, truth and lore cards

What people say about the dark, what is actually true, and what your character knows are three different things.

- **Local folklore.** The stories, warnings and superstitions everyone has heard: hang garlic at the window, never answer a voice calling from the trees, salt the threshold. Some of it is right, some is half right, and some is wrong. It's where an investigation starts.
- **The truth.** What a creature really is and what really stops it. The GM knows it; you find it out.
- **Lore cards.** What your character has established for themselves: one finding at a time, with its limits and how you know it. A card holds only what you found out, never a creature's whole story (see *Discovery clocks and lore cards*).

A real weakness works whether or not anyone holds a card for it. Act on what you suspect: load silver, salt the doorway, try fire. The GM will ask what your idea rests on, and a good reason is all it needs.

# Monsters and magic

## The Sight

A few people are born **Sighted**. They see ghosts, the true faces of monsters wearing human ones, and magic at work. To everyone else the darkness is a rumor, until it isn't.

## Monstrous forms

Werewolves and vampires have a **monstrous form**: the wolf, or the feral face. Changing is a free action, but fury, terror or pain can force it (a Will roll), and a werewolf has no choice at moonrise under the full moon. While changed, the form's profile applies, and only then. Your sheet has a **Human form / Monstrous form** switch: it shows the numbers of the form you're in (the human ones beside them), and the dice roller and the GM's Encounter panel follow it. Horses bolt (−2 to Riding), and a monstrous form seen by townsfolk marks a box of **Whispers**.

## Magic

**Practitioners** work **rituals** in a tradition (see *Magic*): fill a clock with **Occult** rolls at your tradition's pace, usually ten minutes a roll, an hour for a Hermeticist, a minute for a Braucher's healing charm. Most western magic is slow, so practitioners prepare: a finished ritual stored as a **charm** is how a practitioner keeps up in a gunfight.

| Weight | Segments | For example |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | 2 | A charm against a hex, a candle that shows the hidden, a sleep that lasts the night |
| Significant | 4 | A curse that withers a crop, a ward on a house, a question asked of the dead |
| Major | 6 | Binding a demon, raising the dead, lifting a werewolf's curse for a month |

- **Trappings.** The right materials (grave dirt, silver, a lock of hair, the hour of midnight) remove one segment.
- **Charms.** Carry as many ready charms as your Occult training allows: Trained 2, Specialty 3, Expert 4. A Sorcerer carries only one.
- **Folk talismans.** Anyone, practitioner or not, can carry a charm made in the old folk ways through the **Talisman** Edge: a mojo hand from a hoodoo root doctor, a gris-gris from a Voodoo mambo in New Orleans, a Himmelsbrief (heaven's letter) from a Pennsylvania German Braucher, a blessed buckeye from an Appalachian granny woman. It holds a Minor ritual and doesn't count against a charm limit. Once broken, it's renewed only between jobs, and only by going back to the one who made it or another of the same tradition: a mojo hand from a root doctor, a Himmelsbrief from a Braucher. A job that keeps you far from them leaves it spent.
- **Attention.** Magic worked openly can mark a box of **The Dark**, and the Order of the Iron Cross hunts known practitioners.

**The old country's magic.** German settlers brought theirs west with the wagons. **Braucher**, the folk healers of the Pennsylvania settlements, the Ohio valley and the Texas Hill Country, heal with charms whispered from Hohman's *Long Lost Friend* and never take pay. Soldiers sew **heaven's letters** into their coats against bullets. The *Sixth and Seventh Books of Moses*, a German grimoire, passes from hand to hand, prized by Hermeticists and hoodoo root doctors alike. And where there is a Braucher, there is a **Hexenmeister** who will curse for money.

### Hexes

A **Sorcerer** trades preparation for speed: they know **hexes**, quick spells cast with **one action and an Occult roll**. Hexes known: **Trained 2, Specialty 3, Expert 4**. Occult as a Specialty gives **1 Boost**, as Expert **2**; each Boost spent is −1 to the roll. Each hex below says whether Dodge applies or which attribute resists, never both (see *Defense or resistance* in *Magic*). A failed hex marks a box of The Dark.

| Hex | Base effect |
|---|---|
| Hellfire | A lash of flame at Short range; marks 2 boxes. Dodge applies. |
| Devil's Shove | Unseen force hurls the target down: 1 box, and they lose their next move (Body resists). |
| Evil Eye | The target is at −2 on all rolls for the scene (Will resists). |
| Grave Chill | The target flees or freezes (Will resists). |
| Hold | The target is held where they stand for the scene, or until they break free (Body resists). |
| Misfire | One gun jams or one lamp gutters out; clearing a jam takes an action. No defense or resistance: it works on the gun or the lamp, not the person. |
| Shroud | +3 to Stealth for the scene; eyes slide off you. |
| Witch-sight | See spirits, curses, charms and things wearing human skin. |
| Stanch | Stop the bleeding: clear one injury box, once per set of injuries. |

## Weaknesses

Every monster has a weakness, and finding it is often the whole job: a discovery clock, a survivor's account, or a test that works (see *Discovery clocks and lore cards* in the Players' Guide). The Monster Hunter advantage and the Sighted's Monster's Eye help with the Identify rolls. **A real weakness works whoever uses it, card or no card:** silver marks its extra box fired by someone who has never heard of werewolves, and a hunter who suspects silver can load it on purpose. Called shots (at a heart, a head) follow the ordinary combat rules. What a weakness doesn't give anyone is an unfamiliar ritual: a banishment, a binding or a cure still needs its procedure, the knowledge, the skill and the components.

| Creature | Weakness |
|---|---|
| Werewolf | Silver: an extra box, and its toughness doesn't help. Wolfsbane keeps it away. |
| Vampire | A wooden stake through the heart incapacitates but doesn't kill; fire, sunlight and beheading destroy. Holy symbols hold it back; it can't enter a home uninvited. Destroying a natural vampire frees its whole lineage (see *Vampires*). |
| Zombie | A shot to the head (−2 to hit) or fire puts it down for good; anything else just slows it. |
| Ghost | Can't be hurt. Lay it to rest by finishing its business, or bind it with a ritual. |
| Wraith | Only silver, cold iron, salt and fire hurt it. It can't cross a line of salt or iron filings. Destroyed for good only when its bones are found and burned. |
| Banshee | Weapons don't touch her. She leaves at dawn if the one she keens for is still alive. A hit with silver, or an iron bell rung at her, silences her for the scene. |
| Ghoul | Fire and daylight. It flees from holy ground. |
| Demon | Holy water burns it. A true demon is not fought like a beast: a major ritual or a powerful holy talisman banishes it (see *Demons*). It must keep a bargain to the letter. |
| Hellhound | Holy water drives it off for a time. Its hunt ends only when the claim on its quarry's soul is resolved or changed, or a major ritual interferes with the pact (see *Hellhounds*). |
| Wendigo | Ordinary weapons wound it, but it heals unnaturally, and only fire stops the healing (only fire marks more than 1 box at a time). It is destroyed for good only when it is beheaded and its body burned. It is strongest in deep winter and weakest in summer heat. |
| Skinwalker | Underneath, it is a person. In a stolen shape, ordinary wounds mark only 1 box at a time; in its own shape, it is as mortal as anyone. Speak its true name to its face and it is forced back into its own shape for the scene. |
| Headless Rider | Its head, hidden near where it died: laid back in the grave, the rider rests for good. It can't cross running water, so a bridge over a creek is safety. |

## Bites and curses

- **Zombie bite.** A bite that marks a box starts a 4-segment **Infection** clock, ticking each hour. A successful Medicine roll with an Occult remedy stops it; when it fills, you die, and rise.
- **Werewolf bite.** A bite under the full moon can pass on the curse; the GM decides, and lifting it is a major ritual.
- **Vampire bite.** A vampire who drains a victim to Dying can choose to turn them. The turned belong to its lineage (see *Vampires*).
- **The wendigo's hunger.** It begins with someone who ate human flesh to survive a starving winter in the high passes. A character who does starts a 4-segment **Hunger** clock, ticking each week they go without it again; when it fills, they belong to the wendigo. Only a major ritual lifts it.
- **A wraith's touch.** Someone killed by it rises as a wraith at the next new moon, unless they're buried with salt in the coffin.
- **A banshee's keening.** She is tied to a family's blood, not a place: once she has keened for one of a line, she can follow that family from town to town.

## Vampires

- **Lineages.** Each **natural vampire** sustains its own lineage: everyone it has turned, and everyone they have turned in turn. There is more than one natural vampire.
- **Freeing a lineage.** Destroy the natural source, and the whole chain reverts to human, including those infected only indirectly. Destroying a vampire it turned (an intermediate) cures nobody.
- **A stake isn't the end.** A stake through the heart incapacitates a vampire; it doesn't destroy it. Staking the source doesn't free its lineage; destroying it does.
- **What reversion does to the body.** Wounds that vampiric regeneration already healed stay healed. Wounds that hadn't healed remain, and have their ordinary human consequences at once: someone reverting with three boxes marked is Dying. Reversion itself repairs nothing.
- This cure is a vampire's alone: no other creature's victims are freed by killing their maker.

## The wendigo

- Ordinary weapons wound it; only fire marks more than 1 box at a time.
- It heals unnaturally fast, and only fire interferes with that healing.
- It is destroyed for good only when it is **beheaded and its body burned**. After beheading, the body can still lash out, but blindly: its actions have no direction.
- How fast it heals, how long fire holds the healing back, and how the headless body's blind attacks are run are not yet set; until they are, the GM rules.

## Hellhounds

- A hellhound's hunt is a **curse tied to a claim on someone's soul**.
- The hunt ends only when the conditions behind the claim are **resolved or changed**. A powerful practitioner can interfere with the pact through a **major ritual**.
- **Holy water** drives a hellhound off and protects its quarry for a time; it doesn't cancel the pact.
- Whether destroying a hellhound's physical body buys the quarry time, and how much, is not settled: the GM rules case by case.

## The shadow realm

The **shadow realm** is a real place in the same world as ours, not another Earth: it has no copies of our towns or our people. It is an independent and brutal ecology, with its own hunters and prey. Some of its creatures cross over, and some of those spread a **contagion** (a bite, a curse) that makes more of their kind; others never do. Each threat has its own weaknesses, found out on their own terms: not every horror is an infection.

Some hold that the things of the dark are **fallen angels** and their brood. Others call that a preacher's story. No one has settled it, and the game doesn't.

## Demons

Three kinds of thing get called a demon, and they aren't the same:

- **Demonic creatures** native to the shadow realm: beasts and hunters of that ecology.
- **Creatures under demonic influence**: something, or someone, bent by a demon's power.
- **True demons**, native to the deeper **Abyss**: a realm of no light and of eldritch powers, not a fiery hell. A true demon isn't a creature of fire.

A **true demon can't enter our world unaided**: it must be **summoned**, or come through a **possession**. A person can be possessed only if they are corrupted and have no faith of any kind at all. True demons are exceptionally powerful, and a generic fight is not how they are met: the encounter is a **ritual or a negotiation**, and the countermeasures are major rituals and powerful holy talismans.

- **Banishment by a talisman.** A sufficiently powerful holy talisman can banish a demon by breaking its binding to our world. What happens to the host depends on the talisman and how it acts: a holy sword that fatally wounds the host banishes the demon and kills the host.
- **Banishment by ritual.** A properly completed banishment ritual preserves the host, if their body can survive without the demon holding it up.
- **A possessed body.** A demon can keep a body moving through wounds that would otherwise be fatal, but possession heals nothing and grows nothing back. Ordinary injury to the body doesn't kill the demon. This is not a vampire's regeneration: when the demon goes, the body's wounds are all still there.
- What counts as faith, how a possession is resolved at the table, and whether a possessed host's wounds can be stabilized before the banishment are not yet set; until they are, the GM rules.

## Rituals and remedies

A remedy or a ritual against the dark always has a procedure: what it requires, how long it takes, which rolls it calls for, its components, what happens if it's interrupted, and what failure costs. Zombie treatment, lifting a curse, binding a ghost, banishing a demon and interfering with a hellhound's pact all work this way. Use the *Magic* rules (a ritual clock in Occult rolls) and the weights above for any the GM hasn't spelled out.

# Threats

Opponents use the same rules as the characters. **Minor opponents** (hired guns, a mob, shambling dead) have **one box**: any hit that gets through takes them out of the fight.

**Adjusting difficulty.** For a tougher version, add +1 to its targets and one box; for a weaker one, subtract.

<!-- SECTION:advantages -->

**Badges.** A badge is legal authority, not a magic word: outlaws and the powerful don't always care about it, and it carries a duty. Your superiors can give you orders, and abusing the badge or failing in your duty can cost you it. Law roles (Marshal, Deputy) usually take one, as their advantage or as a second advantage Edge. A Pinkerton detective takes Pinkerton Credentials instead: the agency's reach and files, but no badge.
