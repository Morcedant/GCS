<!-- SECTION:intro -->

A thousand years ago the **Crown of Aeon** bound a sorcerer-empire together. When the last emperor died, the Crown shattered into nine shards, and the empire with it. Each shard gives power to whoever holds it, and each slowly devours them. Now warlords, sorcerer-princes and merchant-kings fight over the empire's corpse, and the old things beneath its cities are waking.

*The Shattered Crown* is a sword-and-sorcery setting for the Legend Dynamics RPG system. You are not heroes. You are reavers, thieves, sellswords and sorcerers who live on steel, wits and luck in a world that has no heroes left.

The years are counted **After the Shattering (AS)**. The story begins in the year 712 AS, but the GM can set it anywhere in the Crown's long ruin.

**Gunpowder** is common but slow. Anyone can carry a pistol or an arquebus, but reloading takes a **full round** (a crossbow takes one action), and rain or seawater ruins the powder until it's dried. An arquebus misfires on a natural 17 or 18; any missile weapon fails on a natural 18 (a string snaps, a bolt jams, the powder fizzles).

*Example:* a guardsman's club leaves Kara Wounded (one box). A sellsword's sword that gets through marks two more: Kara is at three boxes, Incapacitated and Dying, and a further 2-box hit would kill. Spending a Fortune point on that sword stroke reduces it to one box, leaving her Seriously wounded and still swinging.

To take someone down without killing them, use fists, a club, a grapple, or the Sleep cantrip.

<!-- SECTION:medicine -->

Healers have herbs, needles, wine and prayers. Poison and fever kill more adventurers than swords do. The **Mend** cantrip clears one box once per set of injuries; it does not replace a healer's care.

<!-- SECTION:tracks -->

The party shares four **Troubles**. **Infamy** is the rulers and the watch taking notice; **Doom** is the old powers learning your names; **Debt** is what you owe moneylenders, patrons and sorcerers; **Hunger** is supplies, coin and morale running thin on the road. They fill as your deeds catch up with you; the GM narrates what each box means.

- **Marking:** killing in the open or a beast form seen in town (Infamy); blatant sorcery or taking a cursed treasure (Doom); borrowing or buying on credit (Debt); each day in the wilds without supplies (Hunger). A success at a cost can mark any of them.
- **Clearing:** lying low between ventures (Infamy); a cleansing rite (Doom); paying up (Debt); reaching a town, or a Survival roll each day in the wilds (Hunger).

Each character also keeps a personal **Corruption** track on their sheet (see *Sorcery*).

# Tales

What your heroes know of the old powers, and how they come to know it: the difference between legend and truth, how an investigation runs, and the tales that hold what you've established.

## Legend, truth and tales

What the legends say, what is actually true, and what your hero knows are three different things.

- **Legend.** What the skalds sing, what the temples warn, what an old woman mutters at the well. Some of it is right, some half right, some wrong. It's where an investigation starts.
- **The truth.** What a sorcerer, a demon or a thing from the Deep really is, and what really stops it. The GM knows it; you find it out.
- **Tales.** What your hero has established for themselves: one finding at a time, with its limits, how you know it and what it cost. A tale holds only what you found out, never the whole saga (see *Discovery clocks and tales*).

A real weakness works whether or not anyone holds a tale for it. Act on what you suspect: bring fire, salt the threshold, speak the name. The GM will ask what your idea rests on, and a good reason is all it needs. Learning, though, is never free.

# Sorcery

Sorcery is real, rare and expensive. It is worked by **Sorcerers** and **Witches**, by anyone with the **Gifted** advantage, and by those whose blood grants it (the Witch-blooded's *The Old Blood*, the Serpent-kin's *Temple-Taught*, the Demon-marked's *Infernal Gift*, the Godblooded's *Divine Spark* and the Undying's *Deathless Sorcery*). Everyone else can resist it, fear it and pay for it.

## Cantrips

A caster knows a few **cantrips**, the quick spells of *Magic*: as many as their **Sorcery** training allows. Every cantrip holds three **Seals**, each hiding a deeper power, and Sorcery breaks them.

| Sorcery | Cantrips known | Seal | Boosts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trained | 2 | the First Seal | none |
| Specialty | 3 | the Second Seal | 1 |
| Expert | 4 | the Third Seal | 2 |

Cast one with **an action and a Sorcery roll**, −1 for each Boost you spend; each cantrip below says whether **Dodge applies** or which attribute **resists**, never both (see *Defense or resistance* in *Magic*). Choose your cantrips in the creator.

| Cantrip | Base effect |
|---|---|
| Shadow Bolt | A ranged attack with Sorcery; marks 2 boxes. Dodge applies. |
| Witchfire | Light, or set something burning: 1 box a round in the flames. Aimed at a person, it's an attack: Dodge applies; once alight, the flames are ordinary fire. |
| Ward | Attacks against one ally are at −2 for the scene. |
| Hex | The target is at −2 on all rolls for the scene (Will resists). |
| Charm | The target is Charmed: it treats you as a friend for the scene (Will resists). |
| Dread | The target is Frightened: it flees or freezes (Will resists). |
| Sleep | The target sleeps (Will resists); minor opponents just drop, without resisting. |
| Mend | Clear one injury box, once per set of injuries. |
| Beast-tongue | Speak with an animal, and command it for a scene (a hostile animal's Will resists). |
| Sorcerer's Sight | See magic, curses, spirits and disguised monsters. |
| Shatter | Break a lock, chain, blade or door. No defense against an unattended object; against a blade or shield in someone's hand, it's an attack: Dodge applies. |
| Veil | +3 to Stealth for the scene; you're hard to notice. |

**Changing cantrips.** Cantrips are learned, not prayed for each morning, so a caster keeps the ones they know:

- **New cantrips.** When **Sorcery** advances (Untrained to Trained, or Trained to Specialty), choose the new cantrips at once, and every cantrip you know breaks its next Seal.
- **Swapping.** Between ventures, a caster can swap **one** cantrip for another with **a week of study** and a source to learn it from: a teacher, a grimoire, a captured sorcerer's notes or a Crown shard's memory. The GM may make finding the source part of the next venture.
- **Never mid-venture.** No swapping during a venture, unless the GM offers it as the reward for something remarkable (a demon's bargain, a dying sorcerer's last lesson).

## Rituals and talismans

Bigger workings are **rituals** (see *Magic*), worked with **Sorcery**: ten minutes a roll, an hour for great workings. **Priests** perform *rites* the same way, using **Lore**.

| Weight | Segments | For example |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | 2 | A charm against poison, a candle that shows the hidden, a night's protection |
| Significant | 4 | A curse on a household, a ward on a tower, a question asked of the dead |
| Major | 6 | Binding a demon, raising the dead, cursing a bloodline |

- **Trappings.** The right materials (a true name, the blood of a king, a Crown shard's light, the hour of an eclipse) remove one segment.
- **Rites.** A cleansing rite (Significant) clears a box of Corruption or Doom.
- **Talismans.** A finished ritual can be bound into a bone, a ring or a knotted cord and released by breaking it: Trained 2, Specialty 3, Expert 4 ready at once.

### Enchantments

An **enchantment** is a ritual worked into an object: a blade that bites deeper, a cloak that hides its wearer, a ring that turns a blow. Where a talisman is spent when it breaks, an enchantment **lasts**, and anyone can carry the item. Work it like any ritual, with the item in your hands: describe the empowerment, the GM sets its weight, and you fill the clock with Sorcery rolls.

| Weight | What the item gains | For example |
|---|---|---|
| Minor (2) | +1 to one use of the item, or one small wonder | A sword +1 to hit; boots +1 to Athletics; a lantern that shows the hidden |
| Significant (4) | +2 to one use, or its hits mark one more box, or it turns one hit a fight (a hit of 2 or more boxes marks one fewer) | A war-axe that bites deep; a cloak +2 to Stealth; a coat that turns a blade |
| Major (6) | A cantrip bound into it, at its First Seal, cast once a scene by whoever holds it (with their Sorcery, or at 10 without it), or one great property | A ring of Ward; a staff of Witchfire; a blade that wounds what nothing else can |

| How long it lasts | Clock |
|---|---|
| A scene | As its weight |
| A day | +1 segment |
| A moon | +2 segments |
| Until broken | Major only: it needs a rare trapping (a true name, a Crown shard's light, the blood of a king) and marks **another box of Corruption** |

- **One enchantment to an item.** A second working on the same item fails, or breaks the first (the GM's choice).
- **Your limit.** Your ready talismans and your enchantments still holding together can't exceed your talisman limit: **Trained 2, Specialty 3, Expert 4**. When an enchantment ends, its place is free again.
- **The price.** Finishing a Major enchantment marks a box of Corruption, as any Major ritual does.
- **Seeing and breaking.** Sorcerer's Sight shows an enchantment and who made it. Shatter at its Third Seal breaks one, and so does a rite of unbinding (Significant).
- **Born with one.** Two Edges bring enchantment to any character, sorcerer or not: **Talisman** (a ready talisman, renewed every venture) and **Enchanted heirloom** (an item with a lasting Significant enchantment).

## Corruption

Power has a price. Every character keeps a **Corruption** track of four boxes on their sheet:

| Boxes | Effect |
|---|---|
| 0 | Untouched |
| 1 | **Marked:** a visible sign of sorcery (black veins, cold eyes, a shadow that lags behind) |
| 2 | **Tainted:** −2 to Persuasion with the pious |
| 3 | **Twisted:** the GM can trigger a madness or compulsion, as with a complication |
| 4 | **Lost:** the power claims you; only a great rite can bring you back |

A **failed cast** marks 1 box; a **natural 18** marks 2; finishing a **Major ritual** marks 1, whatever the roll. Holding a **Crown shard** marks 1 each venture. A **cleansing rite** or a **pilgrimage** between ventures clears one box.

# The world

The empire is gone, but its bones are everywhere: roads no one repairs, aqueducts that feed dead cities, sorcerer-towers that still glow at night. Its heirs quarrel over the pieces.

- **The Gilded Cities.** The empire's heart, now a coast of rival city-states: silk, lotus, slave markets and assassins. Ruled by **merchant princes** and the occasional **sorcerer-prince**.
- **The Frozen North.** Clans, longships and giants' graves. Northlanders raid south every summer and trade every winter.
- **The Burning Sands.** Nomads, caravan cities and buried temples of the **serpent kings** who ruled before humans.
- **The Shattered Isles.** Corsair havens, sea-witches and sunken ruins; the Islanders' free captains answer to no one.
- **The Deeps.** Deepkin cities beneath the mountains, and below them, older things that dream.
- **The Ashlands.** The empire's blasted center, where the Crown broke. Nothing grows; shards and madmen are found there.

## Powers

- **The Crown shards.** Nine in all; four are held by known rulers, three are lost, and two are rumors. Each grants one great power and whispers to its bearer.
- **The sorcerer-princes.** Rulers who bought their thrones with sorcery and keep them with fear.
- **The merchant princes.** Gold rules the Gilded Cities more than any king.
- **The Cult of the Burning God.** Zealots who burn sorcerers, and sometimes their neighbors.
- **The Iron Company and its rivals.** Mercenary companies who decide most wars, for a price.
- **The Thieves' Guild.** Every city has one; they don't all get along.

## The first venture: The Thirsting Well

A starter venture for three to seven, for one or two sessions. A tomb in the Burning Sands, a merchant princess's gold, a dying god's last well, and something old waking under it. It shows off the desert road and Hunger, the Doom track and a tale, sorcery and its corruption, and a hard choice at the end. It's written for the seven pregenerated characters, and works with any.

**The hook.** In the caravan city of Qasreth, the merchant princess **Ysolde Varr** is hiring: there's a serpent-king tomb under the oasis of Ain Haddu, three days into the sands, and she'll pay 200 gold crowns a head to anyone who brings her its treasure. Everyone in Qasreth knows the oasis is drying up. Give each of them a reason of their own:

- **Oren Sael**: Ain Haddu is the last well of Haddu, his god. The water has been going down for a month, and the flame at his shrine with it.
- **Ithrann**: the tomb is Ssa'kethi. The drowned temple's lost library could be inside.
- **Vessa Thorn**: the tomb's upper door is sealed sixty feet up a sheer wall, and she's the only one who can reach it.
- **Gorrum Ironjaw**: Ysolde hired the Iron Company to guard the dig, and Gorrum's squad drew the job.
- **Mira Saltwind**: Ysolde holds her crew's debts. Bring back the gold, and they're paid.
- **Nyx**: the oasis tribes are sick, and a hedge-witch can sell cures to people who can't go anywhere else.
- **Cinder**: the Ash Guild has sent someone after the same tomb. Cinder knows the style.

**What's really happening.** Under Ain Haddu sleeps **Ssethra**, a serpent-priest of the old kings, bound there when the serpent kings fell. A month ago, grave robbers broke the outer seal, and Ssethra began to wake. It's drinking the oasis dry from below, and every day it drinks, it grows stronger. When the well runs dry, Ssethra wakes fully, the tomb-dead rise with it, and Haddu dies. Ysolde Varr knows the tomb is guarded by something; she hasn't said so.

**1. Qasreth.** Ysolde receives them on her barge, under silk awnings. She pays half in advance. A Read People roll sees she's afraid of the tomb, not of the competition. Her captain, a **Sellsword captain** named Dask, is coming too, and Dask has his own orders.

**2. The road.** Three days across the Burning Sands. Each day, someone rolls Survival; on a failure, Hunger marks a box (short rations, a bad well, a sandstorm). On the second night, a **Cultist** band of the Burning God tries to take Ithrann and Nyx: sorcerers, they say, and the tomb should be burned with them inside. This fight can be short; it's a chance to learn the rules.

**3. Ain Haddu.** A few palms, a shrinking pool, a tribe of herders who won't go near the water after dark, and Oren's god's shrine, its flame almost out. The herders say the water hisses at night. Their dead have been missing from the graves.

**4. Into the tomb.** The upper door, for whoever can climb to it; or the lower one, broken open by the grave robbers, whose bodies are just inside. Start a **tale** about the thing in the tomb (*Tales*, in the Players' Guide), with **Doom** ticking as they dig: it learns their names. The clues, and who finds them best:

- **The carvings** (Lore, or Ithrann reading Ssa'kethi): the priest was bound by its true name, carved over the sealed chamber and chiseled off by the grave robbers.
- **The grave robbers' bodies** (Healing, Investigation): bitten, poisoned, and dry as old leather. Not a drop of blood in them.
- **The water** (Survival, or Oren's faith): the pool drains through a crack into the tomb. Haddu's water still carries the god, and the thing drinking it flinches from the god in it.
- **The library** (Lore): the Ssa'kethi scrolls, intact in a dry room. Among them, the priest's name, in a list of the bound.

When the tale's Identify phase is done, they know what it is: a **serpent-priest**. Filling the clock earns the tale that says how it's beaten: speak its true name, or bind it with Haddu's water.

**5. The hall of kings.** The tomb-dead rise from their niches, two of them and one more for each box of Doom: **Ghouls or tomb-dead**. Fire and daylight drive them back. In the middle of the fight, Dask makes his move: his orders are to take the gold and leave the hirelings inside.

**6. Ssethra.** In the drowned chamber, half-awake in the water it stole, the serpent-priest commands the snakes in the walls and speaks with the voice of the well. Sorcery against it pulls at the caster: every spell cast in its chamber is at risk of corruption. Its true name, spoken by someone who read it, makes it roll Will or be bound for a round; Haddu's water burns it like fire.

**7. The choice.** The tomb is coming down as the bound thing thrashes. They can carry one thing out: Ysolde's gold (the debts paid), the Ssa'kethi library (Ithrann's life's work), or the god's water, carried back to the pool before it's gone (Haddu lives, and Oren's flame burns again).

**Rewards.** 200 gold crowns a head, if they bring the gold, and Debt clears a box. A tale on serpent-priests. If Haddu lives, Oren's god owes them a blessing, and the herders' tribe their lives. If they left Dask alive, Infamy marks a box: he'll talk. If Ssethra was only bound, not destroyed, Doom keeps one box: something remembers their names.

**Threats.** Cultist ×4 (one blessed); Sellsword captain (Dask) and two Guardsmen or bandits; Ghoul or tomb-dead ×2 or more; Serpent-priest (Ssethra).

# Threats

Opponents use the same rules as the characters. **Minor opponents** (guards, bandits, cultists) have **one box**: any hit that gets through takes them out of the fight.

| Threat | Attack (target) | Dodge | Boxes | Weapon | Trait |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guardsman or bandit (minor) | Melee 11 | 7 | 1 | Spear or club | **Numbers.** Three or four of them; they flee when half are down. |
| Cultist (minor, many) | Melee 10 | 6 | 1 | Dagger | **Fanatics.** One in each group carries a blessing: Ward on the others. |
| Sellsword captain | Melee 14, Leadership 12 | 10 | 3 | Sword, pistol | **Command.** Their band gets +1 while the captain stands. |
| Assassin | Melee 14, Stealth 15 | 11 | 3 | Poisoned dagger | **First strike.** Attacks from hiding at +2; poison: Body roll or Reeling. |
| Sorcerer | Sorcery 14 | 9 | 3 | Shadow Bolt, Hex | **Warded.** Attacks against them are at −2 until the Ward is broken (Shatter or a hit by 5+). |
| Serpent-priest | Sorcery 13, Persuasion 14 | 10 | 3 | Venom, Charm | **Serpent-lord.** Commands snakes; its bite poisons. |
| Ghoul or tomb-dead | Brawling 12 | 7 | 2 | Claws | **Hunger.** Fire and daylight drive them back; they never stop coming. |
| Giant ape or great beast | Brawling 15 | 9 | 5 | Claws and jaws (2) | **Huge.** Hits of 1 box mark nothing. |
| Demon | Melee 15, Sorcery 14 | 11 | 4 | Claws (2), Witchfire | **Bound.** Holy rites and its true name mark an extra box; it must keep a bargain to the letter. |
| Shard-bearer | Melee 15 or Sorcery 15 | 11 | 5 | As the bearer | **Crown shard.** One great power: command, fire, flight, terror. Breaking the bearer's will ends it. |
| Thing from the Deep | Brawling 16 | 8 | 6 | Tentacles (2) | **Horror.** Once per scene, everyone who sees it rolls Will or is Frightened. |

**Adjusting difficulty.** For a tougher version, add +1 to its targets and one box; for a weaker one, subtract.

**Gifted and the old bloods.** *Gifted* (or an ancestry's equivalent) lets a character of any role learn cantrips and work rituals. Sorcerers and Witches don't need it. Taking it means training **Sorcery** too, or the gift has nothing to work with.
