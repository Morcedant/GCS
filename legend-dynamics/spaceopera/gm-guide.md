This book is for whoever runs the game. It gathers what the GM reaches for at the table: job seeds, the threats and enemy ships with their numbers, and how to use the GM view's battle map and Encounter panel. The rules themselves are in the **Players' Guide**; this book builds on them.

Opponents use the same rules as the characters. **Minor opponents** have **one box**: any hit that gets through takes them out of the fight.

# Data records

Running investigations and recording what the crew learns. The rules players use are in the *Data records* chapter of the Players' Guide; here is the GM view's Discovery panel, for clocks and records.

## Making data records

For the GM: when a data record is earned, and how to write one. A data record is a record of what the characters established, so writing it well matters more than how often you hand one out.

1. **Decide that something is established.** A data record is earned when a discovery clock fills, or when the characters see or test something themselves, hear it from a source you judge reliable, or are told it by the subject itself with evidence to back it up. A guess, however right, isn't established, and neither is a rumor. Ask what supports their idea; when the answer is good, let them act on it, and give the data record once they've confirmed it.
2. **Write the finding narrowly.** One fact, in the words the characters would use, and no more than they found out: "bright light drove it back", not "it fears light". Two separate facts make two data records. Never write the subject's whole truth onto a data record; later discoveries add more data records.
3. **Write its limits.** What the finding doesn't show: how often it was seen, under what conditions, and what is still unknown. The limits are what keep the next investigation interesting.
4. **Note the evidence or source.** What they saw, tested, read or were told, and by whom. A source's reputation doesn't make it right: if it was only probably true, the limits say so.
5. **Charge the price.** A data record from a discovery clock costs nothing more: the scenes, failed rolls and pushes were its price. Any other source has its price (see *Earning a data record*): choose the one that fits how they learned it, and mark it.
6. **Choose the owner and the recipients.** Whoever did the finding owns the data record. Everyone who was there, or heard it from a reliable source, gets a shared copy.
7. **Record it.** In the GM view's **Discovery** panel: **Mint** a full clock's data record, or use **Record a finding without a clock**, which marks the price for you. Then save everyone's characters to file, so the players' sheets carry it. A player can also write a data record on their own sheet, when you agree it's been earned.

*Example:* **The Kessel Deep derelict.** Established: *Its reactor powers down whenever the corridor beacon goes silent*. Limits: *Seen twice, from outside the hull; not what restarts it*. Source: The crew's own sensor logs (scanned or tested firsthand). Cost: Countdown +1 (the hours spent watching it).

- **When a later finding disagrees.** Write a new data record that says what they now know; the old one stays on the sheet as what they believed then, and the new one's limits can say so.
- **A data record is never proof of identity.** Holding a data record about one subject doesn't prove that the thing in front of them is that subject: the Identify phase does.
- **Weaknesses work anyway.** Don't hold back a real weakness because no one holds the data record: if they try it, it works.
- **Classified Archive.** Agree each starting data record with its player before play: it must come from the character's role or species, be narrow, and be a real secret: something that isn't in any database or published record. Make sure it fits what you know to be true, adjust it if it doesn't, and keep a note of it.

## Example data records

Seven subjects the crew might investigate, each with what goes wrong on a failed roll or a success at a cost in each phase, what its record permits, and the skill that lets a character skip Confront. A failure is where the GM may start a Countdown, when the stakes are real.

### Halvane lattice core

A crystalline power system found on derelict Halvane haulers, the work of a people lost behind a collapsed corridor. Ordinary engineering can't read it.

- **Confront the unknown:** get the core to respond. *On a failure:* it pulses and drains nearby power cells; suits and tools lose charge.
- **Identify:** learn it feeds on the crew's own power signatures. *On a failure:* resonance builds; the GM may start a Countdown to critical.
- **Uncover:** find the harmonic frequency that controls it. *On a failure:* a feedback surge injures whoever is linked to it.
- **Permits:** waking, powering down or rerouting any Halvane lattice core. **Skill:** Engineering.

### The Warden of the Ashkarr

One damaged military intelligence, the Warden, still running aboard the derelict Hegemony warship *Ashkarr*. An AI Minder's training covers AIs in general; this record covers only the Warden's own command authority.

- **Confront the unknown:** get it to acknowledge the crew. *On a failure:* it activates the ship's defenses; the GM may start a Countdown to full lockdown.
- **Identify:** learn it is still executing a wartime directive and takes the crew for enemy boarders. *On a failure:* it vents a section or seals bulkheads, splitting the party.
- **Uncover:** find its original command authority code (the captain's quarters, the black-box logs, a dead officer's implant). *On a failure:* it detects the tampering and purges the trail; another source must be found.
- **Permits:** issuing orders the Warden obeys, or shutting it down cleanly without tripping its failsafes. **Skill:** Computers.

### A corridor engine

An ancient machine, inert until approached. Corridors are natural folds, and no one builds them; but where a fold already runs close, this machine forces open a mouth that no chart shows. Who made it, and why, is a question for another investigation.

- **Confront the unknown:** detect that it's active, not wreckage. *On a failure:* a spatial pulse throws crew or objects a short distance.
- **Identify:** learn it is still keyed to a far mouth, and something on the other side is listening. *On a failure:* the mouth half-opens; something comes through, or a signal goes out.
- **Uncover:** decode its address glyphs and its sealing sequence. *On a failure:* it locks onto a random mouth; the GM may start a Countdown to an unstable opening.
- **Permits:** opening its mouth to a known destination along the fold, or sealing it for good. **Skill:** Science.

### Spatial fracture

An unstable region where space folds on itself; the sensors return contradictory readings.

- **Confront the unknown:** map the fracture's edge. *On a failure:* the ship clips it; mark Hull, or a system goes offline.
- **Identify:** learn it shifts in a cycle tied to an outside rhythm (a pulsar, a nearby drive's signature). *On a failure:* time slips; the crew loses hours.
- **Uncover:** plot the safe corridor that opens on each cycle. *On a failure:* the ship is caught mid-fold; the GM starts a Countdown to hull failure.
- **Permits:** navigating this fracture, and fractures of its kind, safely. **Skill:** Navigation.

### A living hull

A living ship that treats intruders as an infection.

- **Confront the unknown:** realize the ship is alive and aware. *On a failure:* tissue walls close passages or secrete irritants.
- **Identify:** learn its immune response escalates with each disturbance, and in the end digests foreign matter. *On a failure:* antibody organisms attack; the GM may start a Countdown to its full immune response.
- **Uncover:** find the chemical or bio-signature it accepts as self (from its dead crew, its nursery, its pilot cradle). *On a failure:* the wrong signature marks the crew as its first target.
- **Permits:** moving through the ship unopposed, and coaxing its doors, systems or will to cooperate. **Skill:** Science or Medicine.

### Corporate black-site encryption

A proprietary security layer guarding a corporate facility or data vault.

- **Confront the unknown:** recognize it as an adaptive, corporation-specific system, not standard encryption. *On a failure:* the attempt is logged; mark Heat.
- **Identify:** learn it has a dead-man response: tampering calls corporate security or wipes the vault. *On a failure:* the GM may start a Countdown to the security response or the wipe.
- **Uncover:** obtain a valid credential pattern (a defector, a stolen badge, a maintenance back door). *On a failure:* the credential is flagged, and its source is burned.
- **Permits:** bypassing this corporation's black-site security, or forging credentials it accepts. **Skill:** Computers.

### An alien weapon cache

A stockpile of weapons built by a hostile species, perhaps still being used against the crew.

- **Confront the unknown:** survive or witness the weapons in use. *On a failure:* one discharges, or is turned on the crew.
- **Identify:** learn they're keyed to their makers' biology, or tuned to a frequency. *On a failure:* a weapon self-destructs or alerts its owners.
- **Uncover:** find how they fail (a frequency that overloads them, a biometric spoof). *On a failure:* a booby trap goes off; if the cache is rigged to blow, the GM may start a Countdown.
- **Permits:** disabling these weapons in a fight (a telling hit's break-gear effect works on them even in the makers' hands), or turning them against their makers. **Skill:** Engineering or Firearms.

## Rival clocks

A careful crew can investigate without ever starting a Countdown. To keep the pressure on, give the discovery a **rival**: an ordinary danger clock named for someone racing the crew to the same find. It ticks on delays, noisy methods and open digging. When it fills, the rival gets there first. No new rules are needed.

| Rival | What they want |
|---|---|
| A Draelin cartographic guild | Corridor data, which they sell to both sides |
| The Veyari archives | The discovery preserved, perhaps out of the crew's reach |
| A corporation | Salvage and technology it can own and sell |
| A Hegemony house | Imperial ruins and technology returned |
| A syndicate | Anything it can fence |

## The Discovery panel

The GM view's **Discovery** panel runs discovery clocks and the Countdown they race (it stays idle until you **Start the Countdown** after a failed roll or a success at a cost). Start a clock with its segments per phase (or a plain progress clock for a repair or a task in the same race), and fill it as the investigation rolls succeed. It stops at the Identify gate until you name the subject; then it shows every data record the characters in the GM view already hold for it. The **Failed roll** and **Push** buttons tick the track for how they were digging. When a clock fills, **Mint** gives the primary investigator the data record and everyone you tick as present a shared copy. **Record a finding without a clock** gives a data record for something established directly (seen or tested, a reliable account, a creature's word with evidence behind it) to whoever you tick, and marks its price: the track or the Countdown that source costs. Save all to file afterwards, so the players' character files carry it. The Discord bot's `/discovery` and `/cards` commands do the same at a table that plays online.

# The GM's tools

## The battle map

The GM view of the character creator has a **battle map** for fights where it helps to see who is where. It is a tool, not a rule: everything on it follows the *Personal combat* rules in the Players' Guide, and a fight without it works the same way. Open **How to use the map** on the map itself for the buttons, step by step.

**Two kinds of map.**

- **A hex map** for fights where position matters: cover, doorways, who can reach whom. The GM chooses what one hex is: 1 yard for a brawl in a room, 5 or 10 yards for a street, 50 for open ground. Dragging a token or measuring shows the distance and its **range band**, so the GM can read the penalty from the Range table in the Players' Guide.
- **A range track** for fights where only distance matters: a chase, a running fight across open country. It has a lane for the crew and one for each range band. Moving a token into the next lane moves it one band in or out.

**Tokens.** The GM places the crew with one button. Their tokens stay linked to the characters in the GM view: marking Injury, Dying, the stunned condition or a Resolve point on a token marks it on the character, and the other way round. Opponents come from the setting's threats with their boxes filled in. Their pips fill as they are hurt, and a token whose boxes are all marked is taken out.

**Keeping a map.** The map stays in the GM's browser. **Save map to file** keeps both maps, their pictures and tokens in one file, to prepare a fight ahead of time or share it with another GM.

**Ship fight.** In this setting the range track is for ship fights, with the bands Close (under 10 km), Short (up to 1,000 km), Medium (up to 10,000 km), Long (up to 100,000 km), Extreme (about a light-second). **Add our ship** places a token linked to the crew's Hull track, so damage marked on the map is marked on the sheet. Enemy ships come from the Enemy ships table with their Hull boxes. Each lane is a range band from our ship. When the pilots make their opposed Piloting roll, drag the enemy one lane in or out. Weapons take −2 one band beyond their range and −4 two bands beyond.

## The Encounter panel

The GM view also has an **Encounter** panel that runs a fight by the *Personal combat* rules and shows every roll. Add characters from the GM view and opponents from the Threats table (or make one up on the spot), then start: it keeps the turn order by Agility, and for each attack works out the range, the defense (Dodge, Parry or Block, one a round, and a second at −2 with Additional Defense), the boxes marked and any telling hit, offering a Resolve point choices at the moment they apply. Hits land on the character cards and the battle map, and with gritty combat on, grievous wounds are rolled and kept. The panel keeps a log and a summary of the fight, which can be saved to a file. It's a helper, not a rule: the GM can rule any result differently, and the log says so.

**The shared table.** The GM can start a shared table from the GM view and give the players its code; each player joins from their character's Play tab. While they're connected, every roll from a sheet is rolled by the table server, so the whole table sees the same dice as they land, in a live log on the Play tab and on the GM view, along with who rolled, against what, and any telling hit. A point reroll answers its own roll, once. Each player's character is shared with the GM too: it appears among the GM view's characters, marked at the table, and its injuries, points, ammunition and form stay in step both ways, whether they're changed on the sheet, on the GM's card or in an encounter. In a fight, each player's Play tab shows the GM's battle map and the encounter's turn order and latest results; a player drags their own token, and on their turn sends the GM what they'll do, which the GM puts into the Encounter panel and runs. The GM can remove a player from the table, and give the table a new code: everyone at it moves with it, and the old code stops working. Without a table, the roller works exactly as it always has.
