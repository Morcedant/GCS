Legend Dynamics
Legend Dynamics RPG system

Heroes are made of skill, nerve and a little luck.

A tabletop roleplaying system built on three six-sided dice and concrete skills. One core, many worlds: Gothic Westerns, starships, and swords against sorcery.

Legend Dynamics RPG system

Legend Dynamics is a roleplaying system for heroic stories in any genre: concrete skills, a 3d6 roll-under core, and a small pool of Legend points that reward characters for their flaws. These core rules are setting-neutral. A setting pack supplies the roles, origins, skills, gear, weapons, tracks and dangers of one genre, and renames a few terms to suit it.

The rule to remember

Roll 3d6 at or below your adjusted skill to succeed. Your skill already includes your training and attribute. Do not add the attribute again.

Example: a skill of 13, with a −2 penalty for bad conditions, gives a target of 11. A roll of 9 succeeds by 2. A roll of 12 fails by 1.

How this book is organized

These are the Legend Dynamics core rules, which every setting shares. A setting pack adds its roles, origins, skills, gear, weapons and dangers, and may rename terms: Legend points might be Grit in a Western or Resolve in space opera.

Create your character

Work through the five steps in order. The character creator follows the same steps and checks the rules for you.

Step 1: Choose your role

Choose a role and describe what you bring. Your role places you in a group, which only changes your sheet’s colors and seal. A title or rank is agreed with the GM and gives no bonus.

Your setting pack supplies this (roles).

The creator marks your role’s four core skills Trained when you choose it. Keep them, raise one to your Specialty, or swap any of them for other skills; changing role swaps the core skills you haven’t changed.

Generalist or Specialist

Choose how your character trained (see Step 3):

  • Generalist: one Specialty at +3 and nine Trained skills.
  • Specialist: one Expert skill at +5 and seven Trained skills: a lifetime spent at one thing.

Step 2: Choose your origin, spend Edges, assign attributes

Attributes

Attribute Used for
Body Strength, endurance, resisting injury
Agility Coordination, reflexes, movement
Intellect Reasoning, observation, knowledge
Presence Leadership, persuasion, social influence
Will Resolve, concentration, resisting fear and influence

Origin and tier

Choose your origin from the Origins chapter. Every attribute starts at 10, and your origin adjusts some of them; the result is your base. Adjustments always total zero.

Origins can differ in power, so each has a tier from 0 to 3. Every character has 3 points to split between origin power and Edges: the higher the tier, the fewer Edges. Edges are useful in every scene, which keeps characters of every tier equally worth playing.

Your setting pack supplies this (origin tiers).

Edges

Spend all your Edges. Each one buys:

Edge What you get Limit
+1 attribute point One more point to spend in the next step, within the normal maximum 2
Extra Trained skill One more Trained skill in Step 3 3
Second advantage A second general advantage, with no extra complication 1
Extra Legend Start every session with 3 Legend points instead of 2 1
A friend A named contact who helps once per mission 1
A fine weapon or tool An heirloom weapon, a master’s instruments or a fine kit: +2 to one named skill when it’s used 1

Spending points

After applying your origin, distribute five additional points among the attributes, plus one for each attribute Edge.

  • The starting maximum is 13, plus any origin bonus to that attribute.
  • You cannot lower an attribute below its base to gain points.

Custom origin

You can build your own origin with the GM. See Making your own for the steps.

Step 3: Choose trained skills

A Generalist character chooses ten different skills: one Specialty at its governing attribute +3 and nine Trained skills at attribute +1. A Specialist chooses one Expert skill at +5 and seven Trained skills at +1. Each Extra Trained skill Edge adds one more Trained skill. All other skills are Untrained at attribute −4 when an attempt is plausible. Each skill has its own final target number; do not add the attribute again when rolling.

The Expert’s extra bonus matters most when the work is hard. With an attribute of 13, an Expert has a target of 18, which only natural 17–18 can fail; under a −4 “very difficult” penalty the target is still 14. If Athletics is the Expert skill, Dodge adds +2, the same as a Specialty.

Some origins give one skill Trained for free; it doesn’t use one of your Trained picks.

Broadening. A Specialist can broaden later (the GM’s call, usually after a significant mission): the Expert skill becomes a Specialty at +3, and two more skills become Trained.

Step 4: Describe your character

Choose your name, title if any, and a short reason for what you do. Then choose one advantage and one complication.

Top 10 reasons

Your setting pack supplies this (reasons).

  • Advantage. Choose either a general advantage or one of your origin’s advantages. An origin advantage replaces the general advantage and always brings its companion complication.
  • Complication. Choose one complication. You keep it even if you take an origin advantage, so a character with an origin advantage has two complications.
  • Allegiance (optional). Choose an allegiance below, or none.

An advantage gives +2 to one narrowly defined kind of task when the GM agrees it applies. It cannot improve every use of a broad skill. A complication creates choices and problems; it is not a permanent penalty.

Allegiance

Anyone can owe allegiance to one of the setting’s powers. It’s a benefit paired with a burden; the burden works like a complication and earns Legend when it causes you a real problem.

Your setting pack supplies this (allegiances).

Step 5: Prepare

Your Dodge is Agility −2, plus your Athletics training bonus: +0 if Untrained, +1 if Trained, or +2 if a Specialty or Expert. Begin uninjured, with two Legend points (three with Extra Legend) and your gear. Name your friend or your fine weapon or tool, if you took those Edges. Describe one connection to another player’s character.

Skills and gear

Choose the skill that matches your method. The GM chooses one primary skill for each distinct task.

Your setting pack supplies this (skills).

Untrained actions

A plausible untrained attempt uses attribute −4. Specialized work such as surgery requires relevant training or direct guidance. Guidance permits an attempt only when the GM finds it credible; it does not grant the expert’s skill.

Gear

Gear makes tasks possible rather than easier. Ordinary gear grants no blanket bonus; exceptional tools may justify +2. Missing essential tools can make a task impossible, or very difficult (−4) when the GM finds an attempt plausible.

Your setting pack supplies this (gear).

Actions and teamwork

When to roll

Routine work succeeds automatically when you have appropriate training, gear and time. Roll when failure could change the situation. Before rolling, establish your goal, the relevant skill, any modifiers, and what is at stake.

Circumstances Modifier
Favorable conditions or excellent preparation +2
Ordinary challenge under pressure 0
Difficult −2
Very difficult −4
Extreme −6

Apply the main circumstance modifier once. Add separately specified effects such as assistance or injury when they apply. The GM avoids charging twice for the same obstacle.

Results and margins

Add the three dice. A total at or below the adjusted skill succeeds. Margin of success equals the adjusted skill minus the roll. A larger margin may mean better information, more precise work, or a stronger position.

A natural 3 or 4 always succeeds. A natural 17 or 18 always fails. An impossible action receives no roll.

In an opposed contest, each side rolls its relevant skill. One success beats one failure. If both succeed, the larger success margin wins. A tie, or two failures, favors the defender or preserves the situation. For an automatic success above its target, count its contest margin as zero.

Assistance

One qualified assistant can roll a relevant skill before the lead character rolls. Success grants +1 to the lead roll; success by 5 or more grants +2. Failure gives no bonus. An 18 introduces a complication described by the GM. Other characters can do other tasks but cannot stack assistance bonuses on the same roll.

Failure and another attempt

Failure changes the situation: time passes, the trail goes cold, supplies run out, or danger grows. Do not repeat an unchanged roll until it succeeds. A new approach, new evidence, better tools, or more time may justify another attempt.

Success at a cost

When a roll fails by only 1 or 2, the GM may offer success at a cost: you get what you were after, but something else goes wrong. Typical costs are an injury box, broken gear, lost time (tick a clock), a box on a track, or someone noticing. The GM names the cost before you accept; you can always take the plain failure instead. A natural 17 or 18 is never a success at a cost.

Progress clocks

A clock is a circle cut into 2, 4, 6 or 8 segments that tracks something that takes time or builds toward disaster.

  • Danger clocks fill when things go wrong: a failed roll, a success at a cost, or time passing. When one fills, the danger happens.
  • Progress clocks fill as the characters succeed: one segment for a success, two for success by 5 or more. When one fills, the goal is achieved.
  • The GM sizes the clock (2 = quick, 4 = urgent or simple, 6 = substantial, 8 = long or complex) and shows it to the players.

Investigation and social scenes

Ask concrete questions and explain your approach. Evidence may require several different tasks. A successful roll does not force someone to abandon a core belief or reveal what they do not know.

Chases

Run a chase as a progress clock for the pursuers against a danger clock for the quarry’s escape. Each exchange, the characters roll the skill that moves them (the chase skill, or Athletics on foot): success ticks the pursuers’ clock, failure ticks the escape. Terrain, a tired mount or a damaged engine, and attacks made on the move (at −2) adjust the rolls.

Personal combat

Turn order and actions

Act in descending Agility order. Players choose the order of tied characters; the GM resolves other ties. A round lasts a few seconds. On your turn, move a short distance and take one meaningful action, such as attacking, reloading, helping, or attempting a task.

Attacking and defending

Roll a ranged skill for a ranged attack, a melee skill for a weapon in hand, or an unarmed skill for fists and grappling. If the attack succeeds, an aware target able to move may attempt Dodge. Dodge is Agility −2, with +1 for Trained Athletics or +2 for Athletics as a Specialty or Expert. Each target gets one Dodge attempt per round. A successful Dodge cancels the hit. Apply injury penalties to Dodge.

Solid cover blocks a shot. Partial cover imposes −2 on the attack. Weapon effects apply after a hit survives any Dodge. Success margin does not increase damage.

Injury

Boxes marked Condition Effect
0 Uninjured No injury penalty
1 Wounded −1 to physical actions
2 Seriously wounded −2 to physical actions
3 Incapacitated Unable to act; medical aid needed

Injury penalties replace each other rather than stacking. They apply to movement, attacks, Dodge, and other tasks the injury physically hinders.

Dying and death

  • Dying. A hit from a weapon that marks 2 or more boxes and brings you to three boxes leaves you Incapacitated and Dying. A Dying character dies at the end of the scene unless someone stabilizes them: one action and a successful Medicine roll with a medical kit.
  • A further hit. While you are at three boxes, any hit from a weapon that marks 2 or more boxes kills you. Fists, clubs and knives just keep you down.
  • Certain death. Some fates (a fall into a furnace, a beheading, disintegration) bypass the track entirely.

Your protection is avoiding the hit: Dodge, cover, and a Legend point, which reduces a hit by one box.

Weapons and damage

How many boxes a hit marks depends on the weapon, not on how well the attack succeeded.

Your setting pack supplies this (ammo).

Medical care

Diagnosis and stabilization

Roll Medicine when the diagnosis is uncertain or an urgent treatment decision matters. Emergency stabilization takes one action and a successful Medicine roll with a medical kit. It ends Dying and stops deterioration, but removes no boxes.

Field treatment

With a medical kit and several uninterrupted minutes, successful Medicine removes one injury box; success by 5 or more removes two. Each injury receives one field-treatment attempt; further attempts need changed circumstances.

Proper care

A proper infirmary grants +2 to Medicine. A successful treatment removes two boxes; success by 5 or more removes three. Allow the time the treatment requires.

Natural recovery

Each full day of rest clears one injury box. Rest means nothing strenuous. At three boxes, rest alone clears nothing: treatment must clear the first box.

Legend points and tracks

Using Legend points

Begin each session with two Legend points (three with Extra Legend); unused points do not carry over. Spend no more than one point on a single roll or injury event.

Spend one point to Timing and limits
Reroll your own roll Declare after seeing the result but before its consequences. Keep the second result, even if worse.
Reduce an injury by one box Declare when a hit would mark boxes, before applying them. Does not prevent certain death.
Establish a small preparation Propose a plausible detail, such as having packed something useful. GM approval is required.

Gain one point when one of your complications creates a meaningful problem you accept, including a companion complication. You can decline the proposed complication without penalty.

Tracks

Your setting pack supplies this (tracks).

Advancement

After a significant mission, the GM may authorize one relevant skill to advance from Untrained to Trained or from Trained to Specialty, or allow revision of an advantage or complication. Only a Specialist’s starting skill is Expert; a skill raised later tops out at Specialty (+3) unless they broaden (see Step 3).

Running missions

Mission seeds

Roll 3d6 on each table and read the total, or pick what you like. Middle results come up most often; the strangest sit at 3, 4, 17 and 18. The GM view in the creator rolls these for you and can start the clock.

Your setting pack supplies this (seeds).

The journal

End each mission with a short entry, written together:

  1. The mission. What happened, and did we succeed: fully, partly, or not at all?
  2. Who stood out? A moment worth remembering.
  3. Complications. Which ones came into play, and what did they cost?
  4. What changed? New allies or enemies, damage, open threads for next time.
  5. Advancement. After a significant mission, the GM may authorize one skill step for each character.

Making your own

The roles and origins in this book are a starting point, not a limit. With the GM’s agreement, any player can create their own role or origin. The creator has a Custom option at the end of each list, and its checklist holds a custom choice to the same rules as everything else.

A custom role

A role is a concept, not a package of powers: it gives no bonus of its own. A custom one only needs a clear place in the group.

  1. Name it and describe it. One sentence for what your character does that no one else does, in the style of the roles above: “Smuggler: knows every back door, bribe and hidden hold.”
  2. Choose a group. Pick the one it belongs with: one of your setting’s groups. The group only sets your sheet’s colors and emblem.
  3. Pick four core skills. The skills it can’t do without. Mark them Trained yourself in step 3 (the built-in roles do this for you); they count toward your normal Trained skills.
  4. Check it with the GM. It should fit the setting and overlap the existing roles as little as possible.

In the creator: in step 1, choose Custom role… at the end of the Role list, then enter its name and group. Write the description in What you bring.

A custom origin

Build it with the GM, one step at a time.

1. The concept

Two or three sentences: where they come from, what sets them apart, and how others see them. It should be something the origins in this book don’t already cover.

2. The tier

The tier measures how much the origin gives on its own. Each tier costs one of your 3 Edge points, so stronger origins start with fewer Edges.

Tier Edges Use it for
0 3 Ordinary people: a homeland, upbringing or culture, with no special abilities
1 2 One small, constant edge: keen senses, a tough hide, a free Trained skill, resistance to one hazard
2 1 Clear inhuman or supernatural gifts: venom, a second form, strong resistances
3 0 Remarkable beings: several strong abilities, and serious weaknesses to match

When in doubt, choose the higher tier.

3. Attribute adjustments

Give adjustments from −2 to +2 per attribute that total zero: a +2 needs a −2 or two −1s. The adjustments move the attribute’s base (+1 starts it at 11) and raise its starting maximum (13 plus the bonus). Most origins change two to four attributes.

4. Core rules (tier 1 and up)

One to three short rules that always apply: what they can do (see in the dark, resist cold, a free Trained skill) and what it costs (a weakness, a people’s distrust, a hazard they can’t abide). A strength with no drawback is worth more than it looks, so price it a tier higher.

5. Origin advantages

Write one to three. Each works like a general advantage: +2 to one skill in a narrow situation, or a small, limited permission (“once per scene…”). Each comes with a companion complication: a problem the trait causes, with a clear trigger and a real consequence, which earns a Legend point when it bites, like any complication. A character takes one advantage, general or origin, so make them distinct choices.

6. An alternate form (optional, tier 2 or 3)

If the origin changes shape, give the form attribute bonuses (usually +2 to one attribute and +1 to another), two or three traits, and its drawbacks. The shared rules for forms in Origins apply.

In the creator

  1. In step 2, choose Custom origin… at the end of the Origin list.
  2. Name it, choose the tier you agreed, and set its adjustments. The checklist confirms they total zero.
  3. Write its core rules in Other ability (GM approval required) in step 4.
  4. In step 4, choose Custom origin advantage… and enter the advantage and its companion complication.

Example

Hill-folk (tier 1). Stocky herders from high, cold country. Adjustments: Body +1, Presence −1. Core rule: at home in the heights: no penalty for thin air or cold. Origin advantage: Sure-Footed: +2 to Athletics to climb and keep your footing. Companion: Clannish: you trust your own people first; when it matters, accept the cost of that loyalty.

Complications

Choose one complication. These are sources of meaningful choices, not constant penalties. The GM proposes a concrete problem and offers one Legend point if you accept it. Every complication needs a trigger agreed with the GM; the creator suggests three for each.

Your setting pack supplies this (complications).

Quick reference

Creation: attributes start at 10, adjusted by origin (always totaling zero), plus 5 points; maximum 13 plus any origin bonus. 3 points split between origin tier and Edges. Generalist: one Specialty and nine Trained. Specialist: one Expert at +5 and seven Trained. One advantage (general or origin) and one complication; an optional Allegiance.

Skills: Untrained = attribute −4; Trained = attribute +1; Specialty = attribute +3; Expert (Specialist) = attribute +5.

Modifiers: favorable +2; ordinary 0; difficult −2; very difficult −4; extreme −6. Natural 3–4 succeeds; natural 17–18 fails.

Assistance: success +1; success by 5 or more +2; 18 causes a complication. One assistant per roll.

Near misses: fail by 1–2 and the GM may offer success at a cost; never on a natural 17–18.

Clocks: 2, 4, 6 or 8 segments. Danger clocks fill on failures and time; progress clocks fill on successes (2 segments on success by 5).

Combat: move plus one action. Hit → optional Dodge → weapon effect. Dodge = Agility −2 plus Athletics training (+0, +1, +2). A 2-box weapon that brings you to three boxes leaves you Dying; at three boxes, another 2-box hit kills.

Medicine: stabilize in one action. Field care clears 1 box (2 on margin 5+). Proper care gives +2 and clears 2 (3 on margin 5+). Rest: one box per full day, not from three.

Legend: start each session with two. Reroll, reduce an injury by one box, or establish a small preparation. Earn one from an accepted complication.

Play on Discord

One dice bot runs every Legend Dynamics setting. It rolls 3d6 under your target and keeps your table's points, clocks and tracks. The commands are the same everywhere; only a few names change with the setting.

Getting started
  1. The GM picks the setting. Run /game set once per Discord server; /game show checks it.
  2. Type / in the game channel. Discord lists every command and shows each option as you fill it in.
  3. Bring your sheet. The bot doesn't know your character: read your target number off your sheet and add up every modifier yourself.
