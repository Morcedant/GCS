Dominion: a calmer early game

19 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The computer players no longer race to out-arm each other early on. Until about turn 75, a computer power that's at war with no one keeps a defensive fleet and builds up its worlds instead, then eases into the usual arms race.
  • They start no wars of their own before turn 50 on Normal (60 on Easy, 35 on Hard). If you attack them, they arm as usual.
  • In test games, computer rivals' fleets at turn 60 were about a quarter smaller than before, with more buildings. By turn 80 they're back to full strength.
  • To keep their stronger economies from ending the game sooner, they don't start building the Corridor Engine before turn 100 on Normal (120 on Easy, 80 on Hard). Games last about as long as before.
  • The Vael were never building faster than anyone else. Their ships move farther and heal anywhere, which makes their fleets seem to be everywhere.

Dominion: earlier victories are remembered

19 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • If you win and keep ruling, the Objectives tab now shows the victory you played on from, for example "Won by the Corridor Engine on turn 133".
  • The end-of-game screen shows it too, when you finally retire or the game ends.

Dominion: the strategy guide, made general

18 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The strategy guide no longer gives specific advice: no build orders, research lists, fleet sizes or numbers to follow.
  • It covers the principles and the important steps to stay competitive instead: how the game is won, the early, middle and late game, choosing a path to victory, playing to your people's strengths, war, defense, threats, exploration, diplomacy, and common mistakes.
  • For how each rule works, it points to How to play in the game's Menu.

Dominion: a strategy guide

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A new strategy guide (Menu → Strategy guide, or /dominion/strategy.html): how you win and lose, and an opening for your first 30 turns that works for every people.
  • Three complete plans to follow: the Corridor Engine (every technology in order, with its cost), conquest (fleets, sieges, troops, supply and refits), and the highest score. Plus a plan for holding the Engine in open games.
  • Each people's best plan, and how the economy, battles (with hit chances), defense, pirates, creatures and the Wardens, exploring, diplomacy and difficulty work.
  • A list of common mistakes.
  • Its numbers come from the game's own data, so they stay right when the rules change.

Dominion: each people's own ships, and a new token sheet

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Every people now builds its ships in its own style, with its own outlines and class names. The Concord fly Aegis-class cruisers and Sovereign-class dreadnoughts; the Tessan, Magistrates and Dynasts; the Kel, Mandibles and Hivemothers; the Hegemony, Praetors and Imperators; the Vael, Mantas and Abyssals. All eight ship types have names.
  • What each ship does is the same for everyone. Only the look and the name change.
  • The map, the fleet panels, the build list and the battle reports all use them.
  • The fuel tanker and the traders' merchant convoys have their own outlines now.
  • A new printable token sheet: a page for each people's ships, a table of every class name, tokens for the traders, pirates, creatures and Wardens, and markers for orbital stations, batteries and fuel depots.

Dominion: difficulty choices for the computer players

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Easy, Normal and Hard are now presets for what the computer players do. Each option can be changed on the new-game form, under Computer players, and the difficulty then shows as Custom.
  • Strength, meaning fleet size and how readily they go to war: Gentle 0.7×, Standard 1× or Fierce 1.35×.
  • Build deep-space stations (Hard): computer players put stations in open space on the approaches toward a rival, about one per four colonies and four at most.
  • Fortify frontier outposts (Normal and Hard), Raid your fuel depots (Normal and Hard), and Form coalitions against the Engine holder (all three).
  • Pay pirates instead of fighting (Easy and Normal). On Hard they always fight.
  • The choices are fixed for the game. Games saved earlier use their difficulty's preset.

Dominion: deep-space stations

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • With Orbital shipyards, an outpost ship in an empty hex of open space can build a deep-space station there. It uses up the ship and costs 135 credits. It can't be built in a rift or with hostile ships present.
  • A deep-space station works like an outpost with an Orbital station: light guns, repairs, sight, supply for your fleets, fuel sales, and territory. It's shown on the map as a square, named after the nearest system.
  • It can be invaded, blockaded and bombarded like any outpost. If its station is destroyed, the site is gone.

Dominion: shipyards do early refits

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Any Shipyard now refits ships with the early systems (Deep sensors, Fold drives and Deflector shields) when they end a turn there.
  • Everything later (Targeting computers, the ship prototypes, Composite armor, Fire control, Drive tuning) still needs a Drydock.
  • The refit buttons and the standing order send ships to the nearest place that can fit what they're missing: a shipyard if it's only early systems, otherwise a drydock.

Dominion: sending ships for refits

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The Empire tab has a Refits card that counts your out-of-date ships, and a ⚙ refit tag on each fleet that has them.
  • "Send all out-of-date ships to drydock" splits them off their fleets and sends them to the nearest drydock that can refit, in one click.
  • A fleet's panel can send the whole fleet to the drydock, or just its out-of-date ships.
  • A standing order (on or off, in the Refits card): idle fleets with out-of-date ships go to the nearest drydock by themselves. Fleets told to hold position stay put.

Dominion: research reaches ships at a drydock

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Ships now carry the systems their power had researched when they were built: sensors, drives, shields, targeting, armor, fire control, fuel tanks and the ship prototypes.
  • New ships are built with everything you've researched. Ships already in service get new research only by ending a turn at one of your Drydocks, where they're refitted at no cost and new armor is added to their hull. It can't happen while enemy warships hold the drydock's orbit.
  • A fleet's panel says which ships need a refit and how far the nearest drydock is.
  • The drydock's class upgrades (frigate to cruiser, cruiser to dreadnought) are gone.
  • Your existing ships keep everything you'd researched before this update.
  • The computer powers research Orbital shipyards early, build a drydock at their capital, and send out-of-date fleets there before going on the attack.

Dominion: stations and drydocks do different jobs

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Orbital stations are forward bases: ships there now repair 2 hull a turn, not to full. Their guns, sight and supply are unchanged.
  • The Drydock is the home yard. Ships there still repair to full every turn, ships built at that world now cost 15% less, and ships there can be refitted a class up: a frigate into a cruiser, or a cruiser into a dreadnought.
  • A refit costs the difference in price, paid in credits and exotics, and the ship comes out at full hull. Refits are on the fleet panel.
  • Every world of yours still repairs your ships 1 hull a turn.
  • The computer powers refit ships at their drydocks when they're rich.

Dominion: outposts sell fuel and exotics

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • An outpost with a fuel depot or station sells fuel: +2 credits a turn, or +3 on a gas giant.
  • An outpost on an exotics deposit exports them: +2 credits a turn, on top of the exotics it produces.
  • Merchant convoys refuel at outposts with a depot or station within a hex of their route, paying the owner 2 credits each time.
  • A blockaded outpost sells nothing, and neither does one whose depot has been raided dry.
  • Each outpost's panel shows what it earns.

Dominion: orbital stations, and guns over outposts

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Orbital station (needs Orbital shipyards, 90): build one over a colony or an outpost. It has light guns (4 hull, one shot of 2, or +4 hull added to a battery's).
  • Ships at a station are repaired to full each turn. The station sees 2 hexes farther, and gives supply like a fuel depot.
  • Outposts can now have an Orbital battery or an Orbital station. Outposts have no industry, so you buy these with credits from the outpost's panel: 150 for a battery, 135 for a station.
  • The computer powers fortify outposts near their enemies when they're at war.

Dominion: pirate ransom and tribute, patrol pacts, depot raids, and the Free Traders

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Pirates demand payment: a ransom when their raiders hold one of your worlds, and tribute when a haven sets up near you. Pay and that haven's raiders leave all your worlds alone for 20 turns; refuse and they come anyway.
  • Pirate patrol pacts (Diplomacy tab): partners' warships guard each other's worlds from raiders, and each gets half the bounty when the other destroys a pirate ship. The computer powers propose and form them.
  • Depot raids: at war, warships holding an enemy fuel depot's system can raid it. They fill their own tanks and tankers, and the depot gives its owner no supply for 5 turns.
  • The Free Traders: merchant convoys run between colonies with an Exchange, paying both worlds' owners when they arrive. Pirates hunt convoys with no warships beside them.
  • Traders who reach your worlds sometimes offer a deal: exotics, a used frigate, star charts, or a rumor of where a relic lies.
  • Relics are no longer hidden right next to a homeworld.

Dominion: choose how many pirates and creatures

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A new setting, Pirates and creatures, with three levels: None, Light or Heavy. Choose it when you start a game, or change it at any time from the Menu.
  • Light is the default: one pirate haven at a time (two on large maps) with one raider fleet, a couple of crystal swarms, and one void leviathan from turn 70. Heavy keeps the full numbers.
  • Turning it down mid-game removes the extra havens, raiders and swarms at once, those nearest the settled worlds first. None removes them all.
  • Games saved earlier switch to Light.
  • Raiders who lose a ship at a world stay away from it for 15 turns, so defending a world drives them off.

Dominion: pirates no longer stop expansion

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Pirate havens first appear on turn 30 (was 14).
  • Raiders only go after established colonies (population 3 or more, founded at least 12 turns ago). Outposts and new colonies are left alone.
  • Pirates and crystal swarms never fire on unarmed ships, so colony ships, outpost ships, scouts, transports and tankers pass safely. A swarm still keeps anyone from settling its own system.
  • A pirate or creature blockade no longer starves a world or starts the surrender clock: it halves yields and stops growth only while it lasts.
  • Pirates and creatures had cut how far the powers spread by about a third by turn 60. Now it's about 3%.

Dominion: relics of the old empires, and the Outer Reach

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Eleven relics, one of each, hidden in ruins and anomalies. The first fleet to end its turn at one recovers it, and it's kept at the finder's capital. Each is worth 10 score.
  • A relic belongs to the world that keeps it: take the world and you take its relics. If the world is destroyed, they lie there, or nearby, for anyone to find.
  • The Founders' Star Chart expands the galaxy. Read it once and four rings of uncharted space, the Outer Reach, open beyond the rim, with new systems linked into the corridors and four more relics to find.
  • The Gate Shard opens a new corridor from one of your worlds every 15 turns.
  • The rest work by being held: faster ships, repairs anywhere, the whole map charted, more industry, science or credits, stronger guns, more population room, and protection against the Wardens.
  • The computer powers read the Star Chart as soon as they find it, and use the Gate Shard to link their capital to their farthest worlds.

Dominion: the Corridor Engine in open games

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • In an open game, finishing the Corridor Engine no longer ends the game. Its holder gets free corridor travel, a gate that jumps one fleet from the Engine world to any of its worlds every 5 turns, +2 credits on every colony, and 2 score a turn.
  • Only one Engine can stand. Others already under construction wait, with their work kept.
  • Capture the Engine world and the Engine is yours. Hold its orbit with its guns down and a barrage destroys it, and the race to build one starts again.
  • While enemy warships hold the Engine's orbit, it gives its holder nothing.
  • The computer powers resent the holder, and once it has held the Engine a while they form a coalition against it.
  • Standard 200-turn games are unchanged: finishing the Engine wins.

Dominion: advances, anomalies, new buildings, pirates and monsters

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Advances: once a technology is done, research builds on it a level at a time: +5% industry, science or credits a level (to 5), +1 warship hull or skill (to 2), +1 population room (to 3), +2 fuel (to 3). Each level costs more. Science never runs out of use.
  • More ways to research: anomalies (✦ on the map) that a scout surveys for 40–160 research, research pacts (+5% science per trade treaty, up to three), and the wrecks of pirates and creatures.
  • Four new buildings: Garrison barracks, Deep-space observatory (studies nearby anomalies without a ship), Drydock (full repairs every turn) and Orbital habitat (+2 population room).
  • Pirate havens appear near the settled worlds from turn 14 and send out raiders that blockade weak worlds and steal credits. Burn a haven for 100 credits and 50 research.
  • Crystal swarms guard rich and exotic systems from the start: clear one to settle there. Void leviathans drift in from turn 40 and feed on unguarded worlds. Both pay research when destroyed.

Dominion: Orbital batteries that defend a world

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The Orbital battery is a fortress now: 6 hull (8 with the shield grid), two shots a round at skill 13 for 2 damage each, and it repairs 2 hull a turn when no enemy holds the orbit. It costs 100 (was 70). Before, two cruisers silenced it every time without losing a ship; now two frigates or a lone cruiser die against it, and it takes a real fleet to bring it down.
  • While a world's guns stand, a siege can't blockade it: full industry, credits, trade and growth, and the siege clock (hunger, surrender) only starts once the guns are silent. Enemy warships in orbit still stop repairs, so a fleet that holds on wears the guns down.
  • A battery's troops add 2 to the world's garrison against invasion.
  • The world panel shows the guns' hull against their full strength, and the siege panel says whether the blockade has started.

Dominion: fuel tankers, and supply lines to defend

15 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Fuel tankers (any shipyard, 50): they carry 12 units of fuel. Travelling with a fleet, one tops up the tank when it runs dry, so the fleet reaches farther. Parked, it's a supply point: your fleets within 2 hexes stay in supply, a unit of its fuel each a turn. It fills up again in supply at home.
  • Supply lines can be cut: enemy warships in a supply point's system (a parked tanker, a fuel depot, a besieged colony) stop it giving supply. Tankers are unarmed, so escort them.
  • Tankers have their own muster point, and the computer powers bring one behind their main war fleet.

Dominion: fuel and fuel depots

15 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Fleets carry fuel: their thirstiest ship's tank, in turns of travel beyond supply (scouts 10, frigates and colony ships 6, cruisers and transports 5, dreadnoughts 4). Each turn a fleet moves beyond supply burns one; out of fuel it crawls a hex a turn. In supply it refuels. The Vael's living hulls need none.
  • Fuel depots: an outpost now gives supply only with one. Claim an outpost with its depot (40 credits), or build one there later (60 credits). A colony always gives supply.
  • A fleet's panel shows its fuel, and warns when a course ends beyond supply with too little in the tank.
  • The computer powers build depots and keep their attacks within reach of their fuel.

Dominion: muster points for new ships

15 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Standing orders for where new ships go: all of them, or warships, troop transports, colony and outpost ships or scouts on their own, to a world or outpost of yours. A kind's own order overrides the one for all ships.
  • Set them in the Empire tab's Strategic orders, or from a world's own panel (Send warships here). New ships leave as soon as they're built and join their kind waiting there.
  • A muster point that's taken or razed is struck off, and you're told.

Dominion: managing big fleets

15 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A fleet's ships are listed by type, with how many and their hull together; each ship's hull is a click away.
  • Split off by type: one, half or all of a type into a new fleet, the most damaged first (to send the wounded home); the admiral stays.
  • Join all here: every fleet of yours in a system joins the selected one in a click.
  • The Empire tab lists war fleets apart from scouts, transports and colony ships, with where each is, and folds a long list away.

Dominion: trade, an exotics market, and real sieges

15 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Trade: each colony earns credits from trade links, other colonies within 4 hexes that are yours or a treaty partner's; an Exchange doubles it.
  • An exotics market (with an Exchange): buy at the price, sell for three quarters of it; every unit moves the price, which drifts back.
  • Sieges have a cost: a besieged world makes half its industry and credits, trades and grows nothing, loses a population every 3 turns, and its garrison weakens toward half, so landings get easier. After 10 turns an ordinary colony starved to 3 population surrenders; a capital never does.
  • The computer powers send fleets to break sieges of their worlds, and use the market. In all-computer games worlds now change hands (5 to 8 in a typical war), by landings and surrenders.

A Difficulty setting for the GM

14 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The GM view's Map and fight tab has a Difficulty card: Standard, Tough (+1 to the threats' attack targets and one more box), Hard (+2 and two boxes, and one more foe in each group) and Deadly (+3 and three boxes, two more in each group). A minor foe keeps its one box.
  • It applies to threats as they're added: in the Encounter panel, on the battle map, and when a playtest sets up a scene. It's the core's rule for a tougher version, for every threat at once, in every setting and both fan games.

Aurora's playtest: good guys against bad guys

14 November 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Dead Air is rewritten as a clear heroes-against-villains case, to teach the game: save a terrified girl and forty commuters on a stalled train, find out who's behind the blackout, and stop him.
  • A new villain, Overcharge (Class IV), the Foundation's enforcer, sets the trap for Subject 19. The investigation pays off in the fight: take out the girl's tag and his force field drops from IV to III.
  • Sergei Grachyov no longer offers a trade; he watches from the platform at the end, a hook for later. The case ends with the girl home and the villain in cuffs.
  • The run sheet, the Campaign tab's outline, people and places, and the battle map's tokens (with a prompt for Overcharge's) follow the new story.

Dominion: sieges work, and a targeted barrage

14 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A world's orbital guns used to repair a point at the end of every turn, straight after the battle that knocked them out, so its orbit was never clear and troops could never land on a world with a battery or a capital.
  • Now a world under siege (an enemy's warships in its orbit) can't repair its guns, launch the ships it finishes, or raise a new battery: keep your warships in orbit through the end of a turn, and your transports can land the next.
  • Targeted barrage: a fleet holding an enemy world's orbit can destroy one of its buildings a turn. The Shipyard takes the ships in its yards with it; no population is lost. The computer powers use it too.
  • The invasion panel says how.

Aurora: super-speed by range band, and one Fallout track

13 November 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Speed, Flight and Super Leaping move by range band, never yards on the battle map: the sheet, the GM's cards and the table show the band (Class I to Medium, II to Long, III to Extreme, IV and up anywhere in the scene) beside the move on foot.
  • A new Super-speed fight range track on the battle map: the first lane is the fight itself, and each lane out is a range band from it.
  • Collateral, Scrutiny and Backlash are now one shared Fallout track of 6 boxes. Each mark is one of the three kinds; how many boxes are marked sets how bad it is, and the kind with the most marks says what form it takes. Clearing removes a mark of its own kind.
  • Tracks saved under the old names join Fallout as marks of their kind.

Consistency pass: core rules and the four settings

13 November 2026 · Rules change

  • Core: success margin doesn't increase damage unless a weapon's own entry says so (as the core's homebrew rules and the Shattered Crown's greatweapons already allowed).
  • Aurora: its own scale for Operant threats (attacks 14-17, Dodge 9-14, 3-6 boxes, 7-8 for the most powerful), beside the core's guidelines for ordinary foes.
  • Aurora: the quick reference gives Class V and VI damage and Toughness boxes.
  • The creator no longer shows the creation checklist for the GM's characters (notable Operants and extras), which are built from stat blocks.

Aurora: Toughness boxes, and harm above Class IV

13 November 2026 · Setting rules change

  • A hero gets one Toughness box for each Class of their best Defense (Toughness, a Force Field, an Aegis suit). Toughness boxes are marked before the three Injury boxes, carry no penalty, and clear when the fight ends.
  • Against anyone with more than 3 boxes, Class V attacks mark 4 boxes and Class VI mark 5, before Defense.
  • An Aegis suit now gives its Toughness II (physical and energy) in the GM view's encounters, as the guide says.
  • The sheet, the GM cards, the battle map tokens and the shared table show the Toughness boxes; ending an encounter clears them.

Aurora: a telling hit gets through any Defense

13 November 2026 · Setting rules change

  • An attack at or below a Defense's Class still marks 1 box on a telling hit (made by 5 or more, or a natural 3 or 4). Attacks a Class above it still mark a box fewer, and a telling one always marks at least 1.
  • This is Aurora's one exception to the core rule that a telling hit never adds boxes.
  • In test fights the party went from never beating a Defense IV-VI villain to winning 93% against Steel Hawk, about 80% against Helios and Grachyov, and 43-44% against Autuarch and the Atomic Man.

Alternate forms with their own numbers

7 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Any character can have their own alternate form (Powers step): its name and their normal shape's, attribute, Dodge and Move changes, the powers that work only in the form, and a note.
  • The sheet's and the table card's form switch, skills, Dodge, Move and the Encounter panel all use the form's numbers while the character is in it; a form-only power gives no attack, Defense or move outside it.

Aurora: every notable Operant as a ready-made sheet

7 November 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Eight supers the history names now have stat blocks: Herbert Unler, Zarloff the Elder, the Ivory Phoenix, Charlie Swift, Warlord I, Armageddon, Johnny Vanroy (Mr. Power) and John Marcus Mars Jr.
  • All 30 named Operants are ready-made sheets for the GM, built from their stat blocks; the GM view adds them as NPCs. Mr. Marvel, Uathach and the Ice Reaper come with their alternate forms.

Aurora: Piercing, and the neurogun

7 November 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Piercing (armor-piercing rounds): a piercing attack meets a Defense one Class higher than it is, but marks its own Class's boxes. Armor with a Defense rating protects as usual; a plain body armor vest doesn't help against it.
  • The flechette rifle is Class II, Piercing. The neurogun is an advanced taser: a Class II physical stun, Body −4 to stay up, which Defense II stops. A stun weapon with a Class now meets Defense like any other attack.

Aurora: damage types and the Vulnerability complication

7 November 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Four damage types: physical, energy, mystic and mind. Each Defense covers its own (Toughness physical; Force Field physical and energy); against another type it counts one Class lower. No Defense but Mind Defense helps against the mind, and force made by the mind is physical.
  • Weak (a box more), resistant (Defense +1) and immune (nothing below Class VI) to a type, element or material. The Vulnerability complication is a weakness chosen with the GM.

Aurora: Defense takes an attack one Class above it

7 November 2026 · Setting rules change

  • An attack must be at least one Class above a Defense to do anything, and then marks one box fewer; an equal or lower one does nothing (before, an equal attack marked a box fewer and a higher one hit in full). Falls follow: Toughness II walks away from a rooftop, IV from a tower, V from a fall from a plane.

Aurora: unstable powers come in levels

7 November 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Unstable I flares on a natural 18 with Powers, II on 17–18, III on 16–18, IV on 15–18. Lab-made gives Unstable II; the Unstable Powers complication gives the level chosen (I to III); the two add, to IV. The GM can raise or lower a level in play, and Strain 3 flares any power on 16–18.

Aurora: the Foundation's retrieval teams, and Dead Air

7 November 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Retrieval teams carry neuroguns, flechette rifles and stun grenades besides their tranquilizers and dampening net, and will kill to leave no witnesses (a Foundation hardware table in the GM's Guide).
  • The first case is now Dead Air, and Subject 19 has no codename.

No one starts below 10

7 November 2026 · Rules change

  • Origins only raise attributes, and their bonuses come out of the five attribute points, so every origin starts with the same total. Custom and homebrew origins: 0 to +2 each, +3 at most.
  • Ordinary people: 10 in every attribute, Dodge 8, Move 5 yards, 6 in anything untrained. An attribute drops below 10 only for a reason in play: an injury, a curse, a form.
  • The pregenerated characters are rebalanced to match, and older characters are put right when they're opened.

The GM view: NPCs, powers on the cards, and the playtests in one click

7 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • NPCs (the people the characters meet) are full characters in the GM view, from any threat or written in: their own boxes, portrait and token, linked to their battle map token, and on their own side in the Encounter panel.
  • Table cards list powers by Class and spells, show a power's move apart from the move on foot, and label the injury boxes; the same character isn't added twice unless they're a generic NPC.
  • A playtest loads in one click: its campaign, the ready-made characters, its NPCs, clocks and files, and each scene's map with its enemies (who grow with the adventure's threat).

Music, battle maps and character pictures

7 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Music for each setting, to play at the table or download; battle maps, tokens and portraits for the playtests, from the table server, with replaced pictures picked up.

The settings and the core rules, brought in line

28 October 2026 · Rules change

  • What the settings had worked out separately is now in the core rules, for every setting: chases where one side is clearly faster, chases by range (an opposed roll moves the range a band), and a damage track for vehicles; acting first and acting more than once a round; helping in a fight; pins that hold against someone much stronger.
  • Falls mark boxes by height (a story 1, a rooftop 2, a tower 3 and Dying, higher is certain death), with fire and foul air beside them. Every setting's fall uses it; Aurora's adds Classes.
  • Conditions, defined once: stunned, Frightened (keep away from the source, −2 against it, Will each turn to shake it) and Charmed. The settings' fear spells and horrors (Grave Chill, Dread, the Thing from the Deep, Emotion Control) now say Frightened.
  • Weapons: what "3, and Dying" means, explosions, stun weapons and jams; and armor, toughness, weaknesses and materials, the way every setting's gear and threats already use them. Guns now jam on a natural 18 in Trails of Blood & Dust too, as in the other settings.
  • Investigation: a head start (a character trained in the right field skips Confront) and a rival clock, from Fracture Lines; and enemies' files on the characters (+2 when it applies, destroyed when found), from Aurora, now in every setting's GM view.
  • Tracks: shared and personal tracks described once. A Mind track (Aurora's mental harm) is described as an option any setting can use; its middle box is now Overwhelmed, since Reeling is the Shattered Crown's word for stunned.
  • Smaller fixes: The Shattered Crown's Charm is resisted with Will (it said Presence), as everywhere else; Fracture Lines' ship fights are described as they're actually run.

Aurora: mind powers, a Mind track and Mind Defense

27 October 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Every character has a Mind track of three boxes beside the injury track: Shaken (−1 to mental actions), Overwhelmed (−2), Broken (unconscious or catatonic). Psychic attacks mark it; it never leaves anyone Dying, and a night's sleep clears it. It's on the play tab, the printed sheet, the GM view's cards and the battle map.
  • Mind Defense from Telepathy, Mind Control, Possession or an ExLAW psi-dampening headset: a weaker mind power does nothing, an equal one is resisted at +2. A Class IV mind against someone without it is resisted at −2.
  • Psychic attacks: Telepathy II (a psychic stab) and Illusion III (fears made real), one Class below the power, Powers against Will, never Dodge, Toughness or Force Field.
  • Rules for reading minds (and being noticed), mind links, being controlled (the player keeps playing; +2 against a harmful command, +4 against hurting a friend; Spark for another roll), emotions, illusions and sensing lies.
  • The Encounter panel resolves psychic attacks as Will contests on the Mind track (Lord Marrow's nightmare no longer meets Toughness or Dodge), and the sheet's roller applies the Mind penalty to mental actions.

Aurora: speed, flight, height and falling

27 October 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Speed, Flight and Super Leaping move a range band in a fight: Medium at Class I, Long at II, Extreme at III, anywhere in the scene from IV. The sheet's Move shows it.
  • Speed's steps, evened out: acts first (I), a second defense and blows at speed (II), two actions once a scene (III), two actions a round (IV), three (V). Blows at speed hit one Class below your Speed. Celerity's now hit at Class III.
  • Chases against vehicles: a car is Class I, a helicopter II, a jet III, a fighter IV; a higher Class wins without a roll.
  • Height counts in the distance (and on the battle map, where a token can have a height). Dives hit one Class higher; carrying slows you a Class; a flier stunned, knocked out or knocked back falls unless they make a Powers roll.
  • A fall is an attack with a Class by its height (I to IV), so Toughness protects against it.
  • The Encounter panel puts Speed first in the turn order, gives the extra actions and the second defense, offers a Dive to fliers, and warns when a flier falls.

Aurora: super strength, and every power's attacks in the Encounter panel

27 October 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Super strength has its own rules: lifting (one step past your Class is a push), objects with a Class to break, throwing (Athletics; a car also hits everyone next to the target), weapons in strong hands (the higher Class; ordinary weapons break above Class II), knockback from Class III, and pins that hold against weaker targets (Body −2 per Class of difference).
  • Strength and Size give +2 to Body for feats and breaking holds: a new choice in the sheet's roller.
  • A player character's attack powers now come into the Encounter panel at their Class: Strength (blows and throws), Natural Weapons, Blast, Telekinesis, Elemental and Weather Control, Gravity, Shadow, Manifest and Stretching. Before, a hero with powers fought there with fists and gear only.
  • The Encounter panel applies strong hands and logs knockback and how hard a pin is to break.

Aurora: Exposure, villains' files and team-ups

27 October 2026 · Setting rules change

  • A character with a Secret Identity has Exposure, a track of four boxes: marked when the secret slips, cleared by alibis. When it fills, the hero is unmasked and the player chooses the price. It shows on the play tab and in the GM view.
  • Exposure is relative: each hero keeps a list of who already knows the secret (family, a team, ExLAW, a villain). A legal status adds its own (Registered: the EMD; Reserve Agent: ExLAW's Director). Exposure measures the secret getting past them, and someone new who learns it goes on the list.
  • Villains keep files on the heroes: what they know, and +2 against that hero when it applies. The GM records them in the GM view; a hero who finds a file sees it on their sheet, and can destroy it.
  • Team-ups: two heroes combine powers in one action. The attack counts one Class higher and both mark a box of Strain. The Encounter panel has a Team-up choice for attacks with a Class.

Aurora: stats for every named character

The campaign builder, with card triggers

25 October 2026 · Website and wording

Trails of Blood & Dust: special loads and the hunter's shot

22 October 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Special loads: silver rounds, rock salt shells, cold iron shot, blessed rounds and incendiary shells, tracked as three uses a job each rather than counted bullets. Silver bullets in the gear list become silver rounds.
  • A load works against the weakness it's made of, whoever fires it. With a lore card for the creature and the matching load, a hunter's shot makes the called shot or effect the card permits.
  • Coming by loads can cost: buying silver marks Whispers only if folks learn what it's for, and church silver or the makings of fire mark Heat. Blessed rounds burn a demon but never banish it.

Legend cards and the price of information

20 October 2026 · Rules change

  • A Legend card records one thing the characters have established: the finding, its limits, the evidence or source, how it was learned, what it cost, and who holds it. Each setting names its cards.
  • A card can be earned by a discovery clock, or directly: seen or tested firsthand, a reliable account, or the subject's own word backed by evidence.
  • Information is never free: each direct source has a price, ticking the threat clock or a track. A clock's card costs what the clock already cost. Proposing an idea is free.
  • A real weakness works whether or not anyone holds a card for it.
  • A new section for the GM: making Legend cards.

Movement, reach and range bands

18 October 2026 · Rules change

  • Tighter range bands: Short 25 yards, Medium 100, Long 300, Extreme 1,000; Close stays 10. Running covers 20 yards.
  • Move: each character has a Move in yards, worked out from their attributes, Athletics and origin; Run is twice Move.
  • Weapon reach: every weapon in hand has a reach of 1, 2 or 3 yards.
  • Injury and hard going slow Move; stepping away from a fight is free.
  • Telling-hit effects depend on the weapon: Pin needs a weapon in hand, fists or a grapple.
  • Origin speed adjustments, and mounts and vehicles.

Balance revision

17 October 2026 · Rules change

  • Fine weapons and tools: +2 to one narrowly defined use of a skill, never a defense.
  • Additional Defense: a second defense each round at −2.
  • Magic limits: Quicker hand, every hostile burst names a defense or a resistance, personal magic marks at most 3 boxes, and charms still face the target's defense.
  • Telling hits: Break gear, Pin, Precise and Knock down clarified, with firm limits.
  • Medical treatment: one field-treatment and one proper-care attempt per set of injuries.
