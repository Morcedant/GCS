Dominion: home territory, admirals' powers, officers, key sites and the assault on the Heart

23 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Home territory: fleets that start a turn in your territory move 1 farther and mend 1 hull a turn; fighting in an enemy's territory out of your supply costs −1 to hit. The Kel's old colonies reach farther.
  • Admirals' powers: each admiral has one (Emergency jump, Overcharge the guns, Damage-control drill or Run silent), used from the fleet's panel, then ready again some turns later.
  • Officers: up to two a fleet (chief engineer, tactical, science, navigator), from anomalies, ruins, captured worlds and burned pirate havens.
  • Key sites: Ancient shipyards, Listening posts and Old waystations, whose holder and allies share the bonus.
  • The assault on the Heart: when it breaks, its prize is shared by every power that damaged it, by its share of the damage.
  • Computer players use their admirals' powers, assign officers, and go after key sites.

Dominion: ship counters, cloaking and space mining

23 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Ship counters: every warship is built to beat one kind of ship and aims for it first (+2 to hit). Frigates beat scouts, torpedo boats and stealth corvettes; cruisers beat frigates; dreadnoughts beat cruisers and orbital guns; the new torpedo boats (Heavy hulls) beat dreadnoughts with double damage.
  • Cloaking: the new Phase cloaks technology builds stealth corvettes. A fleet of only corvettes is unseen except near enemy worlds or by scouts, slips past enemy fleets, strikes first, and preys on unarmed ships. Warden probes are cloaked too.
  • Space mining: asteroid fields (credits), gas clouds (fuel) and crystal drifts (exotics) in open space. Park a mining ship on one to mine it each turn; a deep-space station nearby adds half. Fields run dry, and pirates hunt mining ships.
  • Computer players build counters to what their enemies field, send mining ships to nearby fields, and use stealth corvettes as raiders.

Dominion: signature technologies

23 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Each of the eight peoples has a signature technology only it can research, such as the Kel's Chitin printers (cheaper, tougher frigates) or the Ylith's Skyhook batteries (an extra battery shot).
  • Researching it unlocks a hub building that gives 2 science. Capture a world with a rival's hub and you learn their technology.
  • The Research tab lists all eight, and where you've seen rivals' hubs. The Peoples page and the people picker show each people's signature technology.
  • Computer players research their own and build its hub at their capital.

Dominion: computer players share Warden intelligence

23 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Computer players now tell the powers they like, and their allies, what they know of the Wardens: the probes, the signal and scanned worlds, and they pass on decoded patterns they were given. They check every few turns. Each share raises the receiver's regard for them, and has the same effects as when you share.
  • They share with you too, if they like you enough, and you're told what they shared. Like the Heart's location, or decoded patterns that give your warships +1 against the Wardens.

Dominion: share what you know of the Wardens

23 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • What you learn about the Wardens can now be shared with the other powers as diplomacy, raising their regard for you. Each power's Diplomacy card has a Warden intelligence section, and the Wardens bar over the map has Share what you know, to brief everyone at once.
  • What you can share: the probes (+5; they hunt probes harder), the signal (+5; an easier truce), their own scanned worlds (+3 each), where the Heart will rise if you answered the probe (+8; marked on their map), and how the machines think if you decoded it (+10; they hit the Wardens at +1 too). Each piece once per power.

Dominion: Warden probes, and the build-up to the war

23 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • During the Signs, Warden probes come out of the rifts, at most three at a time, a new one every 5 turns. Small, quick and unarmed, they scan worlds from a hex away, beyond the reach of orbital guns, then go back to the rift. Only warships can catch them.
  • Every probe that gets home makes the Wardens wake stronger (4% each, up to 24%), and the worlds probes scanned are the waves' first targets. Destroying a probe pays 30 research.
  • Something is watching: when you first see a probe, choose to hunt them (double research per probe), warn the other powers (+6 standing, and an easier truce), or let them look and learn (+40 research each time one scans a world of yours, though those worlds are attacked first).
  • It speaks: later, a probe at one of your worlds speaks to you. Answer it and learn where and when the Heart will rise, though its first waves come for you. Or jam it to delay the waking 6 turns for everyone (60 credits). Or record and decode it for 120 research and +1 to hit the Wardens for the rest of the war.
  • The bar over the map now also shows the Signs: probes in sight, what they're scanning, the Watchers, and how many probes have got home. Computer players no longer chase Warden machines they can't see.

Dominion: the Wardens, front and centre

23 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • While the Wardens are awake, a red bar over the map shows the Heart's state, how many machines are at your worlds and where, and any coalition under way. Its buttons call a coalition, show the attack, show the Heart, or open the details.
  • Call to arms: when Warden machines come for one of your worlds, a message offers a coalition against that wave on the spot (free, or paying the lukewarm), or sends your strongest free fleet there.
  • A plea for help: when a computer power that likes you is attacked by the Wardens, it asks you to send a fleet or rally the others. Helping is remembered, and so is refusing, a little.
  • Coalitions can now be called against a moving Warden wave; the members follow it until it's destroyed.

Dominion: an introduction

22 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A 40-second animated intro over the main theme, on a first visit: the corridors light up across the star clusters, some fail, the eight peoples appear, something wakes beneath the rifts, and the title. It opens with a Play button (browsers only play sound after a click) and can be skipped at any moment. Menu → Watch the intro plays it again.
  • An intro video can replace it: upload an MP4 to the music bucket as video/intro.mp4 and the game plays that instead, with its own sound. It shows up within a day of uploading.

Dominion: music that goes further

22 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A quiet pause of about half a minute to a minute between tracks, so the soundtrack breathes and repeats less. The Menu's Music section has a switch for it (on by default).
  • Tracks with no mood (named, for example, "interlude - drift.mp3", or with no mood in tracks.json) now join every mood's rotation, not just play when a mood has nothing of its own.

Dominion: computer players repair their ships

22 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A computer player's war fleet now sends its badly damaged ships for repairs, then back to the fleet. It does this when the fleet is out of combat, no enemy warships are within 3 hexes, and a yard can mend them within 6 turns. The cautious peoples (Concord, Mirrun, Tessan) send ships at 60% hull, the aggressive (Hegemony, Kel) only at 40%.
  • Never on the way to relieve a besieged world, answering a coalition, or heading to strike an enemy world. If too little of the fleet would stay behind, the whole fleet goes to the yard together.
  • Wars against computer players last a little longer: in tests they kept about 3% more warships and lost 2% fewer.

Dominion: stronger dreadnoughts

22 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Dreadnoughts now have 10 hull instead of 8. A dreadnought costs as much as two cruisers but used to lose to them badly: 10 dreadnoughts beat 20 cruisers only 1 time in 40. Now that fight is about even at base technology, and dreadnoughts pull ahead once Deflector shields are researched.
  • Dreadnoughts already in service count as damaged until they're repaired (any of your worlds, a station or a drydock).

Dominion: a more spacious galaxy

22 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • New galaxies are made of star clusters with open voids between them, on larger maps (radius 13, 16, 20 and 25 for small, medium, large and vast) with the same number of systems. Empires start in different clusters.
  • On a medium map the nearest rival homeworld is now about 14 hexes and 6 corridor jumps away, instead of 11 and 4. About a fifth of the map is open void beyond every system's supply range, where before there was none. Long corridors cross the voids, and deep-space stations finally have room to matter.
  • Computer players on Hard plan their deep-space stations along the longer approaches. Games already in progress keep their map.

Dominion: send damaged ships for repairs

22 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A fleet with damaged ships shows where best to mend them, counting the trip and the repair: a drydock fixes everything at once, a station 2 hull a turn, any other world of yours 1. It also shows how many turns that takes.
  • Send for repairs splits off the damaged ships (the sound ones and the admiral stay) and sends them there. In a battle, they withdraw first. Repair, then rejoin this fleet brings them back to the fleet once they're whole, following it if it has moved.

Dominion: call a coalition against the Wardens

22 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • While the Wardens are awake, the Wardens card on the Objectives tab can call a coalition. Pick the Heart or one of its Nodes, and see which powers will come: free if they like you, for credits if they're lukewarm, or not at all, with the reason.
  • Members send their strongest war fleets, up to 60% of their warships, and hold the target with you for 20 turns or until it falls, without withdrawing. When it falls, everyone who fought it thinks better of each other. One call every 10 turns.
  • How to play and the strategy guide mention it.

Dominion: one finished item per world per turn

22 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A world now finishes one item from its build queue a turn. Rushing the first item no longer lets the world's stockpiled industry finish everything behind it at once. Industry left over goes into the next item, which finishes the turn after, so nothing is wasted.

Dominion: portraits found by name

22 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The table server now lists the pictures in the music bucket's peoples/ folder, and the game and the Peoples page use them by their real names. Any of png, jpg, jpeg, webp, gif or avif works, in any capitalisation, named after the empire or its people (mirrun.png, Human.jpeg, Ozhvek.webp).
  • Each picture's address carries a version tag, so a picture replaced or uploaded after the page first looked for it shows at once instead of waiting out the cache.

Dominion: clearer travel times

21 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A fleet's route on the map now shows a numbered marker where it will stop at the end of each turn, so it's plain when corridor jumps carry it a long way in one turn. A jump along a corridor costs one move however long it is (a quarter of a move with Corridor theory).
  • Travel times are counted step by step, the way fleets actually move, so a route through nebulae no longer shows a turn too few. The route label, the fleet panel and the order's message all use the same count.

Dominion: bug reports, crash recovery, and steadier music

21 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Menu → Download a bug report saves a file with any errors the game recorded, your browser, the game's settings, its recent log and your save, plus a note of what happened, so a problem can be played back and fixed. The game keeps a log of the last 40 errors in your browser.
  • If a turn fails partway, the game goes back to exactly where it was before End turn, explains what happened, and offers the bug report, instead of sticking on Thinking.
  • Music: a song that won't load now plays from the table server's copy at normal volume (before, it could play silently, or stop the music altogether). A song that stalls is skipped. If several fail in a row, the music stops and says so, and Next track tries again. Music problems are recorded in the bug report.

Dominion: fix for End turn sticking on Thinking

21 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Fixed End turn freezing on Thinking… partway through a game. The tribute a computer player demands from another empire shared its name with the pirates' tribute demand, and in the built game the pirates' version replaced it, so the turn crashed. Saved games are unaffected: reload the page and carry on.
  • The build now stops if two parts of the game use the same name, so this kind of clash can't slip through again.

Dominion: painted portraits from the music bucket

21 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Painted portraits now come from the music bucket's peoples/ folder instead of the game's files. Upload a square image named after an empire (for example peoples/mirrun.png, or .webp or .jpg) and it shows at once on the Peoples page, in the people picker and on the Diplomacy tab, with no rebuild. Empires without one keep their drawing.

Dominion: three new empires, and the peoples' portraits

21 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Three new empires from the Fracture Lines setting. The Mirrun Commonwealth (Pelagic scholars): +20% science, Ocean worlds hold 3 more population, and they start on an ocean world. The Ylith Aeries (Far sight): ships and worlds see 2 hexes farther, and scouts and colony ships move 1 faster. The Zhaq Free Republics (Never again): garrisons half again as strong that never weaken under siege, and besieged worlds starve half as fast.
  • Each new empire has its own leader and voice, ship classes and outlines, admirals, directives and computer-player character. Up to 4 rivals are drawn from all eight.
  • Every people now has a portrait on a hex token, drawn from the setting's descriptions. They appear in the people picker, on the Diplomacy tab, on the token sheet (hex tokens to mark whose a world is), and on a new Peoples page with each people's looks, trait, leader and ships.
  • Painted art can replace the drawings: the Peoples page has an image-generator prompt for each people, and a square picture named after the empire in the game's art folder is used on the Peoples page.

Dominion: computer players use influence

21 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Computer players now use influence on each other and on you, at most once every 12 turns each, in keeping with their character. A weak power facing a much stronger neighbour sends gifts or makes amends. One losing a war pays a friend to join it. One that resents the leader or the Corridor Engine's holder denounces them to others. Strong powers squeeze tribute from weak ones.
  • You're told when a rival sends you a gift, makes amends to you, or denounces you.
  • Two new computer-player options on the new-game form: Use influence (on at every difficulty) and Demand tribute from you (off on Easy).

Dominion: influence in diplomacy, and a space travel guide

21 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Influence, on each power's card on the Diplomacy tab. Gifts of credits or exotics raise their regard, though gifts close together count for less. You can make amends for a grievance they remember. Each proposal shows whether they'd accept, and you can sweeten it with credits.
  • Pressure: demand tribute from a power whose fleet is far weaker than yours (they pay or refuse, and resent it either way), denounce a rival to a third power, or ask a power to join your war, free if they like you, for credits if they're only lukewarm.
  • How to play has a new Space travel section: ship speeds, open space, corridors, nebulae, rifts, sealed sectors, collapses, the Gate Shard and Engine gate, fuel and supply, and engagements. The strategy guide covers influence in general terms.

Dominion: withdrawing, detaching ships, decisive orbital guns, updated guides

21 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Engaged fleets: a fleet sharing a hex with enemy warships, or with an enemy world's guns, can't simply sail away. Withdraw pulls it out: it fights the next battle's first exchange defensively, then falls back toward its nearest world. Computer players withdraw from battles they're clearly losing.
  • Detach and send: choose exactly how many ships of each type to take from a fleet, then send them to a world or at an enemy. In a battle you can detach some ships and withdraw just those.
  • Orbital guns no longer stalemate: they fire first in each exchange and finish off the most damaged ship. Knocking out a world's guns now counts toward winning the battle, and the battle report says so.
  • The strategy guide has new sections on fuel and supply, battles and withdrawing, sealed sectors and collapses, and settings and music. The in-game How to play and the tutorial cover the same.
  • Music on the website, the fan sites and in Dominion now plays from the music bucket's public, cached address, falling back to the table server if needed.

Dominion: corridor collapses are rarer, and hit harder

20 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A corridor now fails from turn 70, every 60 to 90 turns: about twice in a standard game. Everyone gets 5 turns' warning.
  • It's always one of the corridors that matter most: the one with the longest way round without it, between settled worlds (most of all, between two powers).
  • Charting a new route takes a scout resting 3 turns near either end, and it's worth 60 research.

Dominion: fuel reserve, sealed sectors, corridor collapses

20 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A fuel reserve for every power, shown in the top bar. Colonies make a little fuel, a capital and gas giants more, and fuel depots and stations make the most. Each depot also lets you store 15 more. Fleets moving in supply, refuelling, filling tankers and burning hard all use it, and a short reserve slows your fleets. Raiding a depot steals from its owner's reserve.
  • Burn hard: a moving fleet can go a hex a turn faster, paid for from the reserve in supply or from its own tank beyond supply. Computer players burn hard to relieve a world under siege.
  • Sealed sectors: the Dead Zone and the Warden Graveyard can't be entered until a power researches Rift drives (a new Physics technology), or someone lights a Rift beacon on a world within 3 hexes of the sector (200 credits, 10 exotics). A beacon opens the sector to everyone and adds a corridor into it.
  • Corridor collapses: from turn 45, every 25 to 40 turns, a corridor fails, with 4 turns' warning and a red line on the map. It's never a world's last corridor. A scout that rests near either end charts a new corridor and earns 40 research. Collapses can be turned off on the new-game form.
  • Long games run faster.

Dominion: new sectors

20 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The map grows in a long game. From turn 110, three rings of new space open beyond the rim. An open game opens another sector every 80 turns, up to four in all; a standard game gets one.
  • Each sector has its own character. The Corsair Reach is held by pirate havens and rich in exotics. The Warden Graveyard is full of ruins guarded by Warden husks, whose wrecks pay in research. The Golden Frontier has rich, open, good worlds and no guards. The Dead Zone has no corridors, thick nebula, and few worlds, almost all of them with exotics.
  • Every power hears 10 turns ahead which sector is coming. The Objectives tab tracks the sector that's coming and the ones already open, and a system's panel says which sector it's in.
  • New sectors can be turned off on the new-game form. The pirate and creature sectors only come when the game has pirates and creatures.
  • Long games run faster: the supply check now looks only at nearby worlds.

Dominion: extended play for music

20 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Give a track extended play in the dominion folder's tracks.json: "repeat": 3 plays it three times in a row, and "loop": true keeps it looping until the game's mood changes. Repeats are seamless.
  • The Menu's music card has a Loop this track switch for whatever is playing, and shows "play 2 of 3" or "looping".
  • The main theme can repeat, but never loops forever, so the game's mood always takes over.

Dominion: a main theme

20 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Dominion can play a main theme when it starts up: on the title screen, or in a new game's first turn. Name the track "theme - ….mp3" in the dominion music folder.
  • The theme plays once per visit, through to its end, and then the game's own mood takes over: exploration, war or crisis.

Dominion: send all your ships somewhere in one click

20 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Every explored system's panel now has a "Send your ships here" section. "Send all warships" sends every fleet with a warship in it, including the transports and tankers travelling with them. "Send every ship" sends everything you have.
  • Each fleet sets out by the quickest way, at its own speed. The message says how long the last one will take, and whether any can't find a way.
  • "Undo the last send" puts every fleet back to what it was doing, until you end the turn.
  • It works for your own worlds, empty systems, and an enemy's world you want to attack.

Dominion: the Wardens, a persistent threat

20 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The Signs: about 35 turns before the Wardens wake, dormant Warden Watchers surface by the rifts one by one, armed and watching, while transmissions on every band grow more urgent. Destroy them while they sleep, for research and one less Node later.
  • The Wardens spread like an empire. The Heart wakes with Nodes of its own around it, every Watcher still standing wakes as a Node, and every world they burn to rock becomes a new Node. Each Node sends out hunters.
  • They threaten everyone: one wave in three goes for the strongest power, and half of them go for anyone building the Corridor Engine, the corridors they were made to guard.
  • Harder to kill: the Heart can't be harmed while its own Nodes stand. Nodes and the Heart can only be worn down over several battles, and they mend only when no one is besieging them. Their strength follows the whole galaxy's combined fleets.
  • They come back: break the Heart while a Node still stands, and a new Heart, a quarter stronger, grows there after 30 turns. It's over only when the Heart and every Node are gone.
  • New Objectives-tab states for the Signs and for a broken Heart with Nodes still standing, a NODE marker on the map, research for destroying Watchers and Nodes, and the new machines on the token sheet.
  • In test games the Wardens now hold out for about 20–45 turns after waking (around 30 on average), where before they were broken in about 13.

Dominion: music

20 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Dominion plays music now. Tracks come from the dominion folder of the music bucket, and the mood follows the game: exploration in peacetime, war while you're at war, crisis once the Wardens wake.
  • Name tracks by mood: "exploration - 01.mp3", "war - 01.mp3", "crisis - 01.mp3". Anything else plays in any mood.
  • Tracks are shuffled, so the same one doesn't come round twice in a row, and a change of mood fades into the next track.
  • The ♪ button in the top bar turns the music on or off. The Menu has the volume, a Next track button, and what's playing. Your settings are remembered.

Dominion: provocation brings war, early game or not

19 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A computer power that's truly provoked no longer waits for the early-war turn. That means its regard for you has fallen to −40 or worse, or you've committed a grave offence against it, such as breaking an alliance, in the last 20 turns.
  • It still needs to feel strong enough to attack. Ordinary friction, like crowded borders or a refused trade, still waits until turn 50 on Normal (60 on Easy, 35 on Hard).

Dominion: a calmer early game

19 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The computer players no longer race to out-arm each other early on. Until about turn 75, a computer power that's at war with no one keeps a defensive fleet and builds up its worlds instead, then eases into the usual arms race.
  • They start no wars of their own before turn 50 on Normal (60 on Easy, 35 on Hard). If you attack them, they arm as usual.
  • In test games, computer rivals' fleets at turn 60 were about a quarter smaller than before, with more buildings. By turn 80 they're back to full strength.
  • To keep their stronger economies from ending the game sooner, they don't start building the Corridor Engine before turn 100 on Normal (120 on Easy, 80 on Hard). Games last about as long as before.
  • The Vael were never building faster than anyone else. Their ships move farther and heal anywhere, which makes their fleets seem to be everywhere.

Dominion: earlier victories are remembered

19 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • If you win and keep ruling, the Objectives tab now shows the victory you played on from, for example "Won by the Corridor Engine on turn 133".
  • The end-of-game screen shows it too, when you finally retire or the game ends.

Dominion: the strategy guide, made general

18 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The strategy guide no longer gives specific advice: no build orders, research lists, fleet sizes or numbers to follow.
  • It covers the principles and the important steps to stay competitive instead: how the game is won, the early, middle and late game, choosing a path to victory, playing to your people's strengths, war, defense, threats, exploration, diplomacy, and common mistakes.
  • For how each rule works, it points to How to play in the game's Menu.

Dominion: a strategy guide

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • A new strategy guide (Menu → Strategy guide, or /dominion/strategy.html): how you win and lose, and an opening for your first 30 turns that works for every people.
  • Three complete plans to follow: the Corridor Engine (every technology in order, with its cost), conquest (fleets, sieges, troops, supply and refits), and the highest score. Plus a plan for holding the Engine in open games.
  • Each people's best plan, and how the economy, battles (with hit chances), defense, pirates, creatures and the Wardens, exploring, diplomacy and difficulty work.
  • A list of common mistakes.
  • Its numbers come from the game's own data, so they stay right when the rules change.

Dominion: each people's own ships, and a new token sheet

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Every people now builds its ships in its own style, with its own outlines and class names. The Concord fly Aegis-class cruisers and Sovereign-class dreadnoughts; the Tessan, Magistrates and Dynasts; the Kel, Mandibles and Hivemothers; the Hegemony, Praetors and Imperators; the Vael, Mantas and Abyssals. All eight ship types have names.
  • What each ship does is the same for everyone. Only the look and the name change.
  • The map, the fleet panels, the build list and the battle reports all use them.
  • The fuel tanker and the traders' merchant convoys have their own outlines now.
  • A new printable token sheet: a page for each people's ships, a table of every class name, tokens for the traders, pirates, creatures and Wardens, and markers for orbital stations, batteries and fuel depots.

Dominion: difficulty choices for the computer players

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Easy, Normal and Hard are now presets for what the computer players do. Each option can be changed on the new-game form, under Computer players, and the difficulty then shows as Custom.
  • Strength, meaning fleet size and how readily they go to war: Gentle 0.7×, Standard 1× or Fierce 1.35×.
  • Build deep-space stations (Hard): computer players put stations in open space on the approaches toward a rival, about one per four colonies and four at most.
  • Fortify frontier outposts (Normal and Hard), Raid your fuel depots (Normal and Hard), and Form coalitions against the Engine holder (all three).
  • Pay pirates instead of fighting (Easy and Normal). On Hard they always fight.
  • The choices are fixed for the game. Games saved earlier use their difficulty's preset.

Dominion: deep-space stations

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • With Orbital shipyards, an outpost ship in an empty hex of open space can build a deep-space station there. It uses up the ship and costs 135 credits. It can't be built in a rift or with hostile ships present.
  • A deep-space station works like an outpost with an Orbital station: light guns, repairs, sight, supply for your fleets, fuel sales, and territory. It's shown on the map as a square, named after the nearest system.
  • It can be invaded, blockaded and bombarded like any outpost. If its station is destroyed, the site is gone.

Dominion: shipyards do early refits

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Any Shipyard now refits ships with the early systems (Deep sensors, Fold drives and Deflector shields) when they end a turn there.
  • Everything later (Targeting computers, the ship prototypes, Composite armor, Fire control, Drive tuning) still needs a Drydock.
  • The refit buttons and the standing order send ships to the nearest place that can fit what they're missing: a shipyard if it's only early systems, otherwise a drydock.

Dominion: sending ships for refits

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • The Empire tab has a Refits card that counts your out-of-date ships, and a ⚙ refit tag on each fleet that has them.
  • "Send all out-of-date ships to drydock" splits them off their fleets and sends them to the nearest drydock that can refit, in one click.
  • A fleet's panel can send the whole fleet to the drydock, or just its out-of-date ships.
  • A standing order (on or off, in the Refits card): idle fleets with out-of-date ships go to the nearest drydock by themselves. Fleets told to hold position stay put.

Dominion: research reaches ships at a drydock

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Ships now carry the systems their power had researched when they were built: sensors, drives, shields, targeting, armor, fire control, fuel tanks and the ship prototypes.
  • New ships are built with everything you've researched. Ships already in service get new research only by ending a turn at one of your Drydocks, where they're refitted at no cost and new armor is added to their hull. It can't happen while enemy warships hold the drydock's orbit.
  • A fleet's panel says which ships need a refit and how far the nearest drydock is.
  • The drydock's class upgrades (frigate to cruiser, cruiser to dreadnought) are gone.
  • Your existing ships keep everything you'd researched before this update.
  • The computer powers research Orbital shipyards early, build a drydock at their capital, and send out-of-date fleets there before going on the attack.

Dominion: stations and drydocks do different jobs

17 November 2026 · Website and wording

  • Orbital stations are forward bases: ships there now repair 2 hull a turn, not to full. Their guns, sight and supply are unchanged.
  • The Drydock is the home yard. Ships there still repair to full every turn, ships built at that world now cost 15% less, and ships there can be refitted a class up: a frigate into a cruiser, or a cruiser into a dreadnought.

The sheet, the roller, the shared table and the Encounter panel use them.
  • The core rules' Making your own chapter gives the numbers to keep homebrew in line with the book's.

Fracture Lines: example data records, the Countdown and skill

22 October 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Seven example data records in the GM's Guide: a Halvane lattice core, the Warden of the Ashkarr, a corridor engine, a spatial fracture, a living hull, corporate black-site encryption and an alien weapon cache, each with what goes wrong in each phase.

  • Rival clocks: an ordinary danger clock for whoever is racing the crew to the same find.

Trails of Blood & Dust: special loads and the hunter's shot

22 October 2026 · Setting rules change

  • Special loads: silver rounds, rock salt shells, cold iron shot, blessed rounds and incendiary shells, tracked as three uses a job each rather than counted bullets. Silver bullets in the gear list become silver rounds.
  • A load works against the weakness it's made of, whoever fires it. With a lore card for the creature and the matching load, a hunter's shot makes the called shot or effect the card permits.
  • Coming by loads can cost: buying silver marks Whispers only if folks learn what it's for, and church silver or the makings of fire mark Heat. Blessed rounds burn a demon but never banish it.
  • A wraith can now be hurt by rock salt in the Encounter panel, as its weakness already said.

Legend cards and the price of information

20 October 2026 · Rules change

  • A Legend card records one thing the characters have established: the finding, its limits, the evidence or source, how it was learned, what it cost, and who holds it. Each setting names its cards.
  • A card can be earned by a discovery clock, or directly: seen or tested firsthand, a reliable account, or the subject's own word backed by evidence.
  • Information is never free: each direct source has a price, ticking the threat clock or a track. A clock's card costs what the clock already cost. Proposing an idea is free.
  • A real weakness works whether or not anyone holds a card for it.
  • A new section for the GM: making Legend cards.

Movement, reach and range bands

18 October 2026 · Rules change

  • Tighter range bands: Short 25 yards, Medium 100, Long 300, Extreme 1,000; Close stays 10. Running covers 20 yards.
  • Move: each character has a Move in yards, worked out from their attributes, Athletics and origin; Run is twice Move.
  • Weapon reach: every weapon in hand has a reach of 1, 2 or 3 yards.
  • Injury and hard going slow Move; stepping away from a fight is free.
  • Telling-hit effects depend on the weapon: Pin needs a weapon in hand, fists or a grapple.
  • Origin speed adjustments, and mounts and vehicles.

Balance revision

17 October 2026 · Rules change

  • Fine weapons and tools: +2 to one narrowly defined use of a skill, never a defense.
  • Additional Defense: a second defense each round at −2.
  • Magic limits: Quicker hand, every hostile burst names a defense or a resistance, personal magic marks at most 3 boxes, and charms still face the target's defense.
  • Telling hits: Break gear, Pin, Precise and Knock down clarified, with firm limits.
  • Medical treatment: one field-treatment and one proper-care attempt per set of injuries.
