This book is for whoever runs the game, and anyone can read it. It gathers what the GM reaches for at the table: venture seeds, the threats with their numbers, and how to use the GM view's battle map and Encounter panel. The rules themselves are in the **Players' Guide**; this book builds on them.

# Threats

Opponents use the same rules as the characters. **Minor opponents** (guards, bandits, cultists) have **one box**: any hit that gets through takes them out of the fight.

| Threat | Attack (target) | Dodge | Boxes | Weapon | Trait |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guardsman or bandit (minor) | Melee 11 | 7 | 1 | Spear or club | **Numbers.** Three or four of them; they flee when half are down. |
| Cultist (minor, many) | Melee 10 | 6 | 1 | Dagger | **Fanatics.** One in each group carries a blessing: Ward on the others. |
| Sellsword captain | Melee 14, Leadership 12 | 10 | 3 | Sword, pistol | **Command.** Their band gets +1 while the captain stands. |
| Assassin | Melee 14, Stealth 15 | 11 | 3 | Poisoned dagger | **First strike.** Attacks from hiding at +2; poison: Body roll or Reeling. |
| Sorcerer | Sorcery 14 | 9 | 3 | Shadow Bolt, Hex | **Warded.** Attacks against them are at −2 until the Ward is broken (Shatter or a hit by 5+). |
| Serpent-priest | Sorcery 13, Persuasion 14 | 10 | 3 | Venom, Charm | **Serpent-lord.** Commands snakes; its bite poisons. |
| Ghoul or tomb-dead | Brawling 12 | 7 | 2 | Claws | **Hunger.** Fire and daylight drive them back; they never stop coming. |
| Giant ape or great beast | Brawling 15 | 9 | 5 | Claws and jaws (2) | **Huge.** Hits of 1 box mark nothing. |
| Demon | Melee 15, Sorcery 14 | 11 | 4 | Claws (2), Witchfire | **Bound.** Holy rites and its true name mark an extra box; it must keep a bargain to the letter. |
| Shard-bearer | Melee 15 or Sorcery 15 | 11 | 5 | As the bearer | **Crown shard.** One great power: command, fire, flight, terror. Breaking the bearer's will ends it. |
| Thing from the Deep | Brawling 16 | 8 | 6 | Tentacles (2) | **Horror.** Once per scene, everyone who sees it rolls Will or flees in terror. |

**Adjusting difficulty.** For a tougher version, add +1 to its targets and one box; for a weaker one, subtract.

# The GM's tools

## The battle map

The GM view of the character creator has a **battle map** for fights where it helps to see who is where. It is a tool, not a rule: everything on it follows the *Personal combat* rules in the Players' Guide, and a fight without it works the same way. Open **How to use the map** on the map itself for the buttons, step by step.

**Two kinds of map.**

- **A hex map** for fights where position matters: cover, doorways, who can reach whom. The GM chooses what one hex is: 1 yard for a brawl in a room, 5 or 10 yards for a street, 50 for open ground. Dragging a token or measuring shows the distance and its **range band**, so the GM can read the penalty from the Range table in the Players' Guide.
- **A range track** for fights where only distance matters: a chase, a running fight across open country. It has a lane for the party and one for each range band. Moving a token into the next lane moves it one band in or out.

**Tokens.** The GM places the party with one button. Their tokens stay linked to the characters in the GM view: marking Injury, Dying, the stunned condition or a Fortune point on a token marks it on the character, and the other way round. Opponents come from the setting's threats with their boxes filled in. Their pips fill as they are hurt, and a token whose boxes are all marked is taken out.

**Keeping a map.** The map stays in the GM's browser. **Save map to file** keeps both maps, their pictures and tokens in one file, to prepare a fight ahead of time or share it with another GM.

## The Encounter panel

The GM view also has an **Encounter** panel that runs a fight by the *Personal combat* rules and shows every roll. Add characters from the GM view and opponents from the Threats table (or make one up on the spot), then start: it keeps the turn order by Agility, and for each attack works out the range, the defense (Dodge, Parry or Block, one a round), the boxes marked and any telling hit, offering a Fortune point choices at the moment they apply. Hits land on the character cards and the battle map, and with gritty combat on, grievous wounds are rolled and kept. The panel keeps a log and a summary of the fight, which can be saved to a file. It's a helper, not a rule: the GM can rule any result differently, and the log says so. For each attack, choose what it's made of (cold iron, silver or fire), and the panel applies the rules that turn on it: a shapechanger's Tough in their form (and what gets past it), a weakness that marks one box more, and a creature only the right weapon can hurt.
