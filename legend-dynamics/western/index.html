              Legend Dynamics · Gothic Western

              Dust & Damnation Players' Guide

              A Gothic Western for the Legend Dynamics RPG systemBy Don Smallwood

              The railroad is coming, and it is bringing more than settlers. In the fog-bound timber towns and lamplit mining camps, the old things of the world have followed the wagons west: wolves that walk as men, the dead that will not stay buried, pale strangers who only come out after dark, and witches who know their names. Dust & Damnation is a Gothic Western for the Legend Dynamics RPG system. Most people never see the darkness. You do, and you stand between it and them.

              Dust & Damnation is not tied to a single place or year. Set your game in any territory, from the 1860s to the 1890s.

              The rule to remember

              Roll 3d6 at or below your adjusted skill to succeed. Your skill already includes your training and attribute. Do not add the attribute again.

              Example: a skill of 13, with a −2 penalty for bad conditions, gives a target of 11. A roll of 9 succeeds by 2. A roll of 12 fails by 1.

              How this book is organized

              This book combines the Legend Dynamics core rules, which every setting shares, with the Dust & Damnation setting: its callings, origins, skills, gear, weapons and dangers. In this setting, the core rules’ Legend points are called Grit points.

              Create your character

              Work through the five steps in order. The character creator follows the same steps and checks the rules for you.

              Step 1: Choose your calling

              Choose a calling and describe what you bring. Your calling places you in a group, which only changes your sheet’s colors and seal. A title or rank is agreed with the GM and gives no bonus.

              Calling Group Typical contribution Core skills
              Marshal The Law carries what authority there is (take a Badge advantage), with Leadership and a steady gun. Leadership, Shooting, Law, Riding
              Deputy The Law keeps order, guards prisoners, and backs up the marshal when it comes to shooting. Shooting, Brawling, Riding, Read People
              Detective The Law investigates crimes for the Pinkertons (take Pinkerton Credentials) or a private client, and follows the paper trail nobody else reads. Investigation, Read People, Deception, Stealth
              Bounty Hunter The Law tracks wanted men across three territories and brings them in, upright or across a saddle. Tracking, Shooting, Riding, Survival
              Doctor The Learned digs out bullets, sets bones, and reads what a body says about how it died. Medicine, Investigation, Read People, Persuasion
              Occultist The Learned knows the old books, the rituals and the names of things that shouldn’t exist, and what each one fears. Occult, Investigation, Persuasion, Read People
              Lawyer The Learned knows the law better than the men who wear the badges, and isn’t afraid to say so. Law, Persuasion, Read People, Investigation
              Journalist The Learned asks the questions no one wants asked, and prints the answers. Investigation, Persuasion, Read People, Stealth
              Preacher The Learned comforts the frightened, counsels the troubled, and stands up to bad men with faith and a shotgun. Persuasion, Leadership, Occult, Shooting
              Monster Hunter The Frontier hunts what the law can’t hang, and knows which things fear silver, salt or fire (take the Monster Hunter advantage). Occult, Tracking, Shooting, Melee
              Gunslinger The Frontier the fastest and steadiest gun in the room, and the one who decides when it’s time to draw. Shooting, Athletics, Read People, Riding
              Scout The Frontier finds the trail, reads the land, and keeps everyone alive out in it. Tracking, Survival, Riding, Stealth
              Gambler The Frontier reads people, bluffs, and hears everything the saloon hears. Gambling, Read People, Deception, Shooting
              Wrangler The Frontier breaks horses, drives cattle, and can ride anything with four legs. Animal Handling, Riding, Survival, Brawling
              Undertaker The Frontier buries the dead, and makes sure they stay buried. Occult, Medicine, Investigation, Melee

              The creator marks your calling’s four core skills Trained when you choose it. Keep them, raise one to your Specialty, or swap any of them for other skills; changing calling swaps the core skills you haven’t changed.

              Well-rounded or Old hand

              Choose how your character trained (see Step 3):

              • Well-rounded: one Specialty at +3 and nine Trained skills.
              • Old hand: one Expert skill at +5 and seven Trained skills: a lifetime spent at one thing.

              Spending points

              After applying your origin, distribute five additional points among the attributes, plus one for each attribute Edge.

              • The starting maximum is 13, plus any origin bonus to that attribute.
              • You cannot lower an attribute below its base to gain points.

              Custom origin

              You can build your own origin with the GM. See Making your own for the steps.

              Step 3: Choose trained skills

              A Well-rounded character chooses ten different skills: one Specialty at its governing attribute +3 and nine Trained skills at attribute +1. An Old hand chooses one Expert skill at +5 and seven Trained skills at +1. Each Extra Trained skill Edge adds one more Trained skill. All other skills are Untrained at attribute −4 when an attempt is plausible. Each skill has its own final target number; do not add the attribute again when rolling.

              The Expert’s extra bonus matters most when the work is hard. With an attribute of 13, an Expert has a target of 18, which only natural 17–18 can fail; under a −4 “very difficult” penalty the target is still 14. If Athletics is the Expert skill, Dodge adds +2, the same as a Specialty.

              Some origins give one skill Trained for free; it doesn’t use one of your Trained picks.

              Broadening. An Old hand can broaden later (the GM’s call, usually after a significant job): the Expert skill becomes a Specialty at +3, and two more skills become Trained.

              Step 4: Describe your character

              Choose your name, title if any, and a short reason for what you do. Then choose one advantage and one complication.

              Top 10 reasons

              1. Justice. Somebody has to stand up for the people who can’t.
              2. Vengeance. Someone did me wrong, and I mean to settle it.
              3. Fortune. There’s money out here for anyone with nerve enough to take it.
              4. A fresh start. Nobody out here knows what I did back east.
              5. Duty. I took an oath, and I keep my word.
              6. Freedom. No boss, no fences, no one to answer to.
              7. Family. Everything I do is for my family.
              8. Faith. The Lord sent me here for a reason, and I believe this is it.
              9. Redemption. I did harm once. I mean to balance the ledger.
              10. Nowhere else to go. These people took me in when no one else would.
              • Advantage. Choose either a general advantage or one of your origin’s advantages. An origin advantage replaces the general advantage and always brings its companion complication.
              • Complication. Choose one complication. You keep it even if you take an origin advantage, so a character with an origin advantage has two complications.
              • Allegiance (optional). Choose an allegiance below, or none.

              An advantage gives +2 to one narrowly defined kind of task when the GM agrees it applies. It cannot improve every use of a broad skill. A complication creates choices and problems; it is not a permanent penalty.

              Allegiance

              Anyone can owe allegiance to one of the setting’s powers. It’s a benefit paired with a burden; the burden works like a complication and earns Grit when it causes you a real problem.

              Allegiance Benefit Burden
              The Railroad Once per job, a free ticket, a telegraph message on the company wire, or a word with a division boss. Duty: the company expects you to protect its interests, even when they aren’t right.
              A Cattle Baron Once per job, money, horses or hired hands. Duty: the baron’s range wars become your fights.
              The Army Once per job, help from a fort: supplies, an escort or a doctor. Duty: orders from a commanding officer, and old soldiers’ quarrels.
              The Church Once per job, shelter, records or a favor from the clergy. Duty: the Church gives you orders, and its enemies are yours.
              An Outlaw Gang Once per job, a hideout, a fence or a gun at your side. Duty: the gang calls in favors, and the law knows your face.
              The Order of the Iron Cross Once per job, silver, holy water, a hunter’s help or the Order’s bestiary. Duty: the Order sends you after monsters, and some of its hunters would count you among them.
              A coven Once per job, a ritual, a remedy or a safe house among witches. Duty and Vow: the coven’s secrets and its quarrels.

              Step 5: Prepare

              Your Dodge is Agility −2, plus your Athletics training bonus: +0 if Untrained, +1 if Trained, or +2 if a Specialty or Expert. Begin uninjured, with two Grit points (three with Extra Grit) and your gear. Name your friend or your fine weapon or tool, if you took those Edges. Describe one connection to another player’s character.

              Top 10 connections to another character

              1. Rode together in the war. We served in the same company and still watch each other’s backs.
              2. They saved my life. They cut me down from a lynch mob’s rope, and I owe them my life.
              3. I saved theirs. I pulled them out of a burning barn, and they haven’t forgotten it.
              4. Mentor. They taught me to shoot, ride and survive out here.
              5. Friendly rivals. We’re always competing over the same bounty, the same card game or the same sweetheart.
              6. Same hometown. We grew up in the same town and know each other’s families.
              7. An unpaid debt. I owe them money, or a favor, and I haven’t paid it yet.
              8. A shared secret. We share a secret from a job neither of us talks about.
              9. Saloon regulars. We meet every Saturday night for cards, whiskey and lies.
              10. Bad blood. They blame me for something that went wrong on a past job.

              Untrained actions

              A plausible untrained attempt uses attribute −4. Specialized work such as surgery requires relevant training or direct guidance. Guidance permits an attempt only when the GM finds it credible; it does not grant the expert’s skill.

              Actions and teamwork

              When to roll

              Routine work succeeds automatically when you have appropriate training, gear and time. Roll when failure could change the situation. Before rolling, establish your goal, the relevant skill, any modifiers, and what is at stake.

              Circumstances Modifier
              Favorable conditions or excellent preparation +2
              Ordinary challenge under pressure 0
              Difficult −2
              Very difficult −4
              Extreme −6

              Apply the main circumstance modifier once. Add separately specified effects such as assistance or injury when they apply. The GM avoids charging twice for the same obstacle.

              Results and margins

              Add the three dice. A total at or below the adjusted skill succeeds. Margin of success equals the adjusted skill minus the roll. A larger margin may mean better information, more precise work, or a stronger position.

              A natural 3 or 4 always succeeds. A natural 17 or 18 always fails. An impossible action receives no roll.

              In an opposed contest, each side rolls its relevant skill. One success beats one failure. If both succeed, the larger success margin wins. A tie, or two failures, favors the defender or preserves the situation. For an automatic success above its target, count its contest margin as zero.

              Assistance

              One qualified assistant can roll a relevant skill before the lead character rolls. Success grants +1 to the lead roll; success by 5 or more grants +2. Failure gives no bonus. An 18 introduces a complication described by the GM. Other characters can do other tasks but cannot stack assistance bonuses on the same roll.

              Failure and another attempt

              Failure changes the situation: time passes, the trail goes cold, supplies run out, or danger grows. Do not repeat an unchanged roll until it succeeds. A new approach, new evidence, better tools, or more time may justify another attempt.

              Success at a cost

              When a roll fails by only 1 or 2, the GM may offer success at a cost: you get what you were after, but something else goes wrong. Typical costs are an injury box, broken gear, lost time (tick a clock), a box on a track, or someone noticing. The GM names the cost before you accept; you can always take the plain failure instead. A natural 17 or 18 is never a success at a cost.

              Progress clocks

              A clock is a circle cut into 2, 4, 6 or 8 segments that tracks something that takes time or builds toward disaster.

              • Danger clocks fill when things go wrong: a failed roll, a success at a cost, or time passing. When one fills, the danger happens.
              • Progress clocks fill as the characters succeed: one segment for a success, two for success by 5 or more. When one fills, the goal is achieved.
              • The GM sizes the clock (2 = quick, 4 = urgent or simple, 6 = substantial, 8 = long or complex) and shows it to the players.

              Investigation and social scenes

              Ask concrete questions and explain your approach. Evidence may require several different tasks. A successful roll does not force someone to abandon a core belief or reveal what they do not know.

              Chases

              Run a chase as a progress clock for the pursuers against a danger clock for the quarry’s escape. Each exchange, the characters roll the skill that moves them (Riding, or Athletics on foot): success ticks the pursuers’ clock, failure ticks the escape. Terrain, a tired mount or a damaged engine, and attacks made on the move (at −2) adjust the rolls.

              Personal combat

              Turn order and actions

              Act in descending Agility order. Players choose the order of tied characters; the GM resolves other ties. A round lasts a few seconds. On your turn, move a short distance (up to 10 yards: the Close band below) and take one meaningful action, such as attacking, reloading, helping, or attempting a task.

              Attacking and defending

              Roll Shooting for a ranged attack, Melee for a weapon in hand, or Brawling for fists and grappling. If the attack succeeds, an aware target able to move may attempt Dodge. Dodge is Agility −2, with +1 for Trained Athletics or +2 for Athletics as a Specialty or Expert. Each target gets one Dodge attempt per round. A successful Dodge cancels the hit. Apply injury penalties to Dodge.

              Solid cover blocks a shot. Partial cover imposes −2 on the attack. Weapon effects apply after a hit survives any Dodge. Success margin does not increase damage.

              Range

              Distances fall into five bands:

              Band Distance For example
              Close Up to 10 yards Across a room; one move covers it
              Short Up to 50 yards Across a street or a courtyard
              Medium Up to 150 yards A city block, a long field
              Long Up to 500 yards Across a valley, a park or a harbor
              Extreme Up to a mile, in line of sight Rooftop to rooftop across a town

              Every ranged weapon has an effective band, shown in the weapons table. Attacks within it take no penalty. One band beyond it is −2, two bands beyond is −4, and anything farther is out of range. Weapons in hand and fists reach only someone next to you; move into reach as part of your turn. A thrown weapon’s effective band is Close.

              Moving: your move each turn covers the Close band. Give up your action to run and you cover Short instead.

              Injury

              Boxes marked Condition Effect
              0 Uninjured No injury penalty
              1 Wounded −1 to physical actions
              2 Seriously wounded −2 to physical actions
              3 Incapacitated Unable to act; medical aid needed

              Injury penalties replace each other rather than stacking. They apply to movement, attacks, Dodge, and other tasks the injury physically hinders.

              Dying and death

              • Dying. A hit from a weapon that marks 2 or more boxes and brings you to three boxes leaves you Incapacitated and Dying. A Dying character dies at the end of the scene unless someone stabilizes them: one action and a successful Medicine roll with a doctor’s bag.
              • A further hit. While you are at three boxes, any hit from a weapon that marks 2 or more boxes kills you. Fists, clubs and knives just keep you down.
              • Certain death. Some fates (a fall into a furnace, a beheading, disintegration) bypass the track entirely.

              Your protection is avoiding the hit: Dodge, cover, and a Grit point, which reduces a hit by one box.

              Weapons and damage

              How many boxes a hit marks depends on the weapon, not on how well the attack succeeded.

A revolver shot that gets through marks two more: Ames is at three boxes, Incapacitated and Dying, and a further 2-box hit would kill. Spending a Grit point on that revolver shot reduces it to one box, leaving Ames Seriously wounded and still standing.

              To take someone down without killing them, use fists, a club, a grapple, or a lasso.

              Medical care

              Frontier medicine has chloroform, carbolic acid, laudanum and steady hands, and not much else. Infection kills more men than bullets do, and no doctor’s bag holds a cure for a bite from something that should be dead. See Bites and curses below.

              Diagnosis and stabilization

              Roll Medicine when the diagnosis is uncertain or an urgent treatment decision matters. Emergency stabilization takes one action and a successful Medicine roll with a doctor’s bag. It ends Dying and stops deterioration, but removes no boxes.

              Field treatment

              With a doctor’s bag and several uninterrupted minutes, successful Medicine removes one injury box; success by 5 or more removes two. Each injury receives one field-treatment attempt; further attempts need changed circumstances.

              Proper care

              A doctor’s surgery with instruments, chloroform and a clean table grants +2 to Medicine. A successful treatment removes two boxes; success by 5 or more removes three. Allow the time the treatment requires.

              Natural recovery

              Each two full days of rest clears one injury box. Rest means nothing strenuous. At three boxes, rest alone clears nothing: treatment must clear the first box.

              Grit points and tracks

              Using Grit points

              Begin each session with two Grit points (three with Extra Grit); unused points do not carry over. Spend no more than one point on a single roll or injury event.

              Spend one point to Timing and limits
              Reroll your own roll Declare after seeing the result but before its consequences. Keep the second result, even if worse.
              Reduce an injury by one box Declare when a hit would mark boxes, before applying them. Does not prevent certain death.
              Establish a small preparation Propose a plausible detail, such as having packed something useful. GM approval is required.

              Gain one point when one of your complications creates a meaningful problem you accept, including a companion complication. You can decline the proposed complication without penalty.

              Trouble

              The characters share three Trouble tracks. Heat is the law and the powerful taking notice; Whispers is the town talking about what it saw; The Dark is the things in the night learning your names. They fill as your deeds catch up with you; the GM narrates what each box means. A killing in the street, a monster seen in the open, a ritual worked carelessly, or a success at a cost can mark a box. Lying low between jobs, a good alibi (Deception) or doing the town a public good can clear one per job.

              Track Boxes As boxes fill
              Heat 4 1: Questions at the sheriff’s office; 2: Wanted posters: −2 to Persuasion with lawmen; 3: A posse is out; 4: A warrant and a manhunt: start a Manhunt clock
              Whispers 4 1: Talk of strange things in the saloon; 2: The preacher names you from the pulpit: −2 to Persuasion with townsfolk; 3: Folk bar their doors; 4: A mob with torches: start a Mob clock
              The Dark 4 1: Dogs howl at your door; 2: Signs and warnings: a marked door, a dead bird; 3: Something follows you at night; 4: It comes: start a clock

              Advancement

              After a significant job, the GM may authorize one relevant skill to advance from Untrained to Trained or from Trained to Specialty, or allow revision of an advantage or complication. Only an Old hand’s starting skill is Expert; a skill raised later tops out at Specialty (+3) unless they broaden (see Step 3).

              Running jobs

              Job seeds

              The trail journal

              End each job with a short entry, written together:

              1. The job. What happened, and did we succeed: fully, partly, or not at all?
              2. Who stood out? A moment worth remembering.
              3. Complications. Which ones came into play, and what did they cost?
              4. What changed? New allies or enemies, damage, open threads for next time.
              5. Advancement. After a significant job, the GM may authorize one skill step for each character.

              Monsters and magic

              The Sight

              A few people are born Sighted. They see ghosts, the true faces of monsters wearing human ones, and magic at work. To everyone else the darkness is a rumor, until it isn’t.

              Monstrous forms

              Werewolves and vampires have a monstrous form: the wolf, or the feral face. Changing is a free action, but fury, terror or pain can force it (a Will roll), and a werewolf has no choice at moonrise under the full moon. While changed, the form’s profile applies: the creator’s In monstrous form box adds its bonuses to your rolls. Horses bolt (−2 to Riding), and a monstrous form seen by townsfolk marks a box of Whispers.

              Magic

              Practitioners work rituals in a tradition. Describe the effect; the GM sets its weight and says what it can and can’t do. Fill a clock of that size with Occult rolls at your tradition’s pace (usually ten minutes a roll; an hour for a Hermeticist; one action for a Sorcerer). Success fills one segment, success by 5 or more two; a failure costs time or ticks a danger clock.

              Weight Segments For example
              Minor 2 A charm against a hex, a candle that shows the hidden, a sleep that lasts the night
              Significant 4 A curse that withers a crop, a ward on a house, a question asked of the dead
              Major 6 Binding a demon, raising the dead, lifting a werewolf’s curse for a month
              • Trappings. The right materials (grave dirt, silver, a lock of hair, the hour of midnight) remove one segment.
              • Charms. A finished ritual can be stored in an object and released by breaking it. Carry as many ready charms as your Occult training allows: Trained 2, Specialty 3, Expert 4.
              • Attention. Magic worked openly can mark a box of The Dark, and the Order of the Iron Cross hunts known practitioners.

              Weaknesses

              Every monster has a weakness, and finding it is often the whole job. Occult identifies a creature and its weakness; the Monster Hunter advantage and the Sighted’s Monster’s Eye help.

              Creature Weakness
              Werewolf Silver: an extra box, and its toughness doesn’t help. Wolfsbane keeps it away.
              Vampire A wooden stake through the heart incapacitates; fire, sunlight and beheading destroy. Holy symbols hold it back; it can’t enter a home uninvited.
              Zombie A shot to the head (−2 to hit) or fire puts it down for good; anything else just slows it.
              Ghost Can’t be hurt. Lay it to rest by finishing its business, or bind it with a ritual.
              Ghoul Fire and daylight. It flees from holy ground.
              Demon Holy water burns it; a ritual banishes it. It must keep a bargain to the letter.

              Bites and curses

              • Zombie bite. A bite that marks a box starts a 4-segment Infection clock, ticking each hour. A successful Medicine roll with an Occult remedy stops it; when it fills, you die, and rise.
              • Werewolf bite. A bite under the full moon can pass on the curse; the GM decides, and lifting it is a major ritual.
              • Vampire bite. A vampire who drains a victim to Dying can choose to turn them.

Your superiors can give you orders, and abusing the badge or failing in your duty can cost you it. Law callings (Marshal, Deputy) usually take one, as their advantage or as a second advantage Edge. A Pinkerton detective takes Pinkerton Credentials instead: the agency’s reach and files, but no badge.

              • Marked: holy ground, holy water and consecrated silver burn you (1 box).

              Suggested skills: Occult, Persuasion, Deception, Read People.

              Advantage Effect Companion complication
              Infernal Bargain +2 to Persuasion when making a deal or a promise. The Debt Comes Due. Your patron wants something. Accept the errand, or the price of refusing.
              Whispers from Below Ability: once per session, ask your patron one question and get a true, unhelpful answer. Possessive Patron. Your patron won’t share you. Accept it when it interferes with your friends, your faith or your heart.

              Vampire

              Tier 3 · 0 Edges · Body −1, Agility +1, Presence +2, Will −2 · horror

              Undead, ageless and beautiful, with a thirst that never ends. You walk at night and remember centuries. Horror campaigns only.

              Origin rules:

              • Undead: you don’t breathe or age; Medicine can’t heal you. Drinking blood can: Will against the victim’s Body, and on a success you clear a box and they mark one.
              • Thirst (horror): each night you must drink human blood, or mark a box that only drinking clears.
              • Sunlight: 1 box each round in direct sun.
              • Weaknesses: a wooden stake through the heart incapacitates you; fire and beheading kill; holy symbols give −2 to your actions against their bearer; you can’t cross a home’s threshold uninvited.

              Monstrous form: Body +2; Agility +1; Fangs and claws mark 2 boxes; Tough: hits of 2+ boxes mark one fewer, except fire and wooden stakes; Dark sight. Drawbacks: Your feral face terrifies everyone who sees it.

              Suggested skills: Persuasion, Deception, Stealth, Read People.

              Advantage Effect Companion complication
              Mesmerize Ability: meet someone’s eyes and roll Presence against their Will; on a success they obey one simple command. Old Enemies. Centuries make enemies. Accept it when one turns up.
              Mist and Shadow Ability: once per scene at night, become mist and escape through any gap. Pride of the Old Blood. You won’t be slighted by the living. Accept the feud you start.

              Making your own

              The callings and origins in this book are a starting point, not a limit. With the GM’s agreement, any player can create their own calling or origin. The creator has a Custom option at the end of each list, and its checklist holds a custom choice to the same rules as everything else.

              A custom calling

              A calling is a concept, not a package of powers: it gives no bonus of its own. A custom one only needs a clear place in the group.

              1. Name it and describe it. One sentence for what your character does that no one else does, in the style of the callings above: “Gravedigger: digs the holes, reads the stones, and knows which of the dead are restless.”
              2. Choose a group. Pick the one it belongs with: The Law, The Learned or The Frontier. The group only sets your sheet’s colors and emblem.
              3. Pick four core skills. The skills it can’t do without. Mark them Trained yourself in step 3 (the built-in callings do this for you); they count toward your normal Trained skills.
              4. Check it with the GM. It should fit the setting and overlap the existing callings as little as possible.

              In the creator: in step 1, choose Custom calling… at the end of the Calling list, then enter its name and group. Write the description in What you bring.

              A custom origin

              Build it with the GM, one step at a time.

              1. The concept

              Two or three sentences: where they come from, what sets them apart, and how others see them. It should be something the origins in this book don’t already cover.

              2. The tier

              The tier measures how much the origin gives on its own. Each tier costs one of your 3 Edge points, so stronger origins start with fewer Edges.

              Tier Edges Use it for
              0 3 Ordinary people: a homeland, upbringing or culture, with no special abilities
              1 2 One small, constant edge: keen senses, a tough hide, a free Trained skill, resistance to one hazard
              2 1 Clear inhuman or supernatural gifts: venom, a second form, strong resistances
              3 0 Remarkable beings: several strong abilities, and serious weaknesses to match

              When in doubt, choose the higher tier.

              3. Attribute adjustments

              Give adjustments from −2 to +2 per attribute that total zero: a +2 needs a −2 or two −1s. The adjustments move the attribute’s base (+1 starts it at 11) and raise its starting maximum (13 plus the bonus). Most origins change two to four attributes.

              4. Core rules (tier 1 and up)

              One to three short rules that always apply: what they can do (see in the dark, resist cold, a free Trained skill) and what it costs (a weakness, a people’s distrust, a hazard they can’t abide). A strength with no drawback is worth more than it looks, so price it a tier higher.

              5. Origin advantages

              Write one to three. Each works like a general advantage: +2 to one skill in a narrow situation, or a small, limited permission (“once per scene…”). Each comes with a companion complication: a problem the trait causes, with a clear trigger and a real consequence, which earns a Grit point when it bites, like any complication. A character takes one advantage, general or origin, so make them distinct choices.

              6. An alternate form (optional, tier 2 or 3)

              If the origin changes shape, give the form attribute bonuses (usually +2 to one attribute and +1 to another), two or three traits, and its drawbacks. The shared rules for forms in Origins apply.

              In the creator

              1. In step 2, choose Custom origin… at the end of the Origin list.
              2. Name it, choose the tier you agreed, and set its adjustments. The checklist confirms they total zero.
              3. Write its core rules in Other ability (GM approval required) in step 4.
              4. In step 4, choose Custom origin advantage… and enter the advantage and its companion complication.

              Example

              Ghost-touched (tier 1). You died once, for a minute, and came back with something following you. Adjustments: Will +1, Body −1. Core rule: the dead notice you: you can sense a ghost nearby, but ghosts can sense you too. Origin advantage: Speaks with the Dead: +2 to Occult to call up and question a ghost. Companion: Cold Company: something from the other side follows you; when it matters, accept the chill, the stopped clock or the panicked horse.

              Complications

              Choose one complication. These are sources of meaningful choices, not constant penalties. The GM proposes a concrete problem and offers one Grit point if you accept it. Every complication needs a trigger agreed with the GM; the creator suggests three for each.

              Complication What it does
              Cannot Leave a Mystery Alone A strange discovery tempts you to linger, spend something scarce, or take a risky look. Accept a specific cost.
              Protects Others at Personal Risk You shield someone or volunteer for a hazardous rescue at a real cost to yourself.
              Code of the West You won’t shoot an unarmed man, break your word, or strike from behind. Accept the setback your code causes.
              Law and Order You insist on arrest, trial and due process. Accept the delay or danger it costs.
              Reputation to Uphold Pride tempts you to refuse help or take on too much. Take a real consequence when it matters.
              Haunted by the War A specific reminder of the war causes hesitation or a hard choice. Define the reminder together.
              Keeps a Dangerous Secret An agreed secret threatens trust or safety. Earn a point when protecting or revealing it has a real consequence.
              Old Rival A recurring rival complicates your work with concrete problems.
              Someone Back Home A family or a sweetheart pulls you away from the work.
              Sworn Vengeance You’ve sworn to kill someone. Accept the risk when the chance comes.
              Short Fuse You answer insults with fists or guns. Accept the fight you start.
              The Bottle Whiskey or laudanum has a hold on you. Accept the lost night or the slip at a bad time.
              Can’t Walk Away from a Game A card game, a bet or a dare is irresistible. Accept the time or money lost.
              Wanted There’s a price on your head somewhere. Accept trouble when lawmen or bounty hunters come.
              Owes the Wrong People A debt to dangerous people comes due. Accept their demand.
              Reluctant to Kill You seek a way that doesn’t end a life. Accept the extra danger.
              Cursed Something cursed you, and the curse has a will of its own. Agree what it does; accept it when it strikes.
              Haunted by a Ghost A spirit follows you, and wants something. Accept the haunting and the trouble it brings.
              Marked by the Dark Things that live in the dark know your scent. Accept it when they find you first.

              Quick reference

              Creation: attributes start at 10, adjusted by origin (always totaling zero), plus 5 points; maximum 13 plus any origin bonus. 3 points split between origin tier and Edges. Well-rounded: one Specialty and nine Trained. Old hand: one Expert at +5 and seven Trained. One advantage (general or origin) and one complication; an optional Allegiance.

              Skills: Untrained = attribute −4; Trained = attribute +1; Specialty = attribute +3; Expert (Old hand) = attribute +5.

              Modifiers: favorable +2; ordinary 0; difficult −2; very difficult −4; extreme −6. Natural 3–4 succeeds; natural 17–18 fails.

              Assistance: success +1; success by 5 or more +2; 18 causes a complication. One assistant per roll.

              Near misses: fail by 1–2 and the GM may offer success at a cost; never on a natural 17–18.

              Clocks: 2, 4, 6 or 8 segments. Danger clocks fill on failures and time; progress clocks fill on successes (2 segments on success by 5).

              Combat: move plus one action. Hit → optional Dodge → weapon effect. Dodge = Agility −2 plus Athletics training (+0, +1, +2). A 2-box weapon that brings you to three boxes leaves you Dying; at three boxes, another 2-box hit kills.

              Medicine: stabilize in one action. Field care clears 1 box (2 on margin 5+). Proper care gives +2 and clears 2 (3 on margin 5+). Rest: one box per two full days, not from three.

              Grit: start each session with two. Reroll, reduce an injury by one box, or establish a small preparation. Earn one from an accepted complication.
