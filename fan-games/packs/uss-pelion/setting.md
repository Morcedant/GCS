<!-- SECTION:intro -->

You are a member of Starfleet aboard USS Pelion, NCC-1750. Explore unfamiliar worlds, investigate strange phenomena, protect your crewmates, and make difficult decisions when orders and circumstances collide. Your training matters, and the crew succeeds by working together.

This is an unofficial fan game inspired by *Star Trek*. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Paramount or CBS Studios, who own Star Trek.

This guide presents the Pelion rules in a form you can use at the table. The material for running the game (mission seeds, the threats and enemy ships, and the GM's tools) is in a companion book, the **GM's Guide**. It combines concrete skills and 3d6 roll-under resolution with a small pool of Duty points.

Officers hold ranks from Ensign through Captain. **General Services** is the division for enlisted crew: Crewman 3rd, 2nd, and 1st Class, Crewman, and Yeoman 3rd, 2nd, and 1st Class, and Yeoman. A character with a Crewman or Yeoman rank belongs to General Services. Enlisted crew use the same creation rules as officers, except for their skills (see Step 3): they are specialists rather than generalists. Enlisted crew are the guide's *enlisted specialists*: one Expert skill at +5 and seven Trained skills, where an officer has a Specialty and nine Trained.

**Earning a commission.** If a General Services character is commissioned (the GM's call, usually after distinguished service), they move to the officer package: the Expert skill becomes a Specialty at +3, and they can raise two more skills to Trained to reach nine. Rank and department change to match.

Personal actions and away-team encounters work by the Legend Dynamics core rules. Starship stations use the same skills, and ship fights use the light rules and the ship registry in *The USS Pelion*.

**Phasers and disruptors don't jam**; a primitive firearm jams on a natural 18. Starfleet issues no armor; Klingon and Gorn soldiers sometimes wear it.

*Example:* a Klingon's punch leaves Ensign Reyes Wounded (−1). On the next round a d'k tahg slash marks a second box: Seriously wounded (−2). A disruptor shot instead, on an unhurt Reyes, would mark all three boxes: Incapacitated and Dying, with the end of the scene as the deadline for a medic. Spending a Duty point when the disruptor hits reduces it to two boxes, leaving Reyes Seriously wounded but still standing. If Reyes is already down at three boxes, another disruptor shot or a lirpa blow kills; a punch does not.

To take someone down without hurting them, use a phaser on stun, a grapple, or the Vulcan nerve pinch rather than a weapon from this list.

### Phaser settings

| Setting | Effect on a hit |
|---|---|
| Stun | Target rolls Body at −2. Failure incapacitates them for the scene; success gives −2 on their next action roll. Stun marks no injury boxes. |
| Heat or cut | Controlled tool use. Material, time, and the task determine the skill and difficulty. |
| Kill | Marks three injury boxes: the target is Incapacitated and Dying. Disintegration requires a deliberate setting and an exposed target; the GM determines whether it is possible. |

Declare the setting before firing. Stun effects do not stack; a later failed resistance roll still incapacitates the target. Unarmed strikes normally mark one box. A grapple is an Unarmed Combat attack: a hit that pins (a telling hit, or a called shot for one) holds the target as the *Pin* effect says, and their next action goes to getting free.

### Phaser power

Hand phasers run on power cells, and every shot draws on them. This is a campaign rule; the GM tracks opponents' weapons loosely.

| Phaser | Power |
|---|---|
| Type 1 hand phaser | 8 |
| Type 2 phaser pistol | 16 |
| Phaser rifle | 30 |

| Setting | Power per use |
|---|---|
| Stun | 1 |
| Heat or cut | 1 per use as a tool |
| Kill | 2 |
| Wide-beam stun (everyone in a small area; each target makes the stun roll) | 3 |
| Disintegrate (when the GM allows it) | 4 |

- **Firing spends power whether the shot hits or misses.** A Duty point reroll repeats the same shot and costs nothing extra.
- **A drained phaser won't fire.** Swap in a spare power cell (one action) to refill it, or recharge it fully in the ship's armory between missions. Having packed a spare cell is a good use of a Duty point's *small preparation*.
- **Overload.** A phaser with at least 4 power left can be set to overload: it whines for a round and explodes at the end of the next round, as an explosion (1 box to everyone nearby, 2 at the centre). The phaser is destroyed.

<!-- SECTION:medicine -->

A starship's **sickbay** counts as proper care.

### Sickbay treatment

A functioning, equipped sickbay grants +2 to Medicine for diagnosis and treatment. A successful treatment removes two injury boxes; success by 5 or more removes three. Allow the time the procedure requires rather than completing surgery in one combat action. Sickbay care can follow field treatment.

Removing injury boxes removes their associated penalties, but major organ damage, severe burns, and complex internal trauma can still require a procedure and recovery period set by the GM. The physician determines whether the patient is fit for duty. Pain suppression does not itself heal an injury. Stun normally wears off at the end of the scene; prolonged unconsciousness requires assessment.

### Alien medicine and limits

Unknown illness or physiology requires discovering the cause and a suitable treatment before boxes can be restored. Medicine can receive assistance from Life Sciences, Sensors, or Computers. Existing assistance limits apply. Missing essential equipment or incompatible supplies can prevent a procedure. Disintegration and other unsurvivable injuries cannot be reversed by an ordinary Medicine roll.

Crew of other species may need care suited to their biology. An Alien Physiology Specialist, or a physician with access to the right species data, keeps that care from becoming guesswork.

### Examples from the original series

*Journey to Babel:* McCoy performs complex surgery on Sarek during a ship crisis; compatible blood and postoperative care remain important. *The Devil in the Dark:* McCoy improvises a treatment suited to the Horta's unusual biology. *Miri:* diagnosing an unfamiliar disease and establishing a safe treatment require investigation. These stories support powerful medicine whose limits come from the medical problem and available resources.

Canon reference: StarTrek.com, "8 Times Leonard 'Bones' McCoy's Medical Knowledge Saved the Day." <https://www.startrek.com/news/mccoys-medical-knowledge-saved-the-day>

The crew shares the **USS Pelion**'s tracks: **Shields** and **Hull** (5 boxes each) and six systems of 3 boxes (Weapons, Engines, Sensors, Communications, Transporters, Life support), explained under *The USS Pelion*. Beside them is **Starfleet Command**: how much attention the crew is drawing from Command, with questions about the Prime Directive, risky contacts and orders bent.

- **Marking:** a hit in a ship fight or strain on a system (the ship's tracks); contact the Prime Directive frowns on, a risky deal with a power, orders bent (Starfleet Command). A success at a cost can mark any of them.
- **Clearing:** a starbase repairs the ship, and an engineer can patch a system between fights (see *The USS Pelion*). Command's boxes clear only when the GM says the matter is settled.

# Library records

What your character knows about the unknown, and how they came to know it: an alien species, a lifeform, a piece of technology, a phenomenon, or what a power intends.

## Rumor, truth and records

- **What people say.** Rumor on a starbase, an old survey report, a story from another ship's crew. Some of it is right, some half right, some wrong. It's where an investigation starts.
- **The truth.** What the thing really is and what really stops it or makes it work. The GM knows it; the crew finds it out.
- **Library records.** What your character has established for themselves, one finding at a time, filed in their **library file**: what they found, how far it goes, how they know it, what it cost, and the stardate.

A library record grants **no bonus** to any roll. It records what you established and what it lets you attempt. Whatever is actually true works whether or not anyone holds a record for it: a frequency that disrupts a lifeform disrupts it for whoever finds it. Operating unfamiliar technology, or dealing with a people by their own customs, still needs the right procedure, the knowledge and the skill.

| Subject | What a record might establish | What it might permit |

## Rival clocks

When nothing threatens yet, give the discovery a rival instead: an ordinary danger clock named for someone racing the crew to the same find (a Klingon science vessel, an Orion salvager, a Federation survey team with other orders). It ticks on delays, noisy methods and open digging; when it fills, the rival gets there first.

# On the bridge

## Working on the bridge

Describe the effect you want before selecting a skill. Each exchange gives occupied stations a chance to contribute. Roll for consequential decisions and uncertain work; routine station operation needs no roll.

| Station | Skill and contribution |
|---|---|
| Captain | Orders each exchange: Command, Read the enemy, Hail or Rally (see The captain's orders) |
| Helm | Helm to evade, pursue, or gain position |
| Navigation | Navigation to plot a safe course or escape |
| Science | Sensors to identify threats, weaknesses, or anomalies |
| Engineering | Engineering to repair systems or redirect power |
| Communications | Communications to establish contact or overcome interference |
| Tactical | Phasers to fire ship weapons |

### The captain's orders

The captain, or whoever has the conn, takes one of these each exchange:

| Order | Roll | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Command | Leadership | Success: one station gets +2 to its roll this exchange; success by 5, two stations. |
| Read the enemy | Psychology | Success: you predict their next move. +2 to the pilot's evade or range contest this exchange, or the gunner ignores one band of range penalty. |
| Hail | Diplomacy (a bluff included) | Opposed by the enemy captain's Will. Success: they pause to talk, hold fire for an exchange, or consider surrender; a bluff can make them believe what you say about your ship. |
| Rally | Leadership | After a hit to the hull, steady the crew: anyone stunned or shaken by the hit acts normally again, and the next failed station roll this scene may be rerolled. |

A captain who takes an order doesn't also work a station that exchange. Commanding well is the job.

## The captain's log

End each mission with a short log, written together:

1. **The mission.** What were we sent to do, and did we do it: completed, partly, or failed?
2. **Who stood out?** Name a commendation, or a moment worth remembering.
3. **Complications.** Which ones came into play, and what did they cost?
4. **What changed?** Damage to the ship, new allies or enemies, open threads for next time.
5. **Advancement.** After a significant mission, the GM awards advancement points (see *Advancement*); record them in the log.

The creator's GM view keeps the log, applies each crew member's advancement to the GM's copy, and saves both with the crew file. Players record an approved advancement on their own sheet under Step 3, so their character still passes the checklist.

# Mind and will

Roll against Will to resist overwhelming fear, alien compulsion, or mental intrusion, or to maintain concentration through severe distraction. Apply the normal difficulty modifiers. Ordinary danger does not require a Will roll. If an adversary actively imposes mental influence, use an opposed contest of your Will against the adversary's relevant ability.

Before the roll, the GM states the possible effect and its duration. A failed fear roll leaves you Frightened (see *Fear and other conditions*); a failed concentration roll gives −2 on your next action. Neither forces a belief or personal decision. Mental intrusion can impose a specific effect established before the roll; resisting that effect does not reveal the intruder's identity or intentions.

Will protects your resolve; Presence influences others. Leadership and Diplomacy still use Presence. Species bonuses may raise your starting Will, but mental advantages apply only to their agreed, narrow circumstances.

## Mental harm (an option)

A GM whose missions turn on telepaths and mind-bending aliens (the Talosians, a Melkot) can give every crew member a **Mind** track beside the injury track. Attacks on the mind mark it: the attacker's ability against Will, never Dodge, and armor doesn't help. One box is Shaken (−1 to mental actions: Will, and skills of Intellect and Presence), two Overwhelmed (−2), three Broken: unconscious or catatonic until they rest. It never leaves anyone Dying; a scene's rest clears one box, a night's sleep all of them.

# The USS Pelion

The Pelion is a **Heavy Cruiser**, and it has its own sheet, tracked like an injury track. The GM states what is at stake before the rolls and narrates what each box means. Every Starfleet class, and the ships the Pelion meets, is in the **Ship registry** below, adapted from Tiny Trek.

| Track | Boxes | Effect as boxes fill |
|---|---|---|
| Shields | 5 | While the shields are up, hits mark Shields first. With all 5 marked they're down, and hits go to the Hull. |
| Hull | 5 | 1–2: scarred, minor breaches sealed. 3–4: serious breaches, decks lost. 5: the ship is breaking up; start an *Abandon ship* clock. |
| Weapons, Engines, Sensors, Communications, Transporters, Life support | 3 each | 1 **Damaged**: −2 to rolls using it. 2 **Crippled**: −4, and it can do only its most basic job. 3 **Offline**: it doesn't work until repaired. |

**Power.** Each exchange the ship has **Power** to spend (the Pelion has 5), and it refills at the start of the next. Raising the shields for the exchange costs 1, firing the phasers 1, a torpedo volley 1 (and one torpedo from the load), and a transporter use 1. Each Power left over can be diverted for +1 to one ship roll that exchange, up to +2. With the shields not raised, hits go straight to the Hull.

**Taking hits.** A hit marks the boxes its weapon deals (see the registry): Shields first while they're up, then the Hull. **Every hit that reaches the Hull** also rolls on the *Concussive system damage* table below. A called shot or a telling hit can pick a system instead.

**Defending.** When an enemy ship fires, Helm may roll **Helm** to evade, once per exchange, like Dodge: success cancels the hit. Torpedoes can't be evaded. Engines damage applies its penalty.

**Maneuverability.** Compare the ships' Maneuverability: the most maneuverable ship adds the difference to its Helm contests and its evade rolls. If two ships tie for the highest, nobody gets the bonus.

**Repairs.** In the middle of a crisis, a successful **Engineering** roll clears one box from Shields or a system (two on success by 5), or undoes one concussive result; it takes the engineer's action that exchange. The Hull can only be patched by one box per scene, and fully repaired at a starbase. Shields that went down take an hour per box to restore.

**Firing.** Tactical rolls **Phasers** for ship weapons, against the enemy ship's own tracks, which the GM keeps the same way.

## The first mission: The Silence at Corvan II

A starter mission for three to six, for one or two sessions. A Federation outpost that stopped answering, a people who have never seen the stars, Orion pirates in orbit, and an ancient machine that has decided who the threat is. It shows off the ship and its Power, an away team, a library record and a Countdown, and the Prime Directive with Starfleet Command watching. It's written for the six ready-made crew, and works with any.

**The hook.** The captain is at a starbase conference, and Commander sh'Vari has the conn when the orders come in: the Federation research outpost on **Corvan II**, eight archaeologists studying the ruins of an extinct civilization, has missed three check-ins. Corvan II has a native people, the **Ketari**, at a bronze-age level: the outpost works under the Prime Directive, behind a duck blind. An Orion raider was reported in the sector last week. Give each of the crew a reason of their own:

- **Thelan sh'Vari**: the first command decision with the captain away, and Starfleet Command will read every word of the log.
- **Sevek**: the outpost's last report described power readings from the ruins that contradict known physics.
- **Dr. Vessik**: eight scientists, and the Ketari village two kilometers from the ruins. Civilians, everywhere.
- **Grath**: the outpost's lead scientist, Dr. Imre Haskel, wrote to Grath for help rigging a "field dampener". Grath never heard what it was for.
- **Marisol Reyes**: leads the away team's security, and Orions mean phasers.
- **Rrinn**: Rrinn's sister serves on the outpost's supply run, and her last message ended mid-sentence.

**What's really happening.** The ruins hold a **caretaker machine**, left by the vanished civilization to watch over the Ketari. Dr. Haskel's dampener woke it. It judged the outpost a threat to its charges and put all eight scientists into stasis, unharmed, deep in the ruins. Then Orion salvagers came for the machine, and they've been trading phasers to a Ketari chieftain for help digging. The machine sees the Orions as a threat too, and now the Pelion: when the ship scans the ruins, the machine starts pulling power from orbit.

**1. Arrival.** The Orion raider is in orbit, on the far side of the moon. It hails first: they're "legitimate salvagers", and this is unclaimed space. Run a few exchanges of ship combat if the crew wants it (*The USS Pelion*, in the Players' Guide), or let them bluff, scan or warn it off. The raider runs once its Shields are down.

**2. The pull.** The moment the Pelion's sensors sweep the ruins, the lights flicker: something below is drawing on the ship's power. The Pelion has 1 less Power each exchange, and its orbit begins to decay. Start a **Countdown** of 6: when it fills, the Pelion has to break orbit, leaving whoever is on the surface behind. Grath can buy time: an Engineering roll clears a segment once.

**3. The outpost.** The away team beams into an empty outpost. Start a **library record** on the thing in the ruins (*Library records*, in the Players' Guide). The clues, and who finds them best:

- **The outpost logs** (Computers): Haskel's dampener test, then a power spike, then nothing.
- **The empty bunks** (Medicine, Sensors): no blood, no struggle; personal effects untouched. The scientists walked out.
- **The ruins' energy** (Physical Sciences, Sensors): a field that reads the away team's biology and records it. It knows them now.
- **The village** (Psychology, Stealth from the duck blind): Ketari carrying Orion phasers. The chieftain's people are digging at the ruins at night.

When the Identify phase is done, the crew knows what it is: a caretaker, not a weapon. Filling the clock earns the record of what it is and how it can be reasoned with.

**4. The Ketari.** The chieftain's warriors have phasers and no idea what they are. Contact breaks the Prime Directive; doing nothing leaves a bronze-age war with energy weapons. Every contact the crew makes, open or disguised, marks the **Starfleet Command** track unless they're very careful. Reyes's complication will come up; so will Vessik's.

**5. The Orions.** In the ruins, three **Orion pirates** are cutting their way to the machine's core. This is the big fight. Phasers on stun keep the record clean; a kill marks Starfleet Command.

**6. The caretaker.** The machine speaks through a **Rogue android** shell: courteous, absolute. It will protect the Ketari from everything off-world, Federation included. A Diplomacy or Psychology contest that presents a paradox (protecting them from all outsiders while it is an outsider itself) stuns it for the scene: Sevek's moment. Or the crew can talk to it, as equals.

**7. The choice.** With the Countdown running out: shut the machine down (the scientists wake, and the Ketari lose their protector), convince it to release the scientists and let it go on watching, or leave it alone and get the Orion phasers out of the village before they change Ketari history. Rrinn's sister is among the sleepers.

**Rewards.** Eight scientists home safe. A library record on the caretaker. If the Ketari never learned what the crew were, Starfleet Command clears a box; if they did, it marks one, and an admiral wants a full account. Rrinn finally hears the end of that message.

**Threats.** Orion raider (*Enemy ships*); Orion pirate ×3; Rogue android (the caretaker's shell); Ketari warriors as Security guards (minor), armed with stolen disruptors.
